# Device catalog

One line per labelable device: `id` (kind) Name - summary. Cues: surface markers. Not: nearest confusions.
A variant label also counts as its parent; use the most specific id that fits.


## Induction (`induction`)

Ways of starting trance: moving the listener from ordinary attention into focused, responsive absorption.

- `eye-fixation` (unit) **Eye fixation** - Have the listener hold their gaze on one point until the eyes tire and close.
  - Cues: 'pick a spot'; 'keep your eyes on'; 'stare at' / 'gaze at' / 'look at that point'; 'a little above eye level'; description of blurring, watering or blinking tied to continued looking
  - Not: `eye-closure` (eye-closure suggests the lids shutting; fixation is the staring that comes before); `eye-contact-imagery` (gaze capture uses imagined or on-screen eyes as an attention hold, not a tiring stare at a neutral point); `narrowing-attention` (narrowing says everything else fades; fixation gives the eyes one physical target)
- `pendulum-fixation` (variant, variant of `eye-fixation`) **Pendulum / pocket watch** - Have the listener follow a swinging object with their eyes until the eyes tire.
  - Cues: 'watch' / 'pendulum' / 'pendant' with 'swing'; 'back and forth'; 'follow it with your eyes'; 'side to side'; phrases split to a two-beat swing rhythm
  - Not: `spiral-fixation` (a spiral is a rotating visual on a screen; a pendulum is a swinging object tracked side to side); `metronome` (a metronome is an audio beat; the pendulum is a visual target, real or imagined); `suggestibility-test` (the Chevreul pendulum is held by the listener to show ideomotor movement, not watched to tire the eyes)
- `spiral-fixation` (variant, variant of `eye-fixation`) [text-detectable: partial] **Spiral fixation** - Have the listener stare into the centre of a rotating spiral or looping animation.
  - Cues: 'spiral' / 'swirl' / 'vortex'; 'look into the centre'; 'round and round'; 'turning' / 'spinning' with 'drawn in' or 'pulled in'; stage direction for on-screen visual
  - Not: `pendulum-fixation` (the pendulum swings side to side and is tracked; the spiral rotates around a fixed gaze); `visual-accompaniment` (that entry is the production choice to show visuals; this is the instruction to fixate on one); `staircase-deepener` (descent imagery deepens after trance starts; the spiral is the fixation target that starts it)
- `candle-fixation` (variant, variant of `eye-fixation`) **Candle / light fixation** - Have the listener gaze at a flame or small point of light until the eyes tire.
  - Cues: 'flame' / 'candle' / 'point of light'; 'gaze at' / 'watch the light'; 'flicker'; 'the room grows dim'; 'afterimage' / 'glow behind your eyelids'
  - Not: `embodied-metaphor` (light that fills the body is state-as-substance imagery; here the light is a thing to stare at); `spiral-fixation` (the spiral is a moving graphic; the flame is a near-still light source)
- `eye-roll-induction` (variant, variant of `eye-fixation`) **Eye roll** - Have the listener roll the eyes upward, close the lids over them, then let the eyes relax as the body lets go.
  - Cues: 'roll your eyes up' / 'look up toward your forehead / eyebrows'; 'keep looking up while your eyelids close'; 'let your eyes relax' following an upward gaze; 'float' right after eye release; three-step sequence: up, close, breathe out
  - Not: `eye-fixation` (the parent holds a stare for minutes at an outside point; the eye roll is a seconds-long upward strain with the lids…); `eye-catalepsy` (some eyelid-lock scripts also have the eyes rolled up; label the lock only when the text says the lids cannot open or…); `eye-closure` (eye-closure suggests heaviness until the lids close; here closing is a deliberate step)
- `peripheral-vision-induction` (variant, variant of `eye-fixation`) **Peripheral vision** - Have the listener fix on a point, then widen awareness to the edges of the visual field without moving the eyes.
  - Cues: 'peripheral vision' / 'edges of your vision' / 'corners of your eyes'; 'without moving your eyes'; 'widen' / 'spread' / 'expand your awareness'; 'all of it at once'; 'soft focus' / 'soften your gaze'
  - Not: `eye-fixation` (the parent narrows onto the point; this keeps the point and widens to the whole field); `narrowing-attention` (narrowing fades everything but one thing; this takes in everything at once); `betty-erickson-induction` (5-4-3-2-1 names individual seen items in turn; this holds the whole field with no naming)
- `eye-closure` (unit) **Eye-closure suggestion** - Suggest the eyelids growing heavy and closing, then treat the closing as the start of trance.
  - Cues: 'eyelids' with 'heavy' / 'heavier'; 'let your eyes close' / 'close your eyes'; 'each blink'; 'as your eyes close' followed by a trance claim; 'so heavy they want to close'
  - Not: `eye-fixation` (fixation is the staring; eye-closure is the suggestion that the lids shut); `eye-catalepsy` (the eyelid lock says the closed eyes cannot open and tests it; eye-closure only gets them shut); `heaviness-lightness` (that deepener spreads heaviness through the body; this one targets the lids and ends in closing)
- `progressive-relaxation` (unit) **Progressive relaxation** - Relax the body one region at a time, in a fixed order, until the whole body is at rest.
  - Cues: body parts named in anatomical order; 'relax' / 'loosen' / 'let go' repeated once per region; 'starting with your feet' / 'from the top of your head'; 'moving up into' / 'spreading down through'; 'your whole body' as the closing line
  - Not: `body-scan` (a body scan only notices sensation; progressive relaxation tells each part to relax); `kinesthetic-induction` (sensation focus stays on one sensation and lets it spread; this sweeps region by region); `heaviness-lightness` (that is a depth cue for the whole body, without an ordered tour)
- `tension-release` (variant, variant of `progressive-relaxation`) **Tense-and-release** - Have the listener tighten each muscle group for a few seconds, then let go, so the release is felt by contrast.
  - Cues: 'tense' / 'tighten' / 'squeeze' / 'clench' followed by 'release' / 'let go'; 'hold it'; 'feel the difference' / 'notice the contrast'; a counted hold ('hold for five'); tense-release pair repeated per body region
  - Not: `progressive-relaxation` (the parent only suggests relaxing; this variant commands a contraction first); `micro-compliance` (small tasks are used to build a habit of following; here the task is there for the physical contrast); `apposition-of-opposites` (that pairs two suggested experiences in one sentence; this is a physical act followed by its release)
- `autogenic-phrases` (variant, variant of `progressive-relaxation`) **Autogenic phrases** - Have the listener silently repeat set first-person phrases of heaviness and warmth, limb by limb.
  - Cues: first-person body statements: 'my arm is heavy', 'my legs are warm'; 'heavy and warm'; 'say to yourself' / 'repeat silently'; the same formula repeated verbatim several times; 'my heartbeat is calm' / 'my forehead is cool'
  - Not: `affirmation` (affirmations state traits or goals; autogenic phrases state present body sensations); `first-person-scripting` (that is the general I-form for any suggestion; this is a fixed set of body formulae used to induce); `mantra` (a mantra is one phrase repeated for its own sake; these are a sequence of formulae that move through the body)
- `body-scan` (unit) **Body scan** - Move the listener's attention through the body region by region, noticing sensation without telling anything to relax.
  - Cues: 'notice' / 'be aware of' / 'bring your attention to' with body parts in order; 'you don't need to change anything'; 'just notice'; lists of possible sensations offered without preference; absence of 'relax' commands in a region-by-region passage
  - Not: `progressive-relaxation` (the parent instructs each region to relax; the scan only observes); `pacing` (pacing states verifiable facts in any order to build acceptance; the scan is an ordered tour of the body); `kinesthetic-induction` (sensation focus picks one sensation and stays with it)
- `breath-induction` (unit) **Breath-focus induction** - Use attention to the breath, or a paced breathing pattern, as the way into trance.
  - Cues: 'take a deep breath' / 'breathe in... and out' as an opening instruction; 'notice your breathing' / 'follow your breath'; counted or paced breaths ('in for four, out for six'); description of the breath itself: 'the air coming in', 'the rise and fall of your chest'; phrase rhythm following inhale and exhale before any trance claim
  - Not: `breath-deepener` (a line that only attaches depth or release to each exhale is the deepener wherever it sits; label this entry when the…); `breathe-in-breathe-out` (that gives the in-breath something to take in and the out-breath something to let go, at any stage; this entry attends…); `breath-synced-delivery` (that is a delivery choice about timing the voice, not a script instruction to attend to the breath)
- `counting-induction` (unit) **Counting induction** - Count, usually downward, with each number tied to going further into trance and the last number named as arrival.
  - Cues: 'I'm going to count from ... to ...'; descending numerals spaced through a passage; 'with each number'; 'by the time I reach one' / 'when I reach zero'; a command or state claim on the last number
  - Not: `countdown-deepener` (same form; label the deepener when a trance claim or another induction comes before the count, and this entry when the…); `countdown-structure` (there the numbers only pace unrelated content; here each number is tied to going further in and the last number is…); `losing-numbers` (there the listener counts aloud and the numbers are suggested away; here the writer counts)
- `visualization-induction` (unit) **Visualization induction** - Lead the listener into an imagined scene and let absorption in the scene serve as the trance.
  - Cues: 'imagine' / 'picture' / 'see yourself'; 'a place where'; sensory inventory of a scene (light, sound, air, ground); 'as you settle into this place'; 'it can be real or imagined'
  - Not: `guided-imagery` (guided imagery is the general device at any stage; this is its use as the induction); `safe-place` (safe place is a scene kept as a base to return to; an induction scene need not be about safety); `staircase-deepener` (descent imagery maps downward movement to depth after trance has begun)
- `confusion-induction` (unit) **Confusion induction** - Overwork the listener's effort to make sense with tangled but grammatical language, then offer one clear instruction to escape into.
  - Cues: dense alternation of opposites (left/right, remember/forget, now/then, know/no); long grammatical sentences that do not resolve; stacked negatives; a short plain imperative right after a tangled passage; 'you don't need to follow' / 'it doesn't matter'
  - Not: `confusion-technique` (the general device at any stage; this entry is its use as the induction); `overload-induction` (overload gives too many items to track at once; confusion gives one stream that cannot be made sense of); `rapid-induction` (a rapid induction uses a sudden interruption of seconds, not a sustained verbal tangle)
- `overload-induction` (unit) **Overload induction** - Give the listener more things to track at once than they can hold, so they stop tracking and follow the voice.
  - Cues: several concurrent attention instructions in one passage; 'while' / 'at the same time' / 'and also' / 'still' chains; a hard counting task ('backward by threes', 'by sevens'); 'let it all go' / 'drop all of it' after the stack; explicit mention of too much to hold or track
  - Not: `cognitive-overload` (the general device at any stage; this entry is its use as the induction); `confusion-induction` (confusion makes one stream senseless; overload makes several clear streams too many); `betty-erickson-induction` (5-4-3-2-1 lists sense items one after another in a set pattern, not all at once)
- `rapid-induction` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Rapid induction** - Induce in seconds by breaking an expected action or startling the listener, and giving a firm 'sleep' command in that moment.
  - Cues: 'sleep!' / 'drop' as a one-word command; stage direction for a snap, pull, drop or interrupted gesture; sentence broken off before the command; immediate 'deeper' language after the command; 'in a moment I will...' setup followed by a sudden cue
  - Not: `reinduction-trigger` (a re-induction trigger fires a cue installed earlier; a rapid induction needs no earlier installation); `pattern-interrupt` (the interrupt is the single break in an automatic sequence; rapid induction is the interrupt plus a trance command); `shock-surprise` (a startle alone is that unit; here it is used to open a moment for 'sleep')
- `handshake-induction` (variant, variant of `rapid-induction`) [text-detectable: partial] **Handshake interrupt** - Start a handshake, break it off partway, and suggest trance while the listener's hand hangs with no next step.
  - Cues: stage direction mentioning handshake or offered hand; 'look at your hand' immediately after a greeting; hand left raised or suspended; 'only as fast as' linking the hand lowering to trance; narrated scene of a handshake that does not finish
  - Not: `arm-levitation` (levitation suggests the hand rising by itself over minutes; here the operator places it and it stays); `arm-catalepsy` (the rigid-arm convincer is a tested proof; the suspended hand here is a by-product of the interrupt); `hand-drop-induction` (the hand drop removes a support the listener is pressing on; the handshake breaks a social routine)
- `hand-drop-induction` (variant, variant of `rapid-induction`) [text-detectable: partial] **Hand drop** - Have the listener press down on your hand, then pull it away suddenly and command 'sleep'.
  - Cues: 'press down on my hand'; 'press harder'; stage direction to remove or drop the hand; 'sleep' straight after a pressing instruction; 'let your hand fall' paired with 'and go down with it'
  - Not: `physical-deepener` (the arm-drop deepener lifts and drops a limp limb in trance; this drop starts the trance); `arm-pull-induction` (the arm pull is a tug toward the operator; the hand drop is a support taken away); `handshake-induction` (the handshake leaves the hand suspended; here it falls)
- `arm-pull-induction` (variant, variant of `rapid-induction`) [text-detectable: partial] **Arm pull** - Give a sudden small tug on the listener's arm together with a 'sleep' command.
  - Cues: stage direction with 'tug' / 'pull' / 'jerk' on the arm or wrist; 'sleep' simultaneous with a pull; 'loose and limp' immediately after; instruction to support head or shoulder
  - Not: `hand-drop-induction` (the hand drop removes a support; the arm pull adds a tug); `handshake-induction` (the handshake interrupt breaks a social routine without any pull); `physical-deepener` (the deepener drops a relaxed limb; the pull is the inducing startle)
- `conversational-induction` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Conversational induction** - Induce inside what sounds like ordinary talk, with no formal ritual and no command to go into trance.
  - Cues: no announced induction, count or 'close your eyes' before trance language appears; run of pacing statements about the present moment; 'you've probably had the experience of'; 'I wonder' / 'I don't know whether' followed by a trance response; anecdote about someone else relaxing or drifting
  - Not: `covert-induction` (covert means the listener has not been told an induction is happening; conversational is about the form and can be…); `pacing-and-leading` (that is one building block; the conversational induction is a whole opening made from several); `indirect-suggestion` (an indirect suggestion is a single phrasing; this is an induction built from many)
- `covert-induction` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Unannounced induction** - Run an induction while presenting it as something else, such as a story, an exercise or a relaxation tip.
  - Cues: explicit denial: 'this isn't hypnosis' / 'not a trance'; 'just a story' / 'just an exercise' / 'a quick relaxation tip' followed by induction…; 'before we start' preceding what is already the induction; trance vocabulary appearing only after the cover activity is under way; mismatch between the stated purpose and the suggestions given
  - Not: `conversational-induction` (the parent may be openly agreed hypnosis; this variant hides or mislabels it); `fiction-frame` (a fiction frame openly says the content is a story or play; covert induction hides what the procedure is); `my-friend-john` (that delivers suggestions through a described third person, with or without concealment)
- `revivification-induction` (unit) **Revivification induction** - Have the listener recall a previous trance, or any deeply absorbed moment, in detail until the state comes back.
  - Cues: 'remember the last time you' / 'think back to a time when'; 'a time you were deeply relaxed / in trance'; sensory questions about a memory; tense shift from past to present inside the passage; 'and as you remember it, you can feel it now'
  - Not: `revivification` (the general device relives any memory for its state; this is its use to start trance); `reinduction-trigger` (a trigger fires an installed cue at once; this rebuilds the state through detailed recall); `trance-as-everyday` (that compares trance to everyday absorption to normalize it, without reliving a specific memory)
- `pretend-induction` (unit) **Pretend / as-if induction** - Ask the listener to pretend to be hypnotized and act the part; the acting turns into the response.
  - Cues: 'pretend' / 'act as if' / 'make believe' applied to trance or a trance response; 'just imagine what it would be like to be hypnotized'; 'you don't have to be hypnotized'; 'as long as you keep pretending'; 'forget that you are pretending'
  - Not: `as-if-frame` (the as-if frame explores any imagined outcome; this applies pretending to being hypnotized, to induce); `fiction-frame` (a fiction frame labels the whole content as play; here only the listener's own trance behaviour is pretended); `eye-catalepsy` (the eyelid lock is the tested convincer; pretending is one way of setting it up)
- `repetition-induction` (unit) **Repetition induction** - Induce by repeating one phrase or short loop of phrases, steadily, for long enough that attention settles on it.
  - Cues: same phrase repeated verbatim four or more times in a row; short loop of lines cycling in fixed order; 'again' / 'once more' / 'keep repeating'; stage direction 'repeat for N minutes' or 'loop'; very low vocabulary variety across an opening passage
  - Not: `mantra` (same surface form, and mantra is always a correct label for the repeated block; add this label only when that block is…); `deepening-by-repetition` (that repeats depth words after trance has begun); `boredom-monotony` (monotony is uneventful delivery of any content; this is literal repetition of one phrase)
- `ideomotor-induction` (unit) **Ideomotor induction** - Suggest a small movement that feels as if it happens by itself, and use that movement as the way into trance.
  - Cues: 'by itself' / 'on its own' / 'without you doing it' about a movement; 'you may notice a small twitch / movement'; a movement tied to trance: 'as it moves, you go deeper'; instructions to hold out a hand or hands and watch; 'just let it happen'
  - Not: `ideomotor-signal` (a signal is a small movement used as a yes/no answer, not as the route into trance); `suggestibility-test` (the same movements used before induction as a demonstration, without a trance claim, are a test); `body-control` (puppeteering says the speaker moves the body as proof of control; here the movement is the entry procedure)
- `arm-levitation` (variant, variant of `ideomotor-induction`) **Arm levitation** - Suggest that a hand grows light and lifts by itself, with trance deepening as it rises or when it touches the face.
  - Cues: 'lighter and lighter'; 'lifting' / 'floating up' / 'rising' about a hand or arm; 'balloon' / 'string at the wrist'; 'which hand will lift first'; 'when it touches your face'
  - Not: `heavy-light-arms` (book and balloon contrasts a heavy arm with a light one as a quick test or convincer); `arm-catalepsy` (catalepsy suggests a rigid arm that cannot bend; levitation suggests a light arm that moves); `magnetic-hands` (magnetic hands draws two hands together sideways; levitation lifts one hand)
- `magnetic-hands` (variant, variant of `ideomotor-induction`) **Magnetic hands** - Suggest that the hands or fingers are pulled together as if by magnets, and tie trance to the moment they touch.
  - Cues: 'magnet' / 'magnets' / 'magnetic'; 'palms facing' / 'fingers apart'; 'pulling together' / 'drawn together' / 'closer and closer'; 'the moment they touch'; 'you're not doing it'
  - Not: `suggestibility-test` (the same exercise used before induction with no trance claim at contact is a test); `stuck-convincer` (stuck suggests the hands cannot come apart; this suggests they come together); `arm-levitation` (levitation lifts one hand slowly; this draws two together quickly)
- `elman-induction` (recipe) **Elman induction** - Eye closure and eyelid lock, repeated opening and closing of the eyes, an arm drop, then counting backward until the numbers are gone.
  - Cues: 'relax your eyelids to the point where they won't work'; 'test them' followed by 'stop testing'; 'open your eyes... close your eyes' repeated with 'deeper' each time; arm lifted and dropped 'like a wet dishcloth' / 'limp'; 'count backward from one hundred' with 'let the numbers go' / 'all gone?'
  - Not: `rapid-induction` (rapid inductions take seconds and use a startle; Elman takes minutes and uses a chain of passed tests); `progressive-relaxation` (Elman relaxes in two or three quick waves, not a slow region-by-region tour); `losing-numbers` (losing the numbers is the final step on its own; the recipe is the whole sequence)
- `betty-erickson-induction` (recipe) **Betty Erickson 5-4-3-2-1** - Name five things seen, heard and felt, then four of each, then three, two, one, moving from outer to inner experience.
  - Cues: 'five things you can see' (or hear / feel); see-hear-feel triplets with a falling number; 'now I am aware that I see / hear / feel'; 'it's fine if you repeat one'; 'if you lose count'
  - Not: `counting-induction` (there the writer counts down with suggestions; here the listener lists sense items in shrinking sets); `overload-induction` (overload stacks tasks simultaneously; this is one orderly task done in rounds); `pacing` (pacing is any verifiable statement; this is a fixed pattern of them used as a whole induction)
- `double-induction` (recipe) [text-detectable: partial] **Double induction** - Two voices speak at the same time, one in each ear, so the listener cannot follow both and stops trying.
  - Cues: two labelled voices or tracks in parallel ('Left / Right', 'Voice A / Voice B'); two-column script layout; stage direction: 'simultaneous', 'overlapping', 'pan left/right'; 'you can't follow both' / 'don't try to follow'; marked sync words spoken by both voices
  - Not: `layered-voices` (layering is the production choice at any stage; double induction is the induction built on it); `overload-induction` (overload can be done by one voice stacking tasks; this needs two concurrent speech streams); `subliminal-track` (a subliminal track is mixed below easy hearing; both voices here are fully audible)
- `kinesthetic-induction` (unit) **Sensation-focus induction** - Narrow the listener's attention onto one bodily sensation, then let that sensation grow and spread.
  - Cues: 'put all your attention into your [body part]'; one sensation word repeated: 'warmth', 'weight', 'tingling'; 'the more you notice it, the stronger it gets'; 'let it spread'; sustained passage on a single body part before any other is named
  - Not: `progressive-relaxation` (that tours regions in order and tells each to relax; this stays with one sensation and lets it spread); `suggested-sensation` (that is a single suggested feeling at any stage, often offered as proof; label this entry only for a sustained opening…); `travelling-sensation` (there the movement of the sensation through the body, region by region, is the whole device; here the spread is the…)
- `arousal-induction` (variant, variant of `kinesthetic-induction`) **Arousal-focus induction** - Use the listener's rising arousal as the sensation that absorbs and narrows attention.
  - Cues: 'arousal' / 'that warm feeling' / 'pleasure' as the object of 'focus on' / 'notice'; 'the more aroused... the deeper / the less you think'; arousal language preceding any relaxation or eye-closure language; 'let it pull you down'; build-up verbs: 'growing', 'building', 'rising' applied to the sensation
  - Not: `pleasure-deepener` (the deepener ties pleasure to depth once trance exists; this uses arousal to start it); `the-more-the-more` (leverage uses arousal to get a suggestion accepted; this uses it as the fixation for induction); `kinesthetic-induction` (same method with a neutral sensation such as warmth or weight)
- `reinduction-trigger` (unit) **Re-induction trigger** - Use a cue installed in an earlier session to return the listener to trance at once.
  - Cues: a short cue phrase standing alone: 'deep sleep', 'drop', 'sleep now'; 'you know what happens when' / 'you remember this word'; 'back to where you were last time'; 'if you have listened before'; snap or tone stage direction followed by depth language with no induction passage before…
  - Not: `trigger-installation` (installation states the cue and its response for future use; this is the cue being used to induce); `rapid-induction` (a rapid induction needs no prior cue and relies on a startle or interrupt); `reinduction-seed` (the seed is a general suggestion before waking that future sessions go faster, with no specific cue)
- `induction-by-reading` (unit) **Text induction** - Use the act of reading itself, the eyes moving line by line and the inner voice saying the words, as the fixation.
  - Cues: 'as you read' / 'reading these words'; 'your eyes move along the line' / 'down the page'; 'the voice in your head reading this'; 'each line' / 'the next word'; 'screen' / 'page' named as the focus
  - Not: `eye-fixation` (fixation holds a still point until the eyes close; reading keeps the eyes moving and open); `inner-voice` (inner-voice suggestion says the speaker's words become the listener's thoughts as a lasting change; here the reading…); `meta-commentary` (naming the technique is commentary on method; this describes the reader's act of reading in order to pace it)
- `waking-hypnosis` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Waking hypnosis** - Induce and work with the eyes open and the listener alert, using focus and direct suggestion with no sleep, drowsiness or relaxation language.
  - Cues: 'keep your eyes open', 'eyes open the whole time', 'you don't need to close your eyes'; 'wide awake', 'fully alert', 'more alert', 'more energy' used in the induction, not at…; explicit denial of relaxation or sleep: 'you don't need to relax', 'this is not sleep'; direct suggestions and tests (lock, stick, cannot lift) appearing with no eye closure,…; focus vocabulary in place of depth vocabulary: 'sharper', 'clearer', 'locked on',…
  - Not: `partial-emergence` (that opens the eyes after a conventional trance and declares it ongoing; waking hypnosis starts eyes-open and never…); `conversational-induction` (that is defined by sounding like ordinary talk with no announced procedure; waking hypnosis is announced and usually…); `suggestibility-test` (a test before the induction claims no trance; here the same exercises are the induction and are followed by working…)
- `gateway-induction` (recipe) [text-detectable: partial] **Gateway induction** - The Monroe Institute sequence: box away concerns, breathe with a sustained hum, recite an affirmation, then count the body to sleep while the mind stays awake, over binaural tones.
  - Cues: 'box', 'chest' or 'container' for worries at the very start, before any relaxation; breathing with humming or toning: 'hum', 'tone', 'resonant', 'let the sound vibrate'; a set first-person statement of purpose recited before the relaxation; 'body asleep, mind awake' / 'mind awake, body asleep'; a count from one up to ten with a body region put to sleep at each number
  - Not: `binaural-beats` (that is the tone bed alone, under any script; the recipe is the spoken sequence that runs over it); `body-dissociation` (that is the single suggestion that the body is asleep or distant; here it is the end point of a fixed sequence); `progressive-relaxation` (that is a region-by-region relaxation used alone; here it is one counted step after three preparatory ones)

## Deepening (`deepening`)

Ways of intensifying trance after the induction, so the listener is more absorbed and more ready to respond before the main suggestions start.

- `countdown-deepener` (unit) **Countdown deepener** - Count down, usually from ten or five, and tell the listener that each number takes them deeper.
  - Cues: a descending number series (ten, nine, eight / 5, 4, 3) inside an established trance; 'with each number', 'with every number I say'; 'by the time I reach one', 'when I reach zero'; number followed directly by a depth word: 'Seven. Deeper.'; 'twice as deep', 'ten times deeper' attached to a count
  - Not: `counting-induction` (same form used to start trance; choose this entry only when trance has already been declared before the count begins); `countdown-structure` (numbers only pace other content; here the script says each number itself takes the listener deeper); `losing-numbers` (there the listener does the counting and the numbers are made to disappear)
- `staircase-deepener` (unit) **Descent imagery** - Have the listener imagine moving downward, by stairs, lift or slow sinking, and map each bit of descent onto going deeper.
  - Cues: 'staircase', 'steps leading down', 'elevator', 'escalator', 'lift'; 'down, and down', 'sinking', 'drifting down', 'descending'; 'each step takes you deeper', 'floor by floor'; 'at the bottom you find', 'when you reach the bottom'; a count paired with steps or floors
  - Not: `countdown-deepener` (a bare count with no imagined movement; a counted staircase takes both labels); `visualization-induction` (a scene used to start trance, not built on descent); `heaviness-lightness` (a body sensation of weight, with no journey or scene)
- `false-bottom` (variant, variant of `staircase-deepener`) **False bottom** - When the listener reaches the announced bottom of a descent, declare that it is only the top of a deeper level, or that there is no bottom at all.
  - Cues: 'you thought that was the bottom', 'that was only', 'this is just the beginning'; 'another staircase', 'a door at the bottom', 'a level below this one', 'the floor opens'; 'there is no bottom', 'bottomless', 'no limit to how deep'; 'deeper than you thought possible', 'deeper than the bottom'; a second descent or count that starts immediately after a completed one
  - Not: `staircase-deepener` (the parent is any mapped descent; this is the specific move of revoking its stated end point); `nested-trance` (that runs a second induction inside the imagined scene; this extends the same descent with no new induction); `fractionation` (fractionation brings the listener partly up before each drop; here there is no rise, only a further drop)
- `fractionation` (unit) **Fractionation** - Bring the listener partly up, then send them back down, several times, saying each return goes deeper than the last.
  - Cues: 'open your eyes ... and close them' repeated two or more times; 'each time', 'every time you go back', 'further than before', 'deeper than last time'; a short count up followed immediately by 'sleep', 'drop' or 'down'; 'halfway up', 'just to the surface', 'not all the way awake'; the same drop word used at the end of each cycle
  - Not: `reinduction-trigger` (fires an installed cue once; fractionation is the repeated cycle, though it often uses and builds such a cue); `partial-emergence` (wakes the body and stops there, with no drop back); `fractionated-structure` (the whole session built on wake-and-drop cycles; label the unit for a passage)
- `breath-deepener` (unit) **Breath-linked deepening** - Tell the listener that every breath out takes them a little deeper, so their own breathing does the deepening.
  - Cues: 'with each breath', 'with every breath'; 'every time you exhale', 'each breath out', 'as you breathe out'; breath phrase followed by a depth comparative: 'deeper', 'further', 'more relaxed'; 'your breathing takes you', 'let each breath carry you'
  - Not: `breath-induction` (directs or paces the breath to start trance; this only attaches depth to breathing that is already happening); `breathe-in-breathe-out` (gives the in-breath one thing to take in and the out-breath another to let go; this ties the breath to depth only,…); `deepening-by-linkage` (names any and every stimulus; this names the breath only)
- `deepening-by-linkage` (unit) **Everything deepens** - Declare that whatever the listener hears, feels or thinks from now on sends them deeper.
  - Cues: 'every word', 'every sound', 'each word I say' followed by a depth result; 'anything you hear', 'everything you notice', 'whatever happens'; 'only takes you deeper', 'sends you further down', 'becomes a signal to sink'; a list of stimuli ending in a generalisation ('...everything'); 'even' + a likely distraction or doubt + a depth result
  - Not: `incorporation` (names particular likely interruptions and says they will not disturb or simply belong; choose this entry when the…); `breath-deepener` (one named trigger, the breath); `voice-focus` (narrows attention to the voice without claiming the voice deepens)
- `silence-deepener` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Silence / pause deepener** - Announce a stretch of silence, say that depth grows during it, then stop talking.
  - Cues: 'I'm going to be quiet', 'I'll stop talking for a moment'; 'in the silence', 'during this quiet time'; 'when you hear my voice again', 'when I speak again'; stage directions: [pause], [silence 30s], (long pause); a ratification straight after a marked gap: 'that's right, deeper'
  - Not: `silence` (the delivery choice of leaving gaps; this entry needs the spoken frame that gives the gap a job); `ellipsis-pause` (short written pauses for rhythm, not a framed stretch of silence); `time-in-trance` (also uses a gap, but suggests the gap feels far longer than it is)
- `losing-numbers` (unit) **Losing the numbers** - Have the listener count backward from one hundred, relaxing after each number, until the numbers fade and cannot be found.
  - Cues: 'count backward from one hundred' given as a task to the listener; 'deeper relaxed' after each number; 'let the numbers fade', 'relax them away', 'push them out'; 'are they all gone?', 'when the numbers are gone'; 'double your relaxation' with each number
  - Not: `countdown-deepener` (the writer counts and the numbers stay; here the listener counts and the numbers vanish); `name-amnesia` (a convincer staged to prove trance by forgetting an item; here the forgetting is the route to depth); `dual-task` (gives the mind a counting job to occupy it; here the job is suggested away)
- `depth-scale` (unit) **Depth scale** - Give depth a number, level or dial, then move the number and tell the listener their depth moves with it.
  - Cues: 'on a scale of', 'from one to ten' applied to depth or relaxation; 'dial', 'meter', 'gauge', 'needle', 'level' with a number; 'you are at a six', 'level three', 'down to a four'; 'turn it down', 'watch the number fall'; 'what number are you at?' (interactive scripts)
  - Not: `countdown-deepener` (numbers there are steps in a spoken series, not readings of a position); `anchoring-number` (a one-off magnitude with no scale ('ten times deeper'); choose this entry when a scale is named with end points or…); `control-device-imagery` (the general imagined dial or switch for any quantity; label both when the dial sets depth)
- `cognitive-quieting` (unit) **Mind-blanking** - Suggest that thoughts slow down, drift off or stop, leaving the mind quiet and blank.
  - Cues: 'thoughts slowing', 'thoughts fading', 'thoughts drifting away'; 'blank', 'empty', 'quiet mind', 'mind goes still'; 'nothing to think', 'no need to think', 'thinking is too much effort'; 'let it drift past', thought-as-cloud or thought-as-leaf images; 'switch off', 'turn down the chatter'
  - Not: `critical-faculty-bypass` (says the analytical or judging part steps aside; this reduces all thought); `distraction-of-conscious-mind` (gives the mind a job to do; this takes jobs away); `skill-capacity-suggestion` (a lasting change in the ability to think; this is a passing quiet inside the session)
- `heaviness-lightness` (unit) **Heaviness / floating** - Suggest the body growing heavy and sinking, or weightless and floating, and treat that feeling as a sign of depth.
  - Cues: 'heavy', 'heavier', 'like lead', 'limp', 'loose'; 'sinking into the bed / chair / floor'; 'floating', 'weightless', 'light as', 'drifting'; 'too heavy to move', 'too much effort to lift'; weight word linked to depth: 'the heavier ... the deeper'
  - Not: `progressive-relaxation` (works through body regions in order; this names one whole-body weight feeling); `staircase-deepener` (sinking down through levels, steps or floors is descent imagery; sinking into the surface under the body, with no…); `heavy-light-arms` (a convincer where the arms visibly drift apart as proof)
- `physical-deepener` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Physical drop deepener** - Lift the listener's arm, say that when it drops they go deeper, and let it fall.
  - Cues: 'when your hand drops', 'as it lands', 'the moment it touches your lap'; 'lift your arm / hand' followed by 'let it drop / fall'; 'limp', 'like a wet cloth', 'dead weight'; stage directions for touch: [lift arm], [release wrist]; 'drop with it'
  - Not: `hand-drop-induction` (a rapid induction from waking, using surprise; this is an announced drop inside trance); `fractionation` (repeated up-and-down cycles of the trance itself; this is one physical event); `arm-catalepsy` (the arm is made rigid as proof; here it is made limp and falls)
- `pleasure-deepener` (unit) **Pleasure as deepener** - Tie depth to pleasure, so that going deeper feels good or feeling good takes the listener deeper; often stated both ways.
  - Cues: 'the better it feels, the deeper you go' and its reverse; 'feels so good to' + sink / drop / let go; 'wave of pleasure / comfort / warmth' attached to a depth step; pleasure and depth words in the same clause, joined by 'and', 'as' or 'the more'; 'deeper feels better'
  - Not: `the-more-the-more` (the general one-way yoke of any two quantities; choose this entry when one term is pleasure, comfort or arousal and…); `compounding-loop` (defined by the stated return (A feeds B and B feeds A) with any terms; a pleasure-depth link stated in both directions…); `pleasure-reward` (a burst of pleasure given after a specific act of compliance; here pleasure is tied to depth itself)
- `vicarious-deepening` (unit) **Vicarious deepening** - Describe another person going into trance, step by step, and let the listener follow along with them.
  - Cues: third-person pronouns (he, she, they) as the subject of trance verbs: sinks, drifts,…; 'watch as', 'picture someone', 'imagine a person who'; a described character showing relaxation signs in sequence; a bridging line from the character to 'you': 'just like', 'the same way', 'you too'; quoted deepening suggestions spoken to the character
  - Not: `my-friend-john` (suggestions are addressed to a stand-in as a whole induction or sequence, often from a cold start; here a person is…); `modeling` (shows someone performing a target act and being rewarded for it, as a pattern for later behavior; here what is…); `character-identification` (the listener becomes the character; here they watch from outside and follow)
- `nested-trance` (unit) **Trance within trance** - Inside the imagined scene of the trance, have the listener be hypnotized again, so the second trance stacks on the first.
  - Cues: 'close your eyes again' when eyes are already closed; 'a trance within this trance', 'inside this dream', 'hypnotized here too'; an induction (count, fixation, eye closure) performed on the listener inside an imagined…; an in-scene hypnotist or voice hypnotizing 'you'; layered exits: 'first, come back to the room in your mind, then...'
  - Not: `fractionation` (repeats exit and re-entry at the same level; this adds a level without leaving); `false-bottom` (declares the reached bottom to be the top of a further descent; here a new induction is run on the imagined self…); `nested-loops` (stories opened inside stories to carry suggestions, with no second induction)
- `approach-deepener` (unit) **Approach deepener** - Tie depth to something coming steadily closer, such as a light, footsteps or a voice, and promise full depth at the moment it arrives.
  - Cues: 'closer and closer', 'nearer and nearer', 'coming toward you'; 'the closer it comes, the deeper you go'; 'when it reaches you', 'when it touches you', 'when it arrives' followed by a depth or…; a distant object or sound named first, then restated at shorter distances; the listener is stationary while the described thing moves
  - Not: `staircase-deepener` (there the listener moves downward; here the listener is still and something moves toward them); `anticipation-building` (anticipation announces an event and delays it to raise wanting; here the approach itself is mapped onto depth step by…); `countdown-deepener` (a count ties depth to numbers; an approach ties it to distance, and a counted approach takes both labels)

## Emergence (`emergence`)

Ways of ending a session, or of deliberately leaving it open: waking, handing off to sleep, looping, and the suggestions placed at the exit.

- `count-up-emergence` (unit) **Count-up wake** - Count upward, usually one to five, giving each number a step of returning alertness, and end on a clear wake instruction.
  - Cues: ascending number sequence near the end of the script (one... two... three... four... five); 'I will count from one to', 'on the count of five', 'when I reach five'; 'wide awake', 'fully alert', 'eyes open' on or after the last number; each number followed by a returning-alertness clause ('energy returning', 'more awake…; 'coming up', 'coming back', 'all the way up'
  - Not: `countdown-deepener` (counts toward depth, not toward alertness, whatever direction the numbers run); `fractionation` (a count-up followed by another drop is one cycle of fractionation, not the ending); `self-paced-emergence` (the listener sets the timing there; here the count does)
- `suggestion-sealing` (unit) **Sealing suggestions before waking** - Just before waking, state that the session's suggestions stay in place and keep working afterward.
  - Cues: 'stays with you', 'carry this with you', 'bring this back with you'; 'locked in', 'sealed', 'fixed in place', 'settling in permanently'; collective reference to prior suggestions ('everything I have said', 'all of these…; 'even after you wake', 'long after this session ends'; position: last passage before the wake count or sleep handoff
  - Not: `recap-structure` (a recap restates the suggestions; sealing only says they persist); `posthypnotic-suggestion` (that specifies a particular later response, usually on a cue; sealing is a blanket persistence statement); `permanence-claim` (that asserts irreversibility of a change; sealing says the suggestions carry past the wake)
- `partial-emergence` (unit) **Partial wake** - Bring back the eyes, body or speech while stating that the trance, or the responsiveness to suggestion, continues.
  - Cues: 'open your eyes' or 'wake' paired with 'still', 'remain', 'stay' ('eyes open, still deep'); 'without waking', 'while staying in trance', 'only your eyes wake'; 'awake but', 'your body wakes while your mind stays'; 'just as suggestible', 'still under', 'still mine' after a wake instruction; a wake count that stops short of its announced end, or is followed by continued…
  - Not: `fractionation` (fractionation brings the listener up in order to drop them again; a partial wake holds them up and under); `conditional-wake` (that withholds the wake until a command or condition and restores nothing; a partial wake restores eyes, movement or…); `waking-hypnosis` (that induces and runs trance with eyes open from the start; a partial wake begins from an established eyes-closed…)
- `sleep-ending` (unit) **Drift into sleep** - End with no wake-up: invite the listener to pass from trance into ordinary sleep and wake naturally later.
  - Cues: 'drift into sleep', 'natural sleep', 'sleep through the night' in the closing passage; 'when you wake in the morning', 'wake at your usual time'; no count-up and no 'wide awake' anywhere in the ending; 'no need to wake', 'I will not wake you', 'my voice fades as you sleep'; closing lines that shorten and trail off, often into silence or music
  - Not: `loop-ending` (also has no wakener, but returns to the start instead of releasing the listener into sleep); `self-paced-emergence` (that hands over the timing of waking; this replaces waking with sleep); `rapid-induction` ('sleep' as a command there means trance, at the start, not real sleep at the end)
- `loop-ending` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Loop back** - Write the last lines to lead straight into the first lines, so the file can repeat with no wake and no seam.
  - Cues: last lines repeat or closely echo the first lines of the script; 'again', 'once more', 'begin again', 'back to the beginning', 'around and around' in the…; no wake count and no sleep handoff at the end; 'each time through', 'every loop', 'with every repeat'; stage or production notes: 'loop', 'loopable', 'repeat', 'seamless'
  - Not: `loop-structure` (the structure is the whole file built to repeat; this is the join at its end); `refrain` (a refrain recurs inside one pass; a loop back restarts the whole script); `repetition-induction` (that repeats a short phrase to induce; this repeats the entire file)
- `reinduction-seed` (unit) **Next-time-deeper suggestion** - Before waking, suggest that every future session will take hold faster and go deeper than this one.
  - Cues: 'next time', 'each time', 'every time you listen', 'whenever you return'; comparatives of depth or speed in future tense ('faster', 'deeper', 'more easily',…; 'begin where you left off', 'pick up from this depth'; practice framing ('like any skill', 'the more you practise'); position: late in the script, before the wake count
  - Not: `reinduction-trigger` (the trigger is a specific installed cue that drops the listener at once; the seed only sets expectation for future…); `posthypnotic-suggestion` (the general form; when the later response is trance itself coming faster or deeper and no cue is named, label only the…); `trigger-reinforcement` (that strengthens one named trigger with each use, not trance in general)
- `self-paced-emergence` (unit) **Self-paced wake** - Tell the listener to come back in their own time, when they are ready, instead of waking them on a count.
  - Cues: 'when you are ready', 'in your own time', 'at your own pace', 'take all the time you need'; 'whenever you like', 'no hurry', 'not before' governing eye opening or return; wake instruction with no count attached; 'only as quickly as', 'as soon as your mind has' followed by a return; long pause, silence or music-only stage direction after the instruction
  - Not: `count-up-emergence` (the writer sets the timing there with numbers); `conditional-wake` (there the speaker's command or a set condition must be met and waking is stated as unavailable until then; here the…); `sleep-ending` (that replaces waking with sleep; here the listener still wakes, on their own clock)
- `gentle-reorientation` (unit) **Reorientation check** - Explicitly restore normal body feeling, time, place and alertness, and cancel any session effect that should not continue.
  - Cues: 'back to normal', 'return to normal', 'in every way' applied to body, mind or sensation; 'any heaviness / numbness / stiffness is gone', 'all feeling returns'; outward orientation ('the room around you', 'the surface beneath you', 'here and now',…; movement checks ('wiggle your fingers and toes', 'stretch', 'look around'); 'clear-headed', 'fully present', 'fully back'
  - Not: `count-up-emergence` (a count restores alertness by steps; the check itemizes what is restored and cancels leftovers); `aftercare` (aftercare is emotional settling and reassurance after waking; this is restoring function and orientation); `trigger-removal` (that cancels something meant to act after the session, a trigger or posthypnotic suggestion, by name; this returns…)
- `positive-closing-suggestions` (unit) **Wellbeing suggestions** - Before waking, give general suggestions of calm, good sleep, confidence and feeling well for the rest of the day.
  - Cues: 'you'll sleep deeply'; 'feel calm / confident / refreshed'; 'each day ... more'; general positive suggestions just before waking; 'carry this calm with you'
  - Not: `posthypnotic-suggestion` (specifies one later response, usually tied to a cue and to the session's topic; wellbeing suggestions are general,…); `suggestion-sealing` (says the session's own suggestions stay, and adds no new content); `aftercare` (settles the reaction to intense content now, not lasting wellbeing)
- `conditional-wake` (unit) **Conditional wake** - State that the listener stays in trance, and cannot leave it, until the speaker gives the wake command or a named condition is met.
  - Cues: 'until I say', 'until I wake you', 'until you hear', 'not before' attached to waking or…; 'you cannot wake', 'you can't come up', 'no way out', 'locked in', 'held here'; 'only my voice can wake you', 'only when I count', 'only I can bring you back'; 'you stay in trance until' followed by a task, a count, a word or the end of the session; 'trying to wake only takes you deeper'
  - Not: `partial-emergence` (that returns eyes, movement or speech while trance continues; this returns nothing and makes the full wake depend on a…); `eye-catalepsy` (that locks one movement and stages a test of it; this locks the state and usually stages no test); `phobia-of-leaving` (that attaches dread to stopping; a conditional wake asserts the listener cannot stop yet, with no threat attached)

## Suggestion forms (`suggestion-form`)

The ways a single suggestion can be worded: as a command, an offer, a link, a question, a quotation or a hint.

- `direct-suggestion` (unit) **Direct suggestion** - Say plainly what the listener will do or feel, as an instruction or a statement of fact.
  - Cues: second-person imperative: 'close', 'relax', 'let go', 'sink'; unhedged declaratives: 'you are', 'you will', 'your X is becoming'; no modal softeners ('may', 'might', 'perhaps', 'can') in the sentence; 'now' attached to a command; one named response per short sentence
  - Not: `permissive-suggestion` (permissive wording offers the response ('you may'); direct wording tells); `indirect-suggestion` (indirect suggestion never states the wanted response as an instruction); `embedded-command` (an embedded command is an imperative hidden inside a sentence about something else)
- `indirect-suggestion` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Indirect suggestion** - Get the response across by hint, story, general remark or possibility, without ever telling the listener to do it.
  - Cues: third-person or generic subjects: 'some people', 'a person', 'many listeners'; a remark about the room, the hour or the speaker's own state that names the wanted state…; possibility words: 'perhaps', 'might', 'sooner or later'; the response is described but no sentence instructs the listener to do it; talk about a topic (a garden, a journey) whose terms match the wanted state
  - Not: `permissive-suggestion` (permissive wording still names the listener's response openly; it only softens the demand); `implication` (implication is the specific case where the listener must infer an unstated claim from what is said); `metaphor` (metaphor carries the suggestion in a parallel story or image; label that when a story is present)
- `permissive-suggestion` (unit) **Permissive suggestion** - Offer the response as something the listener can, may or might do, instead of ordering it.
  - Cues: 'you can', 'you may', 'you might', 'you could'; 'allow yourself to', 'let yourself', 'let your'; 'if you like', 'whenever you're ready', 'when you want to'; 'feel free to'; a named response with no imperative verb governing it
  - Not: `permission-giving` (permission-giving lifts a restraint on something the listener is holding back ('it's okay to'); this is a general soft…); `modal-operator` (modal-operator labels the modal word itself wherever it occurs; this labels a whole suggestion offered as optional); `indirect-suggestion` (indirect suggestion does not state the response to the listener at all)
- `contingent-suggestion` (unit) **Contingent suggestion** - Tie the wanted response to something already happening or sure to happen, so one brings the other.
  - Cues: 'as you ..., you ...'; 'while', 'when', 'as soon as' joining an ongoing act to a response; 'each time', 'every time', 'with every breath/word/number'; first clause is a verifiable or inevitable event; second clause is the suggested response; 'and as it does', 'and when that happens'
  - Not: `compound-suggestion` (compound suggestion joins with plain 'and' and states no dependence between the halves); `cause-effect-claim` (cause-effect claim asserts that X makes or causes Y; contingent wording only ties them in time); `temporal-presupposition` (there the time clause holds the suggested thing as a given; here the time clause holds the real event and the main…)
- `stepwise-doubling` (variant, variant of `contingent-suggestion`) **Stepwise doubling** - Attach a stated multiplier or increment of the state, such as twice as deep or ten percent more, to each count, breath or repetition.
  - Cues: 'twice as', 'double', 'doubling', 'ten times', 'a hundred times' applied to a felt state; 'ten percent more', 'one notch more', 'another level' as a fixed increment; the multiplier joined to a recurring event: 'with each number', 'every breath', 'each…; the same multiplier phrase repeated at successive counts or beats; 'twice as deep as before', 'again', 'and double that' - each step measured from the…
  - Not: `anchoring-number` (a reference point is a single stated value that judgment adjusts from; this is a multiplier re-applied at every step…); `the-more-the-more` (that yokes two continuous quantities with comparatives and no numbers; this ties a numbered increase to discrete events); `countdown-deepener` (the countdown is the count with depth attached; label this as well only when a multiplier or increment is stated at…)
- `compound-suggestion` (unit) **Compound suggestion** - Join a statement the listener can verify to a suggestion with 'and', so acceptance of the first carries to the second.
  - Cues: single sentence: verifiable clause + 'and' + suggested clause; first clause describes present posture, hearing, breathing or position; 'and' (or 'and so', 'and now') with no 'as', 'because' or 'when'; the second clause could not be checked the way the first can
  - Not: `contingent-suggestion` (contingent wording states that the response happens as or when the first event does); `pacing-and-leading` (pacing and leading runs several verifiable statements across sentences before the lead; this is one of each inside a…); `suggestion-compounding` (compounding is a claim that suggestions strengthen each other, not a sentence shape)
- `truism` (unit) **Truism** - State something so plainly true about people or experience that the listener can only agree.
  - Cues: generic subjects: 'everyone', 'most people', 'we all', 'anyone'; 'you already know', 'you know what it's like to'; 'sooner or later', 'at some point'; statements of ordinary fact about the body, sleep, learning or time; no instruction and no claim about what the listener is doing right now
  - Not: `pacing` (pacing states what this listener can verify right now; a truism is a general truth that needs no checking); `normalizing` (normalizing says a response is ordinary and expected, to remove worry about it; a truism is true whether or not the…); `universal-quantifier` (that labels the 'every/always' word and need not be true)
- `embedded-command` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Embedded command** - Place a command inside a longer sentence that is on its face about something else.
  - Cues: a bare-verb phrase inside a subordinate clause that reads as an imperative on its own; carrier frames: 'I wonder if you'll', 'you don't have to', 'people can', 'I'm not going…; the embedded phrase is italicized, capitalized or set off by commas or dashes; 'now' placed oddly at the end of the embedded phrase; a name or 'you know' inserted just before the verb phrase
  - Not: `analog-marking` (analog marking is the vocal or typographic setting-apart; the embedded command is the wording being set apart); `interspersal` (interspersal scatters single words or phrases across a narrative; an embedded command is a whole imperative inside one…); `embedded-question` (an embedded question hides a question in a statement; when an 'I wonder' or 'I don't know' carrier also holds an…)
- `interspersal` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Interspersal** - Scatter the words and phrases of the suggestion through a narrative that is about something else.
  - Cues: narrative about an unrelated subject (plant, river, house, journey) with no 'you'…; recurring state vocabulary (comfort, rest, ease, calm, deep, let go) at a rate the topic…; non-human subjects given verbs of resting, relaxing or feeling; target words italicized or marked for emphasis across a passage; the same handful of words recurring every one to two sentences
  - Not: `embedded-command` (an embedded command is one full imperative in one sentence; interspersal is words spread over a passage); `seeding` (seeding mentions an idea early so it is familiar later; interspersal is the delivery of the suggestion itself); `metaphor` (metaphor relies on the story's structure matching the change; interspersal relies on the scattered words and the story…)
- `embedded-question` (unit) **Embedded question** - Fold a question into a statement about the speaker's wondering, so the listener searches for an answer without being asked.
  - Cues: 'I wonder', 'I'm wondering', 'I'm curious', 'I don't know', 'I'd like to know', 'I ask…; followed by 'whether', 'if', 'how', 'when', 'which', 'what'; sentence ends in a full stop, not a question mark; the subordinate question takes the wanted response for granted
  - Not: `suggestion-by-question` (that is a question asked outright, with a question mark and a turn handed to the listener); `conversational-postulate` (a conversational postulate is a direct yes/no question that works as a request to act); `embedded-command` (an embedded command hides an imperative, not a question)
- `conversational-postulate` (unit) **Conversational postulate** - Ask a yes/no question about ability or willingness that the listener answers by doing the thing.
  - Cues: question opening with 'can you', 'could you', 'will you', 'would you', 'are you able…; followed by an action verb the listener could carry out at once; question mark, but the script goes on as though the act was done, not answered; 'for me', 'now' or 'please' appended
  - Not: `tag-question` (a tag question is a short question fixed to the end of a statement, seeking agreement); `suggestion-by-question` (that asks a real question whose consideration produces an experience; this is a request to act dressed as a question); `permissive-suggestion` ('you can' as a statement offers; 'can you' as a question requests)
- `tag-question` (unit) **Tag question** - Add a short question to the end of a statement to invite agreement and soften the claim.
  - Cues: sentence-final ', isn't it?', ', doesn't it?', ', aren't you?', ', can't you?', ', don't…; ', right?', ', yes?', ', is it not?' after a statement; comma + auxiliary + (n't) + pronoun + question mark; positive statement with negative tag, or the reverse
  - Not: `conversational-postulate` (that is a whole question asking for an act; a tag hangs on a statement and asks for agreement); `rhetorical-question` (a rhetorical question is a full question asked for effect, not a tag on a statement); `agreement-harvest` (agreement harvest explicitly asks the listener to say or nod yes)
- `negative-suggestion` (unit) **Negative suggestion** - Name the response inside a negation ('don't relax yet') so the listener has to picture it to understand the sentence.
  - Cues: 'don't', 'do not', 'no need to', 'you needn't' + the target response; limiters: 'yet', 'too quickly', 'too soon', 'until', 'before you're ready'; 'try not to' + response; 'I'm not going to tell you to' + response; the surrounding script goes on to assume the negated response happened
  - Not: `not-doing-not-knowing` (that releases the listener from effort or understanding; this names the target response itself under a negation); `reverse-psychology` (reverse psychology is the strategy of instructing the opposite to exploit opposition; negative suggestion is a…); `double-negative-confusion` (stacked negation aims at confusion by piling negatives up; this uses a single clear negation)

### Presupposition and implication (`presupposition`)

Get a claim accepted by placing it in the background of a sentence, where it is taken for granted, instead of stating it where it can be judged.

- `presupposition-unit` (unit) **Presupposition** - Phrase the sentence so it only makes sense if the target claim is already true.
  - Cues: target response sits in a subordinate clause or noun phrase, not the main verb; the claim survives if the sentence is negated or turned into a question; definite or possessive noun phrase for a state not yet established: 'the heaviness',…; cleft or pseudo-cleft: 'it's the ... that ...', 'what you'll find is ...'; no variant trigger carries the claim; if before/after/when, first/another/again,…
  - Not: `implication` (an implication is inferred and can be denied; a presupposition is required by the grammar and survives negation); `direct-suggestion` (direct suggestion states the response as the main point); `loaded-question` (when the assumption rides on a direct question the listener is expected to answer, label loaded question; this unit…)
- `temporal-presupposition` (variant, variant of `presupposition-unit`) **Time-clause presupposition** - Put the wanted response inside a before, after, while, when or as clause, so the sentence treats it as something that will happen.
  - Cues: 'before you ...'; 'after you ...' / 'once you have ...'; 'while you ...' / 'during ...'; 'when you ...' / 'as you ...' followed by a response not yet established; 'until you ...' / 'since you began ...'
  - Not: `contingent-suggestion` (there the clause holds a fact already true and the main clause is the suggestion; here the suggestion is in the clause); `time-bind` (a time bind offers a choice of when; a time clause offers no choice); `implied-directive` (an implied directive adds an internal response and a signal that it has happened)
- `ordinal-presupposition` (variant, variant of `presupposition-unit`) **Ordinal presupposition** - Use first, next, another or again so the sentence assumes a series of responses, of which this is only one.
  - Cues: 'the first thing you'll notice'; 'which ... first' in a question or 'I wonder' clause; 'next', 'the next time', 'second', 'last'; 'another', 'again', 'once more', 'one more'; 'also', 'too', 'as well' adding a response to one already named
  - Not: `adverb-presupposition` ('still', 'already' and 'even more' comment on one response in progress; 'first', 'another' and 'again' count it as one…); `double-bind` ('which first, left or right' adds listed alternatives; label both when alternatives are named); `suggested-memory` ('again' alone stays here; label suggested memory when the script goes on to describe an earlier occasion that did not…)
- `awareness-presupposition` (variant, variant of `presupposition-unit`) **Awareness predicate** - Ask the listener to notice, realize or become aware of the response, so its existence is assumed and only their awareness is in question.
  - Cues: 'notice how/that ...'; 'realize', 'discover', 'find that', 'recognize'; 'be aware of', 'become aware that', 'are you aware'; 'you may not have noticed yet ...'; 'know that', 'remember that', 'it's nice to know that' followed by the target claim
  - Not: `ratifying-trance-signs` (that names an observable sign and calls it evidence; this assumes the response and asks only for attention); `mind-reading` (mind reading claims knowledge of the listener's thoughts; this directs their attention to an assumed fact); `embedded-question` ('I wonder whether you notice' is an embedded question; label both when the object is an assumed response)
- `adverb-presupposition` (variant, variant of `presupposition-unit`) **Adverb / adjective presupposition** - Attach an adverb or adjective of manner, degree or attitude to the response, so the response is assumed and only its quality is open.
  - Cues: 'how easily / quickly / deeply / completely you ...'; 'already', 'still', 'yet', 'anymore' with a response verb; 'even more', 'even deeper', 'so much more'; sentence-opening evaluative word: 'fortunately', 'luckily', 'happily', 'curiously',…; 'it's interesting / nice / surprising how ...'
  - Not: `comparative-deletion` ('more relaxed' with no standard is comparative deletion; it need not assume a response is under way); `lost-performative` ('it's good to let go' is a value judgment with no judge; it does not assume the letting go is happening); `presupposed-completion` ('already' alone overlaps; label already-happened framing when the whole clause reports the effect as done)
- `change-of-state-presupposition` (variant, variant of `presupposition-unit`) **Change-of-state verb** - Use begin, continue, keep, stop or return with the response, so the sentence assumes the state that came before the verb.
  - Cues: 'begin to', 'start to', 'starting to'; 'continue to', 'keep', 'keep on', 'go on', 'carry on'; 'stop', 'quit', 'no longer', 'not anymore'; 'return to', 'go back to', 'resume'; 'become', 'change into', 'turn into' with a state
  - Not: `presupposed-completion` (that reports the effect itself as present or finished; a phase verb assumes only the state before it); `direct-suggestion` ('relax' asserts; 'continue to relax' assumes relaxing and instructs only its continuation); `present-tense` (progressive tense is a tense choice; this needs a phase verb)
- `presupposed-completion` (unit) **Already-happened framing** - Speak of the effect as already under way or finished, so the listener's only task is to recognize it.
  - Cues: 'already' with perfect or past tense: 'has already begun', 'you've already'; 'it's done', 'that's settled', 'the decision is made', 'that change is made'; 'all that's left is to ...', 'the only thing left is ...'; past or perfect tense for a response the script never requested; 'somewhere back there', 'at some point', 'in the last few breaths' locating the effect…
  - Not: `direct-suggestion` (direct suggestion states the response in the present or future ('your eyes are closing', 'you will relax'); this…); `change-of-state-presupposition` ('continue' assumes a prior state; this states the effect itself as accomplished); `inevitability-framing` (inevitability says it will happen; this says it has)
- `loaded-question` (unit) **Loaded question** - Ask a question that cannot be answered, yes or no, without accepting an assumption built into it.
  - Cues: direct wh-question ('how long', 'when did', 'what is it about', 'why do you') with a…; what it assumes is a wish, need, motive, history or trait of the listener, not a…; 'how much do you want ...', 'why do you keep coming back to ...'; 'do you still ...', 'have you stopped ...'; no direct answer denies the assumption; denying it takes 'I don't' or 'it isn't'
  - Not: `suggestion-by-question` (label that when the question sends attention to a present sensation or response ('which hand feels warmer?'); label…); `presupposition-unit` (the same assumption in a statement is presupposition; loaded question needs a direct question the listener is expected…); `embedded-question` ('I wonder how long you've wanted this' owes no answer; label embedded question, with the presupposition variant for…)
- `implication` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Implication** - Say one thing so that the listener infers another, and holds the inferred thing as their own conclusion.
  - Cues: a limit on a permission or description: 'for now', 'so far', 'at first', 'for the first…; a ceiling that only makes sense if the response is coming: 'only as deep as', 'no faster…; one statement about other people or about one option, with the application to the…; target response never stated as instruction or fact, and not named under 'don't' or…; the inferred claim can be named in a sentence and could be denied without contradiction…
  - Not: `presupposition-unit` (a presupposition is required by the grammar and survives negation; an implication is inferred and can be cancelled); `negative-suggestion` (when the target response is named under 'don't', 'not' or 'won't', label negative suggestion, even with 'yet' or…); `enthymeme` (one statement with its application left open is implication; an unstated premise needs two stated parts (a rule and a…)
- `if-then-implication` (variant, variant of `implication`) **If-then implication** - State a conditional whose 'if' is an easy act the listener controls, so doing the act counts as accepting the 'then'.
  - Cues: 'if you [simple voluntary act], (then) you will / can [response]'; the 'if' clause names something the listener does on purpose, not something happening to…; the act has not been done yet; the sentence comes before it; the act is one the listener is about to do anyway: sit back, close the eyes, take a breath; ratification ('that's right', 'good') in the lines right after
  - Not: `contingent-suggestion` (its first half is already true or inevitable ('as you breathe out'); here the first half is a choice whose taking…); `implied-directive` (an implied directive has the internal response first and a behavioral signal after; here the act comes first and the…); `complex-equivalence` ('X means Y' asserts the meaning outright; label both when a conditional also says what the act means)
- `enthymeme` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Unstated premise** - Give an argument with one step left out, so the listener supplies the missing premise or conclusion themselves.
  - Cues: general statement followed by a particular case, conclusion left unsaid; 'which tells you something', 'and you know what that means', 'you can work out the rest'; two stated parts of an argument (rule and case, or fact and pointer) with the last step…; a pause or line break where the conclusion would go; if the reason and the claim are both stated and joined by 'so' or 'because', label…
  - Not: `reason-giving` (when the reason and the claim are both on the page, label giving a reason; label this only when a step, usually the…); `implication` (implication is one statement with an inference left open; this needs two stated parts of an argument with a step missing); `self-perception-proof` (when the stated fact is the listener's own act and the point is what they want or are, label your-actions-prove-it;…)
- `assumed-common-ground` (unit) **Assumed common ground** - Mark a claim as something speaker and listener already agree on, so it passes as a reminder instead of a new assertion.
  - Cues: 'as you know', 'as you already know', 'as we both know', 'we both know'; 'of course', 'obviously', 'naturally', 'clearly', 'needless to say'; 'you know perfectly well', 'you and I know', 'you know as well as I do'; 'remember, ...' introducing something not said earlier in the script; 'after all', 'as always', 'as we agreed' with no earlier agreement in the text
  - Not: `truism` (a truism really is undeniable; this dresses a contestable claim as already agreed); `awareness-presupposition` ('you know / realize that X' with the claim as the verb's object is the awareness predicate; label this entry for an…); `mind-reading` (mind reading claims knowledge of what the listener thinks or feels now; this claims they already hold a belief in…)

### Artful vagueness (`vague-language`)

Leave out or blur the specifics of a sentence so the listener supplies them from their own experience.

- `nominalization` (unit) **Nominalization** - Name a process or feeling with an abstract noun so the listener fills it with their own meaning.
  - Cues: abstract nouns ending in -tion, -ment, -ness, -ance, -ity (relaxation, awareness,…; noun forms of state or process words: comfort, peace, focus, trust, change, learning,…; 'a certain ...', 'a sense of ...', 'a feeling of ...', 'that ...' before an abstract noun; abstract noun as object of have, find, discover, allow, enjoy; the noun could be replaced by a verb phrase ('your relaxation' = 'you relaxing')
  - Not: `unspecified-verb` (there the process stays a verb with its manner left out; here it has become a noun); `embodied-metaphor` (that gives the state a physical substance (fog, warmth); a nominalization stays abstract); `state-labeling` (that tells the listener what their present state is called; a nominalization only refers to a state loosely)
- `unspecified-verb` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Unspecified verb** - Use a verb that says something happens without saying how, so the listener does it their own way.
  - Cues: process verbs with no manner or means: change, learn, experience, discover, understand,…; 'in your own way', 'however it happens', 'somehow' following the verb; 'begin to ...', 'allow yourself to ...', 'let yourself ...' plus a vague verb; no 'by ...' or 'with ...' phrase saying how the action is done
  - Not: `nominalization` (that turns the process into a noun; this keeps the verb and omits the manner); `not-doing-not-knowing` (that states outright that the listener need not know how; this just leaves the how unsaid); `simple-deletion` (deletion removes an object or topic from the sentence; here the verb itself is under-specified)
- `unspecified-referent` (unit) **Unspecified referent** - Talk about 'people', 'a person' or 'that feeling' without saying who or which, so the listener applies it to themselves.
  - Cues: generic subject plus 'can' or 'may' plus a response verb: 'a person can', 'people can',…; 'that feeling', 'this sensation', 'certain things', 'something' with no antecedent; 'it' or 'this' with nothing earlier in the text to point to; a generic statement sitting between sentences addressed to 'you'; the sentence states a possibility, not a count or report of what others actually do
  - Not: `social-proof` (social proof reports what others actually do or feel ('most people find') as a reason to follow; here the generic…); `truism` (a truism is an undeniable general fact that asks for nothing ('everyone has felt tired'); this offers the wanted…); `normalizing` (normalizing states that the response is ordinary and expected; this only leaves the subject open)
- `comparative-deletion` (unit) **Comparative deletion** - Make a comparison and leave out what it is compared with: more relaxed, deeper, better.
  - Cues: comparatives with no 'than' clause: deeper, more relaxed, easier, heavier, calmer,…; 'more and more', 'even more', 'more deeply', 'less and less'; superlatives with no stated set: 'the best way', 'the deepest level'; 'more' or '-er' adjective directly after become, feel, grow, get, go
  - Not: `the-more-the-more` (that yokes two increases to each other; this is a single comparison with the standard missing); `deepening-by-repetition` (that is depth words restated across a passage; this is one comparative with no standard, on any quality. A run of…); `adverb-presupposition` ('even more' and 'still deeper' also presuppose a prior amount; label that when the point is the assumed earlier state,…)
- `lost-performative` (unit) **Lost performative** - State a value judgment as plain fact, without saying whose judgment it is.
  - Cues: 'it's good to', 'it's right to', 'it's important to', 'it's best to', 'it's wise to',…; evaluative adjective of worth or correctness (good, right, wrong, best, important, wise,…; 'the best way to ...', 'the right thing is ...' with no one named as holding the view; no 'I think', 'for me', 'according to' attached to the judgment; the judged thing is a response or attitude the script wants, not a checkable fact
  - Not: `permission-giving` ('it's okay / all right / fine to' says the response is allowed and is permission-giving; label lost performative when…); `ratification` (a free-standing 'that's right' or 'good' after a response confirms that it happened; a lost performative is a full…); `truism` (a truism can be checked and is undeniable; this is an opinion presented as fact)
- `mind-reading` (unit) **Mind reading** - State what the listener is thinking or feeling as though you know it.
  - Cues: 'I know you ...', 'I can tell you ...', 'I know that you ...' plus a mental verb; 'you're wondering', 'you're thinking', 'you're curious', 'you're starting to feel'; 'part of you ...' plus a thought or wish; second-person mental-state claim with no evidence given; 'you might be asking yourself'
  - Not: `pacing` (pacing states what the listener can verify externally right now; mind reading asserts unobservable thoughts or feelings); `desire-attribution` (that attributes wanting the suggested outcome; mind reading covers any claimed thought or feeling); `objection-preemption` (that names a likely objection in order to answer it; mind reading need not answer anything)
- `cause-effect-claim` (unit) **Cause-effect link** - Say that one thing causes another, usually something already happening causing the response you want.
  - Cues: 'makes you', 'causes you to', 'forces', 'brings', 'sends you', 'takes you' linking a…; 'because', 'so', 'and so', 'that is why' joining a present fact to an involuntary…; 'X will make Y happen' where X is the voice, breath, a sound or a count; a verifiable clause followed by a causal verb and an unverifiable response
  - Not: `contingent-suggestion` (that links by timing ('as', 'while', 'when', 'with every'); this asserts causation outright); `complex-equivalence` (that says X means or shows Y; this says X produces Y); `reason-giving` (there the 'because' clause gives grounds for doing or accepting what is asked; here a present event is said to produce…)
- `complex-equivalence` (unit) **Complex equivalence** - Say that one thing means another: an observable sign is declared to be proof of the state you want.
  - Cues: 'means', 'means that', 'which means'; 'shows that', 'proves', 'tells you', 'is a sign that', 'is how you know'; 'the fact that ... means ...'; 'X is Y' or 'X-ing is Y-ing' where X is an observable event or condition and Y is a…; two unlike things joined by 'is just another way of', 'is the same as', 'is what ...…
  - Not: `cause-effect-claim` (that says X brings Y about; this says X is, or is evidence of, Y); `ratifying-trance-signs` (when X is an ordinary bodily sign of relaxation and Y is trance, label that narrower entry; this is the general…); `sensation-relabeling` (that is the narrower case of giving a felt body sensation a new cause or meaning; label it when X is a sensation being…)
- `universal-quantifier` (unit) **Universal quantifier** - Use all, every, always, never or nothing so the statement covers every case without exception.
  - Cues: all, every, each, any, always, never, none, nothing, no one, everyone, everything,…; 'every time', 'each and every', 'with every ...', 'all of your ...', 'every part of you'; 'whenever', 'wherever', 'no matter what / where / when'; quantifier attached to a recurring event (breath, word, sound, count)
  - Not: `contingent-suggestion` ('with every breath, ...' and 'each time ..., ...' are contingent suggestions (breath-deepener when tied to depth);…); `inevitability-framing` (that asserts the outcome cannot be avoided; a quantifier only removes exceptions from a statement); `hyperbole` (hyperbole is overstatement not meant literally; the quantifier is meant to hold in every case)
- `modal-operator` (unit) **Modal operator** - Choose can, may, will, must or can't to set whether the response is possible, permitted, certain or required.
  - Cues: possibility: can, could, may, might, able to, it's possible to; necessity: must, have to, need to, should, got to, it's necessary; impossibility: can't, cannot, unable to, impossible to, won't be able to; 'you' plus a modal plus a response verb; modals strengthening across a passage (can, then will, then must)
  - Not: `permissive-suggestion` (a suggestion offered with 'can', 'may' or 'might' is permissive-suggestion and needs no modal label; label the modal…); `conversational-postulate` (that is a modal question ('can you...?') working as a request; this is a modal in a statement); `challenge-suggestion` (that invites a test of a suggested inability; 'can't' alone, with no invited attempt, is just the modal)
- `simple-deletion` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Deletion** - Leave out the object or topic of the sentence: ready for what, aware of what, curious about what.
  - Cues: ready, aware, curious, sure, willing, open, certain, prepared with no 'for', 'of',…; know, understand, notice, remember, learn with no object; short 'you are / you feel' plus adjective sentence ending at the adjective; the question 'ready for what?' or 'know what?' has no answer in the surrounding sentences
  - Not: `comparative-deletion` (that omits the standard of a comparison; this omits an object or topic); `incomplete-sentence` (that breaks off mid-sentence so the gap is felt; a deletion sounds complete); `unspecified-referent` (there a noun is present but picks out no one in particular; here the element is absent altogether)
- `phonological-ambiguity` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Sound-alike ambiguity** - Use a word or phrase that sounds like a different word, so a second instruction is heard inside the sentence.
  - Cues: homophone pairs near a suggestion: here/hear, know/no, your/you're, to/two/too, by/buy,…; a deliberate 'misspelling' in a script that swaps one homophone for another; stage direction or emphasis mark on a homophone; the same sound repeated in both senses in one sentence ('you know... no need to')
  - Not: `double-meaning` (that is one word with two senses; this is two different words that sound alike); `wordplay-confusion` (that loops sound-alikes to overload sense-making; this is a single quiet second reading); `embedded-command` (an embedded command is plainly present in the wording; here the command exists only in the alternate hearing)
- `syntactic-ambiguity` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Syntactic ambiguity** - Write a phrase whose grammar can be parsed two ways, so who does what to whom stays open.
  - Cues: transitive verb in -ing form directly before a plural noun ('relaxing muscles', 'calming…; '-ing noun can be ...' as sentence subject; a phrase that could attach to either the subject or the object ('watching you relax with…; sentence gives two sensible answers to 'who is doing the -ing?'
  - Not: `scope-ambiguity` (there the parse is clear and only the reach of a modifier is open; here the grammatical roles themselves are open); `punctuation-ambiguity` (that fuses two sentences on a shared word; this is one well-formed phrase with two parses); `double-meaning` (that depends on one word's two senses, not on grammar)
- `scope-ambiguity` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Scope ambiguity** - Place a modifier where it is unclear how much of the sentence it covers.
  - Cues: adjective or adverb before two or more nouns or verbs joined by 'and'; 'I wonder if / I don't know whether / you may notice that' followed by clauses joined…; a long sentence where a late clause could be inside or outside an earlier hedge; 'as' or 'when' clause followed by coordinated items
  - Not: `syntactic-ambiguity` (there the phrase has two different parses of who does what; here only the reach of a modifier is unclear); `compound-suggestion` (that joins a fact and a suggestion with 'and' to transfer acceptance; label scope ambiguity only when an earlier…); `embedded-question` ('I wonder if' alone is an embedded question; this entry needs the trailing 'and' clause whose coverage is open)
- `punctuation-ambiguity` (unit) **Run-on ambiguity** - Let the last word of one sentence also be the first word of the next, so the two run together.
  - Cues: a sentence that is ungrammatical as a whole but splits into two good sentences sharing…; 'now', 'here', 'deeply', 'relax', 'down' sitting at a join with no punctuation; missing full stop or comma where a sentence boundary is expected; a noun that immediately serves as a verb (watch, hand, rest, feel, sink); script note such as 'no pause' at a sentence boundary
  - Not: `incomplete-sentence` (that breaks off and leaves the thought hanging; this continues straight into a second sentence); `polysyndeton` (that joins whole clauses with repeated conjunctions; here a shared word belongs to both sentences); `non-sequitur` (that drops in an irrelevant statement; here the join, not the content, is what is odd)
- `selectional-restriction-violation` (unit) **Attributing feeling to things** - Say that an object feels or does something only a person can, so the listener takes the statement as about themselves.
  - Cues: inanimate subject (chair, room, bed, tree, stone, candle) with a verb of feeling,…; 'the ... knows how to', 'the ... can relax', 'the ... feels', 'the ... is content to'; an object described with the target state shortly before 'you' is given the same state; one or two sentences only, with no developed story
  - Not: `personification` (personification gives the listener's own body parts or states a will ('your eyelids know'), or addresses the thing;…); `metaphor` (a metaphor is a story or image with parallel structure; this is one predicate on the wrong kind of subject); `unspecified-referent` (that uses a generic human subject; this uses a non-human one)
- `double-meaning` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Double meaning** - Use a word with two senses where both senses carry the suggestion.
  - Cues: words with a literal and a trance sense: down, deep, under, drop, sink, fall, light,…; the same word used twice in one sentence in two senses; an idiom whose literal reading also fits the scene ('sink in', 'let go', 'drift off',…; a direction or position word in a scene that matches a depth word in the trance
  - Not: `phonological-ambiguity` (that uses two different words that sound the same; this uses one word with two senses); `metaphor` (metaphor develops a parallel image or story; a double meaning rests on a single word); `interspersal` (interspersal scatters suggestion words through unrelated narrative; label double meaning when one word is doing two…)

## Pacing and utilization (`pacing-utilization`)

Start from what is already true for the listener, and give whatever happens a job in the process.

- `pacing` (unit) **Pacing** - State things the listener can check right now and find true.
  - Cues: second-person present-tense statements about the immediate situation: 'you are sitting',…; sensory verbs with neutral objects: hear the sounds, feel the chair, see the screen; references to the act of listening or reading itself: 'my voice', 'these words'; no evaluative or state words in the statement (no 'relaxed', 'deeper', 'calm'); typically at the opening of a script or section
  - Not: `truism` (a truism is a general undeniable fact ('everyone has drifted off in a lecture'); pacing describes this listener's…); `mind-reading` (mind reading claims what the listener is thinking, wondering or wanting, which they cannot check against the senses;…); `pacing-and-leading` (pacing and leading adds a suggested statement after the verifiable ones; pacing alone suggests nothing)
- `pacing-and-leading` (unit) **Pacing and leading** - Give several statements the listener can verify, then one suggested statement that borrows their credibility.
  - Cues: two or more verifiable present-tense statements followed by a state or change claim in…; linkers between fact and suggestion: 'and', 'as', 'while', 'and that means'; final clause introduces a state word absent from the earlier clauses: relax, deeper,…; softeners on the last clause: 'you can begin to', 'you may notice'; ratio shifts over the passage: fewer verifiable statements per suggestion later on
  - Not: `yes-set` (a yes set seeks explicit or inner yes answers, often to questions and on any topic; pace and lead uses statements…); `compound-suggestion` (compound suggestion is the single 'fact and suggestion' sentence form; pace and lead is the wider pattern of several…); `contingent-suggestion` (contingent suggestion makes the response depend on a specific event ('as X, then Y'); here the paces only lend…)
- `utilization` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Utilization** - Accept whatever the listener brings or does, and give it a role in the process.
  - Cues: conditional naming of a listener behavior followed by acceptance: 'if you ... , that's…; a behavior that would normally interfere (fidgeting, thinking, doubting, opening the…; link phrases: 'and that can help you', 'use that', 'that is exactly what'; no instruction to stop or correct the behavior; 'whatever you ... is right' followed by a specific use, not only reassurance
  - Not: `resistance-utilization` (that entry covers the special case where the thing used is opposition or a contrary response); `reframing` (reframing only changes what something means; utilization assigns it a function in the procedure); `failure-reframing` (no-fail framing declares every outcome a success; utilization takes one specific behavior and puts it to use)
- `incorporation` (variant, variant of `utilization`) **Incorporation of distractions** - Name likely interruptions in advance and make them part of the trance.
  - Cues: 'any sounds', 'noises around you', 'outside sounds', 'whatever you hear'; a list of everyday interruptions: traffic, doors, phones, voices, a clock; outcome clauses: 'fade into the background', 'won't disturb you', 'only help you', 'part…; exception clause for emergencies nearby ('unless it needs your attention'); placed in the first minutes of an induction
  - Not: `deepening-by-linkage` (that deepener declares every stimulus sends the listener deeper; incorporation only neutralizes or absorbs interruptions); `narrowing-attention` (narrowing attention makes everything but one thing fade; incorporation lets the distraction stay and gives it a place); `safety-override` (the emergency clause that often follows is a separate safety device)
- `symptom-utilization` (variant, variant of `utilization`) [text-detectable: partial] **Using the listener's own material** - Build the suggestion from the listener's own words, interests, habits or complaint.
  - Cues: attribution to the listener: 'you told me', 'you said', 'as you described', 'your word…; a prompt for the listener to supply content, then reuse of it: 'think of a place you…; the listener's occupation, hobby or habit used as the model for the response ('the way…; quoted or unusual vocabulary repeated verbatim from an earlier listener statement; the presenting problem recast as a skill ('the same persistence that keeps you worrying')
  - Not: `matching` (matching mirrors tempo, breathing or sensory vocabulary for rapport; this entry builds the suggestion out of the…); `analogy` (an analogy can use any familiar thing; here the comparison is taken from what this listener supplied); `values-linking` (values linking ties a suggestion to what the listener cares about as a reason; this uses their material as the form of…)
- `resistance-utilization` (variant, variant of `utilization`) [text-detectable: partial] **Using resistance** - Give the listener's resistance a task, so that resisting carries out the suggestion.
  - Cues: acknowledgment of resistance followed by a task for it ('that part can...', 'let it...'); 'keep (doing X) as long as you can'; 'part of you wants to... and that part can...'; instructions to try harder at the resisted behavior
  - Not: `reverse-psychology` (reverse psychology tells the listener to do the opposite; using resistance assigns the resistance a task); `resistance-reframing` (resistance as participation re-describes resistance; using resistance gives it work); `reverse-set-bind` (a reverse-set bind offers choices built for a contrary listener; using resistance needs no choice)
- `normalizing` (unit) **Normalizing** - Describe the response as ordinary, natural and expected.
  - Cues: verdict words: 'normal', 'natural', 'perfectly normal', 'ordinary', 'common', 'to be…; generic statements about how minds and bodies behave: 'minds wander', 'bodies do that',…; reassurance clause: 'it doesn't mean anything is wrong', 'it doesn't mean this isn't…; 'it's natural to' or 'it's only natural that' followed by a response; range statements ('some people ..., others ...') closed by a verdict that all of them…
  - Not: `social-proof` (social proof reports what other people do or feel so the listener will expect or do the same ('most people find their…); `truism` (a truism is a bare undeniable fact used to build acceptance ('minds wander'); normalizing attaches the reassurance…); `myth-correction` (myth correction names and rebuts a false belief about hypnosis; normalizing reassures about an experience)
- `trance-as-everyday` (variant, variant of `normalizing`) **Everyday trance** - Liken trance to absorbed states the listener already knows, such as driving on autopilot or being lost in a book.
  - Cues: stock examples: driving and missing the exit, lost in a book or film, daydreaming,…; 'you've done this before', 'you already know how', 'many times a day'; 'it's just like', 'the same as', 'a kind of trance'; the phrases 'everyday trance', 'natural state', 'highway hypnosis'; past-experience framing with 'you have' or 'remember a time when' followed by an…
  - Not: `revivification-induction` (that induction has the listener relive a past absorbed state in detail until it returns; this only cites such states…); `myth-correction` (myth correction rebuts a false belief ('hypnosis is not sleep'); this offers a positive comparison); `analogy` (analogy is the general device of explaining by likeness; this is its specific use to make trance familiar)
- `permission-giving` (unit) **Permission** - Explicitly permit a response the listener may be holding back.
  - Cues: 'it's okay to', 'it's all right to', 'it's fine to'; 'you're allowed to', 'you have permission to', 'I give you permission', 'give yourself…; the permitted thing is a release or a non-effort: let go, stop trying, rest, feel, not…; the licence wording is what counts: a 'you don't have to' clause with no 'okay' or…; often follows a normalizing statement
  - Not: `permissive-suggestion` (permissive suggestion is the 'you may / you can' phrasing used to offer any response; permission lifts a restraint on…); `not-doing-not-knowing` (that form says no effort or knowledge is needed so the response feels automatic; permission says the response is allowed); `permission-seeking` (there the listener asks the speaker for permission; here the speaker grants it unasked)

## Binds and choices (`binds`)

Offer the listener a choice, or restrict the choices on the table, so that the options offered serve the wanted response.

- `double-bind` (unit) **Double bind** - Offer two or more alternatives that all lead to the wanted response, so choosing any of them is accepting it.
  - Cues: 'X or Y' where both X and Y entail the same response; 'either ... or ...', 'whether ... or ...'; 'I don't know if ... or ...', 'I wonder whether ... or ...'; 'which', 'which one', 'find out which' applied to a detail of the response; disclaimer of preference after the offer: 'it doesn't matter which', 'whichever you…
  - Not: `false-dilemma` (a false dilemma offers two different outcomes and hides the others; a double bind offers one outcome under two…); `covering-all-possibilities` (that lists every possible response including none; a bind offers a short set that all include the target); `forced-choice` (forced choice demands an explicit pick between options that differ in substance; in a double bind the options share…)
- `illusion-of-choice` (variant, variant of `double-bind`) **Illusion of choice** - Give a free, conscious choice between two concrete options that differ only in a detail that does not matter.
  - Cues: 'would you like to ... or ...?', 'do you want to ... or ...?'; two concrete, physical or procedural options joined by 'or' (which hand, which chair,…; 'you can X or Y' where X and Y are both ways of doing the same thing; 'whichever is more comfortable', 'whichever you prefer', 'your choice'; question about manner or position placed before the main instruction
  - Not: `forced-choice` (forced choice demands a pick from options that differ in substance and uses the pick as commitment; here the options…); `time-bind` (label time bind when the only thing that varies is when); `double-bind` (that is an 'or' inside a sentence that moves on; here the listener is asked to actually choose)
- `conscious-unconscious-bind` (variant, variant of `double-bind`) **Conscious-unconscious double bind** - Split the listener into conscious and unconscious mind and give each a job, so the response happens whatever the conscious mind does.
  - Cues: two or more options for the conscious mind joined by 'or' ('can listen, or wander')…; 'whether or not you consciously ...', 'whether your conscious mind ... or ...'; 'you may or may not consciously notice/remember' + what the unconscious does regardless; paired conditionals with a response in both branches: 'if your unconscious ..., the…; 'it doesn't matter what your conscious mind does'
  - Not: `conscious-unconscious-split` (the split addresses the unconscious as a separate listener and offers no alternatives; label the bind only when the…); `not-doing-not-knowing` (that only releases the listener from effort; the bind also assigns the response to the unconscious); `implied-directive` (an implied directive adds a time clause and a signal that the inner response has happened; it can contain this bind)
- `time-bind` (variant, variant of `double-bind`) **Time bind** - Offer a choice only about when the response happens, never whether.
  - Cues: 'now or in a moment', 'now or later', 'right away or in a little while'; 'on this breath or the next'; 'before ... or after ...' with the same response in both; 'I don't know whether ... now or ...', 'I wonder if it will be ... or ...' naming two or…; 'today or tomorrow', 'tonight or in the morning' attached to a stated response
  - Not: `temporal-presupposition` (a time clause ('before you go deeper') presupposes the response with no choice offered; a time bind offers alternative…); `self-paced-emergence` (that hands over the timing of waking as a courtesy, not as a way to secure a response); `illusion-of-choice` (label illusion of choice when the options differ in manner or place, not time)
- `non-sequitur-bind` (variant, variant of `double-bind`) [text-detectable: partial] **Non-sequitur double bind** - Offer alternatives that have no logical connection to each other or to the outcome, in the grammar of a real choice.
  - Cues: 'or' joining two clauses that restate each other with the order of terms swapped; 'either ... or ...' or 'would you rather ... or ...' where the second option shares…; same response word in both halves of an offered choice; question form that cannot be answered by picking a side; plain direct instruction immediately after an illogical choice
  - Not: `non-sequitur` (a non sequitur is any irrelevant statement used to confuse; label the bind only when it is framed as alternatives that…); `confusion-technique` (confusion technique is sustained disorientation over a passage; this is a single choice-shaped sentence); `chiasmus` (chiasmus is the ABBA word order as style; here the reversal is offered as a choice between two things)
- `reverse-set-bind` (variant, variant of `double-bind`) [text-detectable: partial] **Reverse-set double bind** - Phrase the bind so that a listener who does the opposite of what is said ends up giving the wanted response.
  - Cues: 'you won't ... until ...', 'not until you ...' with the wanted response in the main clause; a negated or doubted wanted response followed by a clause accepting either outcome: 'and…; a run of three or more flat negative statements the listener is likely to contradict,…; 'I doubt you can ...', 'you probably can't ... yet' about the wanted response, with…; elsewhere the script names the listener's habit of opposing ('you like to do the…
  - Not: `reverse-psychology` (reverse psychology is a single opposite instruction that pays off only if defied ('don't go under yet'); label the…); `negative-suggestion` (that is the sentence form of naming the response under a negation and relies on the listener having to represent it;…); `challenge-suggestion` (a challenge invites an attempt that is suggested to fail as proof; this invites opposition that produces the response)
- `false-dilemma` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **False dilemma** - Present two options as the only ones, when others exist, so that rejecting one means taking the other.
  - Cues: 'either ... or ...' with two different outcomes, one clearly worse; 'there are only two ...', 'two kinds of ...', 'you have two choices'; 'you can't do both', 'there is no middle', 'no third option'; 'if you're not X, you're Y'; 'the only alternative is ...', 'it's this or ...'
  - Not: `double-bind` (in a double bind both options give the same outcome; here they differ and the other possibilities are hidden); `antithesis` (antithesis sets two opposed ideas side by side for style without claiming they are the only options); `forced-choice` (forced choice asks the listener to pick; a false dilemma is a claim about what options exist and may ask for nothing)
- `forced-choice` (unit) **Forced choice** - Make the listener pick one of a closed list of substantively different options the writer supplies, aloud or inwardly.
  - Cues: imperatives of choosing: 'choose', 'pick', 'decide', 'select'; 'which do you choose?', 'which is it?', 'which one?'; an enumerated or lettered list of options followed by a pause marker; 'only one', 'you have to pick', 'you can't have both'; instruction on how to answer: 'say it aloud', 'nod', 'answer in your mind'
  - Not: `illusion-of-choice` (there the options are equivalent and the choice is a courtesy; here the options differ and the pick is used as…); `choice-as-ownership` (that is the later reference back to a choice as proof of wanting ('you chose this'); forced choice covers only the…); `agreement-harvest` (that asks for a yes to one proposal; forced choice asks for a selection among several)

## Confusion and overload (`confusion-overload`)

Devices that disrupt, saturate or starve orderly thinking so the next clear instruction is taken as relief.

- `confusion-technique` (unit) **Confusion technique** - Say something grammatical but hard to resolve, then give one simple suggestion the listener can take as a way out.
  - Cues: one word repeated in shifting senses within a sentence (know, think, part, right); long sentence with nested clauses that does not resolve to a clear claim; short plain imperative directly after a dense passage; 'it doesn't matter', 'you don't need to work it out', 'just let ...' closing the passage; mixed tenses, directions or negations in one sentence
  - Not: `cognitive-overload` (overload is too much to track; confusion is content that will not resolve); `confusion-induction` (that is a whole induction built on this device; this is the device wherever it appears); `disrupt-then-reframe` (that is one odd phrase plus a reframe in a request; this is a sustained passage plus a suggestion)
- `time-confusion` (variant, variant of `confusion-technique`) **Time confusion** - Tangle past, present and future references until the listener stops tracking when 'now' is.
  - Cues: 'yesterday', 'today', 'tomorrow' (or last/this/next) recombined in one sentence; three or more tense shifts in one sentence; future perfect and past perfect close together ('will have', 'had not yet'); 'before ... after ... before' stacked; ends by resolving to 'now' or 'right now'
  - Not: `time-distortion` (that states that time stretches or shrinks; this scrambles time references without claiming anything); `temporal-presupposition` (one clear time clause that presupposes; nothing is tangled); `age-regression` (regression sends the listener to a specific earlier time; time confusion may precede it but is only the scrambling)
- `spatial-confusion` (variant, variant of `confusion-technique`) **Left-right confusion** - Tangle left and right, here and there, this and that until the listener stops tracking which is which.
  - Cues: 'right' and 'left' several times in one sentence, in more than one sense; 'here' and 'there', or 'this' and 'that', swapped within a sentence; 'that's right' placed inside a left/right passage; 'whichever', 'the one that's left', 'the other one'; followed by a hand, arm or eye suggestion
  - Not: `wordplay-confusion` (wordplay loops on any sound-alike; this is specifically direction and place words); `apposition-of-opposites` (that pairs two opposed experiences clearly (one rises, one sinks); here the labels are scrambled); `double-meaning` (a single pun that serves the suggestion, not a pile-up meant to confuse)
- `double-negative-confusion` (variant, variant of `confusion-technique`) **Stacked negation** - Stack two or more negations in a sentence so the listener has to work out what is actually being said.
  - Cues: 'can't not', 'won't not', 'don't not'; three or more of not / n't / never / no / nothing / un- in one sentence; negated failure verbs: 'never fail to', 'not unable to', 'can't avoid not'; 'no reason you shouldn't', 'nothing you need to not do'; plain positive restatement in the next sentence
  - Not: `negative-suggestion` (one negation that names the response; meaning is clear); `reverse-psychology` (a clear instruction to do the opposite, not a meaning that must be computed); `not-doing-not-knowing` (a clear statement that no effort is needed; negation is not stacked)
- `non-sequitur` (variant, variant of `confusion-technique`) **Non sequitur** - Drop an irrelevant or illogical statement into the flow, delivered as if it belonged there.
  - Cues: sentence with no topical link to its neighbours; 'so', 'because', 'which means' joining unrelated clauses; concrete everyday fact (weather, furniture, weekdays) inside a trance passage; imperative or suggestion directly after the odd sentence; no acknowledgement that anything odd was said
  - Not: `non-sequitur-bind` (a bind offers two unlinked alternatives as a choice; this is a statement, not a choice); `cause-effect-claim` (a causal link the listener can accept as plausible; a non sequitur is meant not to connect); `shock-surprise` (shock relies on suddenness or intensity; a non sequitur is flat and only fails to follow)
- `wordplay-confusion` (variant, variant of `confusion-technique`) **Circular wordplay** - Loop the same word and its sound-alikes through a sentence until the listener loses track of which sense is meant.
  - Cues: one word or homophone pair five or more times in one or two sentences; know/no, here/hear, right/write, mind (noun)/mind (verb), there/their; sentence that ends on the word it began with; same word as noun and verb in one clause; release line after the loop ('let it go', 'never mind')
  - Not: `spatial-confusion` (when the looped words are direction or pointing words (right, left, here, there, this, that) used about the body or…); `semantic-satiation` (the same word in the same sense repeated back to back until it is only sound; here the word changes sense or spelling…); `phonological-ambiguity` (one sound-alike carrying a hidden instruction, not a loop built to confuse)
- `pattern-interrupt` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Pattern interrupt** - Start an automatic sequence the listener knows by heart, break it midway, and give an instruction in the gap.
  - Cues: a count, list or alphabet that stops before its end; a repeated frame or refrain broken on its fourth or later repetition; 'sleep', 'drop', 'stop', 'now' at the break point; a stock saying cut off and replaced by an instruction; stage direction for a sudden stop or change mid-sequence
  - Not: `shock-surprise` (shock needs no prior pattern; an interrupt depends on one being under way); `incomplete-sentence` (that leaves the gap open for the listener; here the gap is filled with an instruction); `rapid-induction` (that is an induction that uses an interrupt plus a sleep command; this is the interrupt wherever it occurs)
- `cognitive-overload` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Cognitive overload** - Give the listener more things to track at once than they can hold, so they stop tracking and just follow.
  - Cues: 'at the same time', 'all at once', 'while also', 'and keep ...'; list of four or more concurrent objects of attention; 'keep track of', 'hold in mind', 'don't lose'; release line: 'let it all go', 'too much to follow', 'stop keeping track'; stage direction for two voices or streams at once
  - Not: `confusion-technique` (confusion is one stream that will not resolve; overload is many clear streams); `overload-induction` (that is a full induction built on overload; this is the device at any point in a script); `sensory-detail-immersion` (dense detail that builds one scene to absorb the listener, not competing items to track)
- `shock-surprise` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Shock / surprise** - Insert one sudden, unexpected word, sound or claim, and place a suggestion right after it.
  - Cues: stage direction: [snap], [clap], [loud], [sudden]; one-word imperative after a long gentle passage ('Stop.', 'Now.', 'Sleep.'); blunt or contrary statement inside a soothing passage; exclamation mark or capitals in an otherwise quiet script; suggestion immediately after the unexpected item
  - Not: `pattern-interrupt` (an interrupt breaks an established sequence (a count, a refrain, a stock phrase) before its next step; if no sequence…); `novelty-orienting` (an orienting cue re-gathers attention and the script then carries on with its content ('Now. - `paradox` (unit) **Paradox** - State something self-contradictory and move on without resolving it.
  - Cues: one sentence asserting X and not-X; self-referring instruction ('ignore this', 'don't do what I say'); 'try not to try', 'effortlessly make an effort', 'remember to forget'; opposed terms joined by 'by', 'is' or 'means' ('awake by sleeping'); no explanation or resolution in the following sentence

- `incomplete-sentence` (unit) **Unfinished sentence** - Break a sentence off before its end so the listener finishes it themselves or is left waiting.
  - Cues: sentence ending in an ellipsis or dash with the grammar incomplete; final word missing from a phrase given in full earlier; line ending on 'and', 'to', 'the', 'so', 'because', 'you can'; next sentence starts a new thought and does not resume; stage direction for a pause or silence after the break
  - Not: `ellipsis-pause` (a pause inside a sentence that then continues to its end); `pattern-interrupt` (the break is filled at once with an instruction); `enthymeme` (an argument with a missing premise; the sentences themselves are complete)
- `boredom-monotony` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Monotony** - Keep the delivery deliberately flat, repetitive and uneventful until the listener stops watching for anything new.
  - Cues: five or more consecutive sentences with the same structure and near-identical words; neutral, low-information content (breath, counting, 'the same'); 'nothing to do', 'nothing happens', 'nothing new'; stage directions: [monotone], [flat], [even], [droning]; long passage with no questions, images or new vocabulary
  - Not: `repetition-induction` (repeats one suggestion or mantra to absorb the listener; here the content is deliberately empty); `deepening-by-repetition` (repeats depth words to push depth, not to bore); `semantic-satiation` (one word repeated back to back until it stops meaning anything; monotony keeps whole sentences meaningful and merely…)
- `distraction-of-conscious-mind` (unit) **Occupying the conscious mind** - Hand the listener's analytical attention a harmless job and say that the rest of them can go on responding.
  - Cues: 'your conscious mind can ...', 'let your thinking mind ...' followed by a named activity; 'keep busy with', 'give it a job', 'occupy itself', 'something to do'; the assigned activity is open-ended attending or wondering (follow the sounds, watch the…; 'while the rest of you ...' directly after the assignment; the assigned focus is not mentioned again in the suggestions that follow
  - Not: `dual-task` (if the job is a procedure that can be done right or wrong (count by threes, spell backward, tap on a word), label the…); `conscious-unconscious-split` (label the split wherever the unconscious is addressed as a second listener; add this label only when the conscious…); `conscious-unconscious-bind` (the bind offers the conscious mind alternatives ('listen or wander') and says the response happens either way; here…)
- `dual-task` (variant, variant of `distraction-of-conscious-mind`) **Concurrent task** - Set a specific ongoing task, such as counting backward, and keep delivering suggestions while the listener does it.
  - Cues: 'count backward from', 'by threes', 'by sevens'; 'keep counting', 'keep going while I talk', 'whatever else you hear'; 'spell ... backward', 'every time you hear ... tap'; a task with a checkable procedure (a count, a spelling, a tally) set before a passage of…; 'if you lose your place, that's fine', 'start again from'
  - Not: `distraction-of-conscious-mind` (that names the purpose and may assign any occupation; this is a specific procedural task running under suggestions); `losing-numbers` (there the counting is the target and is suggested away; here it is a side task that keeps attention busy); `counting-induction` (the writer counts and ties each number to depth; the listener is not given a competing task)
- `semantic-satiation` (unit) **Semantic satiation** - Repeat one word many times in a row, with nothing between, until it stops meaning anything and is heard as pure sound.
  - Cues: one word repeated ten or more times consecutively with no other words between; 'over and over', 'again and again', 'keep repeating the word' followed by a single…; 'until it loses its meaning', 'until it stops making sense', 'no longer sounds like a…; 'just a sound', 'only a noise', 'an empty sound' said of a word; a plain suggestion or a new definition of the same word directly after the run
  - Not: `epizeuxis` (a few repeats for emphasis, with the meaning kept and strengthened); `mantra` (a phrase repeated so its content is absorbed; satiation repeats to empty the content out); `wordplay-confusion` (that loops one word through different senses and sound-alikes; this holds one word in one sense until the sense fades)

## Attention and absorption (`attention-absorption`)

Devices that narrow the listener's attention onto one thing and keep it there.

- `voice-focus` (unit) **Voice focus** - Tell the listener to attend to the speaker's voice and let it be the one thing they follow.
  - Cues: 'my voice' as the object of listen, focus, follow, attend; 'the sound of my voice'; 'only my voice', 'just my voice', 'nothing but my voice'; 'my words' used the same way in text scripts; 'follow my voice', 'stay with my voice'
  - Not: `deepening-by-linkage` (there the voice is declared to send the listener deeper; here it is only what attention rests on); `inner-voice` (that entry claims the voice becomes the listener's own thoughts); `stimulus-discrimination` (that entry restricts a response to this voice and no other; it is about who can evoke the response, not where…)
- `narrowing-attention` (unit) **Narrowing attention** - Suggest that everything except one chosen thing fades into the background.
  - Cues: 'everything else' followed by fades, drifts away, disappears, falls away, doesn't matter; 'nothing else matters', 'nothing but', 'only this'; 'the rest of the world', 'the room around you' with a receding verb; 'narrow', 'tunnel', 'shrinks down to', 'until there is only'; lists of things that become distant or unimportant
  - Not: `voice-focus` (that entry names the voice as the object; this one removes everything else and can leave any object); `cognitive-quieting` (that entry slows or stops thinking itself and leaves nothing to attend to; narrowing may send thoughts to the edge…); `incorporation` (incorporation keeps distractions and gives them a role; narrowing sends them away)
- `inward-focus` (unit) **Turning attention inward** - Direct the listener's attention away from the outside world and onto inner sensation, feeling or imagery.
  - Cues: 'inside', 'inward', 'within' as the destination of attention, awareness, focus or mind; 'go inside', 'turn inward', 'drift inside', 'look inside'; an explicit contrast between outside or the room and inside or within; 'away from' an outer thing paired with 'toward' or 'to' an inner one; 'inner world', 'inner space' introduced as where attention now goes
  - Not: `kinesthetic-induction` (that entry stays on one sensation and lets it spread as the induction; this is only the outward-to-inward turn); `body-scan` (a body scan moves attention region by region; this is a single change of direction); `dissociation-suggestion` (dissociation places the listener outside their experience watching it; inward focus places them inside it)
- `sensory-detail-immersion` (unit) **Sensory immersion** - Describe a scene or sensation in dense detail across several senses so it takes over attention.
  - Cues: three or more sensory modalities within a few sentences; runs of perception verbs: see, hear, feel, smell, taste; concrete nouns with texture, temperature, colour, weight or sound modifiers; second person, present tense description with little or no instruction; 'notice the', 'feel the', 'hear the' repeated with different objects
  - Not: `sensory-specific-language` (that is the phrasing choice at the level of a single suggestion; this is a dense passage of three or more senses whose…); `sensory-overlap` (overlap starts in one sense and leads from it into another through a shared feature; immersion sets several senses…); `guided-imagery` (guided imagery leads through a scene step by step; immersion is the descriptive density, with or without movement)
- `curiosity-hook` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Curiosity hook** - Raise a question or promise something early, then delay the answer so the listener keeps attending.
  - Cues: 'in a moment', 'soon', 'before we finish', 'later' with I'll tell you, I'll show you,…; 'there's something', 'one thing', 'a secret' with the content withheld; 'I'll come back to that', 'more on that later'; 'you might be wondering' followed by deferral; a question posed and left unanswered for more than a paragraph
  - Not: `nested-loops` (nested loops is a structure of several stories opened and closed in reverse; a hook is one opened gap); `anticipation-building` (anticipation announces an experience or event and delays it; a hook withholds information); `incomplete-sentence` (that breaks off inside a sentence; a hook is a complete statement that defers its content)
- `direct-address` (unit) **Direct address** - Speak to the listener as 'you' so every line is about them.
  - Cues: statements about the address itself: 'I'm talking to you', 'this is for you', 'I mean you'; 'you' set alone as a sentence or fragment: 'You.', 'Yes, you.'; a switch into 'you' right after a passage about 'people', 'most listeners', 'he', 'she'…; 'you' contrasted with other hearers: 'not them, you', 'whoever else hears this'; 'I ... you' pairings that state the speaker-listener channel: 'I speak, you listen'
  - Not: `second-person-narrative` (that puts 'you' inside a story as its protagonist; direct address is the speaker talking to the listener); `first-person-scripting` (that is written in the listener's own voice as 'I'); `parasocial-intimacy` (that simulates a personal relationship; direct address is only the grammatical choice it is built on)
- `name-use` (variant, variant of `direct-address`) **Using the listener's name** - Address the listener by their name, a pet name or a title, placed where attention should sharpen.

  - Not: `time-distortion` (the general suggestion that time stretches or stops mattering, with no stated interval or task); `silence-deepener` (a framed gap for deepening with no claim about how long it feels; they often occur together); `post-session-proof` (points at elapsed time after waking as evidence, a different moment and purpose)
- `eye-contact-imagery` (unit) **Gaze capture** - Use the speaker's eyes, imagined or shown on screen, as the point the listener's attention locks onto.

- `novelty-orienting` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Attention call** - Change something noticeable, or call for attention outright, to bring a drifting listener back.

## Imagery, metaphor and story (`imagery-story`)

Devices that carry a suggestion in a picture, a comparison or a story instead of stating it outright.

- `guided-imagery` (unit) **Guided imagery** - Lead the listener through an imagined scene step by step, telling them what to see, hear and feel there.
  - Cues: 'imagine', 'picture', 'see yourself', 'find yourself', 'in your mind's eye'; second-person present-tense description of a place the listener is not in; sequential movement words: 'as you walk', 'ahead of you', 'now you come to'; sense verbs applied to imagined things: 'feel the', 'hear the', 'notice the colour'; open slots: 'a place of your choosing', 'whatever it looks like to you'
  - Not: `visualization-induction` (same technique; label that only when the scene is the script's first trance-producing passage, with no completed…); `second-person-narrative` (that has events, other characters or a goal for 'you'; guided imagery has a place and things to notice, and nothing…); `metaphor` (a metaphor's scene stands for something else; guided imagery is taken at face value)
- `safe-place` (variant, variant of `guided-imagery`) **Safe place** - Have the listener build an imagined place where they are completely safe, and use it as a base to rest in or return to.
  - Cues: 'safe place', 'special place', 'sanctuary', 'a place where you feel completely safe'; listener choice of scene: 'real or imagined', 'wherever feels right to you'; ownership and protection statements: 'this place is yours', 'nothing can disturb you here'; return instructions: 'you can come back here any time'; later callbacks: 'go back to your special place'
  - Not: `comfort-safety` (that tells the listener directly that they are safe, with no imagined place); `visualization-induction` (that is any scene used to start trance; label safe-place when safety and return are the point); `safety-override` (that is an emergency-wake provision, not a scene)
- `metaphor` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Metaphor** - Tell a story or offer an image whose structure matches the change you want, and let the listener draw the parallel.
  - Cues: a story or image about a third party or natural process, placed in a suggestion passage…; process vocabulary shared with the session goal: 'letting go', 'settling', 'opening',…; story openers mid-script: 'there was once', 'I knew someone who', 'a tree in winter'; the story's resolution matches the suggestion that follows or precedes it; no 'like' or 'just as' linking it to the listener
  - Not: `analogy` (analogy states the comparison and its point; metaphor leaves the mapping to the listener); `modeling` (that shows a person performing the target response itself and what it earned them; a metaphor's subject does something…); `my-friend-john` (that reports another person responding to hypnotic suggestions; a metaphor's story is about a different subject…)
- `extended-metaphor` (variant, variant of `metaphor`) **Extended metaphor** - Introduce one image early and return to it at every stage, so each new suggestion arrives as more of an image the listener has already accepted.
  - Cues: the same image nouns recurring in three or more separate sections ('the lake', 'the…; back-references to the image: 'that same river', 'the garden from earlier', 'remember…; each stage phrased as a further feature of one image (deeper water, the bottom, the…; the emergence reverses or completes the opening image; suggestions stated in the image's terms instead of plainly ('let it settle to the bottom')
  - Not: `metaphor` (the parent covers any metaphor, including one told once; label this when one image is reused across stages); `story-frame-structure` (a story frame puts the listener inside a fictional scene for the whole session; an extended metaphor stays a stand-in…); `refrain` (a refrain repeats the same line; this repeats an image in new wording with a new feature each time)
- `embodied-metaphor` (unit) **State-as-substance** - Give the state a physical form, such as fog, warm liquid or light, that fills, coats or drains from the body.

  - Not: `travelling-sensation` (that moves a bare feeling (warmth, heaviness, 'a wave of relaxation') along a path; label this entry only when a…); `imagined-substance` (that is an agent taken in (drunk, breathed, injected) and credited with causing the state; here the material is the…); `analogy` (a 'like' may introduce the material, but the material then moves through the body; an analogy names a familiar case…)

Separate the listener from part of their experience, or divide the self into parts that can be addressed one at a time.

- `dissociation-suggestion` (unit) **Dissociation** - Suggest that the listener watches their own body, thoughts or feelings from outside, at a distance.

  - Not: `body-dissociation` (that changes how the body feels (numb, distant, not yours) without moving the viewpoint); `observer-self` (the variant names a lasting watcher part; the unit is any outside view); `externalization` (that moves a thought or feeling out; here the listener moves out)
- `observer-self` (variant, variant of `dissociation-suggestion`) **Observer self** - Set up a calm part of the listener that only watches, while the rest of them responds.
  - Cues: 'observer', 'watcher', 'witness' as a noun for part of the listener; 'a part of you that just watches', 'simply observing'; 'at the back of your mind', 'from the edge', 'from a safe distance'; 'while the rest of you ...' paired with a watching part; 'without interfering', 'without judging', 'only noticing'
  - Not: `protected-core` (there a part filters, protects or rejects what is unwanted, so vigilance can drop; label observer-self only when the…); `parts-work` (parts are addressed, thanked, given jobs or set against each other; the observer only watches); `conscious-unconscious-split` (there the unconscious is addressed as the listener that matters; here a conscious watcher is kept on duty)
- `automaticity-framing` (unit) **It happens by itself** - Describe each response as happening on its own, so the listener experiences it as not done by them.
  - Cues: 'by itself', 'all on its own', 'automatically', 'of its own accord'; 'without you doing anything', 'you are not doing it', 'without any help from you'; 'it just happens', 'it is happening'; body part or state as subject of a verb of movement or change, with no command: 'the arm…; 'that hand', 'the eyelids' in place of 'your' as the subject of such a verb (with…

- `conscious-unconscious-split` (unit) **Conscious / unconscious split** - Speak of the conscious and unconscious minds as two separate listeners, and address suggestions to the unconscious one.

## Conditioning (`conditioning`)

Build a lasting response by pairing cues with states, attaching consequences to behavior, and repeating until it runs without effort.

- `practice-schedule` (unit) **Repeat listening** - Tell the listener how often and for how long to listen or practice.
  - Cues: frequency phrases: 'once a day', 'every night', 'twice a week', 'for the next N days'; imperatives to replay: 'listen again', 'come back tomorrow', 'play this daily'; a stated course length or end point: 'for two weeks', 'until it feels automatic', 'then…; a missed-day or review clause: 'if you miss a day', 'after a week, decide whether to…; appears in the description, pre-talk or the last lines before the wake-up
  - Not: `reinduction-seed` (says future sessions will go faster and deeper ('each time you listen...'); a schedule says when and how often to…); `obedience-task` (assigns a separate action to carry out (an exercise, a report, an errand); a schedule only prescribes replaying or…); `craving-framing` (describes relistening as need or addiction; a schedule is a plain instruction)
- `habit-cue` (unit) **Habit stacking** - Attach the new behavior to something the listener already does every day, in a when-then sentence.
  - Cues: 'whenever you [daily routine], you [behavior]'; 'right after you ...', 'as soon as you ...', 'every time you finish ...'; first-person plan form: 'when I ..., I will ...', 'if ..., then I ...'; the cue is an ordinary routine: brushing teeth, pouring coffee, sitting down, getting…; the response is a deliberate action the listener performs, not a state that arrives by…
  - Not: `environmental-trigger` (also uses an everyday cue, but the response is framed as automatic and installed, not a plan the listener carries out); `posthypnotic-suggestion` (any suggestion for a later response; habit stacking is specifically an openly stated plan tied to an existing routine); `contingent-suggestion` (links a response to something happening now in the session, not to a recurring cue in daily life)
- `overlearning` (unit) **Drill** - Fire the cue, get the response, reset, and do it again many times in one session.
  - Cues: 'again', 'once more', 'one more time', 'and again' repeated across a passage; three or more consecutive cue-response-reset cycles with near-identical wording; explicit practice language: 'let's practice', 'we'll do this ten times', 'another round'; numbered rounds: 'that was one', 'round three'; a reset instruction between firings: 'and come back up', 'open your eyes', 'let it go'
  - Not: `trigger-test` (one or two firings staged as proof ('let's test that'); three or more identical cycles, counted rounds or 'again' make…); `fractionation` (wake-and-drop cycles with a depth comparison ('deeper than before'); a drill names one cue and its response and…); `repetition-of-suggestion` (the suggestion's wording is repeated but the response is not fired and reset each time)
- `mantra` (unit) **Mantra** - Repeat one short fixed phrase many times, spoken by the voice, by the listener, or by both.
  - Cues: the same phrase of two or more words, four or more times in immediate succession; one identical line recurring five or more times within a passage; 'your mantra', 'chant', 'keep repeating', 'over and over'; repeated lines set as their own block or list; stage directions such as [mantra loops under voice] or [x10]
  - Not: `repetition-induction` (the repeated loop opens the session and is the only induction present, with content limited to listening or relaxing;…); `epizeuxis` (one word or a two-word phrase said two to four times inside a line; a mantra is a whole phrase repeated four or more…); `refrain` (a line that returns at intervals through the script, not in a dense repeated block)
- `repeat-after-me` (unit) **Repeat after me** - Tell the listener to say the suggestion themselves, aloud or silently, in words the writer supplies.
  - Cues: 'repeat after me', 'say it with me', 'say these words', 'tell yourself', each followed…; 'out loud or in your mind', 'in your own voice', 'silently'; a quoted or set-off first-person line followed by a written pause or [pause for response]; a second-person instruction immediately followed by a first-person line; ratification right after the gap: 'good', 'that's right', 'again'
  - Not: `first-person-scripting` (the suggestion is written as 'I' but there is no instruction to say it); `public-commitment` (the supplied line is a promise about future conduct ('I will...', 'I promise...'); repeat after me has the listener…); `self-persuasion` (the listener is asked to supply the words or reasons themselves ('tell me why'); here the writer supplies the exact…)

### Classical conditioning (`classical-conditioning`)

Devices that work by pairing: a cue is presented with a state often enough that the cue alone begins to evoke it.

- `stimulus-pairing` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Pairing** - Present a cue together with a state, again and again, so that the cue alone begins to bring the state.
  - Cues: the same cue word or sound recurring right next to the same state description, three or…; the cue dropped in as a bare word or sound between state descriptions, with no sentence…; 'the word and the feeling', 'linked', 'connected', 'associated', 'tied together', 'go…; cue set off in quotes, italics or caps and followed by a ratifying state phrase ('there…; stage directions placing a sound effect at the moment a state is described
  - Not: `trigger-installation` (that states the rule ('when I say X you will Y', 'each time you hear X'); pairing just makes cue and state co-occur…); `anchoring` (anchoring is an explicit capture step: the script builds or recalls one state and tells the listener to apply a cue…); `positive-reinforcement` (there the pleasant event follows something the listener did; here nothing is required of the listener)
- `pleasure-pairing` (variant, variant of `stimulus-pairing`) **Pleasure pairing** - Pair a target word, act or idea with pleasure or arousal, so the target itself comes to feel good.
  - Cues: 'X feels good', 'X is pleasure', 'it feels so good to X' repeated with the same X; pleasure or arousal words (good, warm, bliss, pleasure, tingle) adjacent to the target…; equations of target and pleasure: 'obedience is pleasure', 'focus is bliss'; 'X and the pleasure, together', 'that good feeling attaches to X', 'the feeling soaks…; no condition on the pleasure (no 'when you do', 'because you', 'as a reward', 'you have…
  - Not: `pleasure-reward` (reward arrives after and because of compliance; pairing has target and pleasure present together with no condition); `pleasure-deepener` (that ties pleasure to trance depth; this ties pleasure to a target word, act or idea); `evaluative-conditioning` (that changes liking by pairing with already-valued things in general; this specifically uses a felt pleasure or…)
- `evaluative-conditioning` (variant, variant of `stimulus-pairing`) [text-detectable: partial] **Evaluative conditioning** - Place a neutral thing next to things the listener already likes or dislikes, so it takes on their value.
  - Cues: a target term repeatedly placed beside strongly valenced words or images without any…; lists that alternate the target with liked (or disliked) items; the target described in the company of warmth, home, safety, success, or of staleness,…; no causal or reward language ('because', 'when you do', 'earn'); the same target with a different valued companion each time
  - Not: `pleasure-pairing` (that pairs the target with a pleasure state felt in the moment; this pairs it with valued things and changes liking); `counterconditioning` (that takes a cue with a definite existing reaction and holds a felt state against it to replace the reaction; label…); `reframing` (reframing states a new meaning outright; evaluative conditioning never asserts anything, it only places things side by…)
- `higher-order-conditioning` (unit) **Second-order conditioning** - Pair a new cue with a cue that already works, so the response passes to the new cue.
  - Cues: an already-installed cue fired in the passage, with a new cue presented at the moment…; old cue and new cue alternating in close succession ('settle, and press', 'chime, and…; 'will do what X does', 'works just like', 'takes on the same power', said while X is…; 'soon Y alone will be enough', 'you won't need X any more'; the original state is not built again; only the old cue is used to bring it
  - Not: `stimulus-pairing` (pairing joins a cue to the state itself; this joins a cue to another cue); `trigger-installation` (installation states what the new cue does, even when it says 'Y now works like X'; label this only when X is actually…); `trigger-chaining` (chaining makes one trigger's response fire a different trigger; here one response is copied onto a second cue)
- `counterconditioning` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Counterconditioning** - Present a cue that brings an unwanted reaction while the listener is in an incompatible state, so the new reaction replaces the old.
  - Cues: a feared or disliked cue introduced while a relaxed state is explicitly maintained; graded approach: 'from far away', 'a little closer', 'the next step', 'only as far as…; 'still calm', 'stay relaxed as you picture ...', 'and the calm stays'; alternation between the difficult image and a return to relaxation or a safe place; 'instead of the old feeling', 'where there used to be tension, now ...'
  - Not: `extinction` (extinction shows the cue with no outcome and waits for fading; counterconditioning supplies an opposing response); `collapsing-anchors` (that fires two installed anchors together; this brings the real problem cue into a prepared state); `rehearsal-imagery` (rehearsal has the listener imagine performing the future behavior; here the listener only holds the difficult image…)
- `stimulus-generalization` (unit) **Generalization** - Tell the listener the conditioned response also happens with similar cues, other voices or new settings.
  - Cues: 'not only here', 'anywhere', 'wherever you are', 'in every part of your life'; 'whether you hear it, read it or think it', 'in any voice', 'spoken or written'; lists of settings or cue forms each mapped to the same response; 'just as well as', 'the same way', 'carries over', 'spreads into'; 'eyes open as well as closed', 'awake as well as in trance'
  - Not: `stimulus-discrimination` (discrimination narrows which cue fires the response; generalization widens it); `higher-order-conditioning` (that deliberately pairs one specific new cue with the old one; generalization extends to a class of similar cues or…); `shared-trigger` (a stated grant that a partner or other named people may fire a trigger is the shareable trigger; label generalization…)
- `stimulus-discrimination` (unit) **Discrimination** - Present a look-alike cue and let nothing happen, then present the true cue and let the response follow, so the response is trained to the exact cue.
  - Cues: a contrast pair in the passage: a similar cue presented with no response, then the true…; the cue word spoken inside an ordinary sentence and followed by 'nothing', 'just a…; 'that one ... and this one', 'you know the difference', 'feel the difference', 'not…; two near-identical words, tones or sounds presented in alternation with different stated…; the true cue fired straight after a non-response and ratified ('there', 'that is the one')
  - Not: `trigger-scope` (a condition that is only stated for a named trigger ('only in my voice', 'in conversation it is just a word') is…); `scope-limits` (a stated limit on where the session's suggestions apply is scope limits; discrimination is the contrast presentation…); `extinction` (extinction presents the trained cue itself with nothing following until it fades; here a look-alike gets nothing and…)
- `extinction` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Extinction** - Present the cue again and again with nothing following it, until the conditioned response fades.
  - Cues: a known cue repeated with explicit non-response: 'nothing happens', 'nothing follows',…; 'just a word', 'just a sound', 'an ordinary word again'; 'fades', 'weaker each time', 'loses its pull', 'less and less'; the cue presented while alertness is stressed ('wide awake', 'eyes open'); a cue that earlier in the script or series was followed by a state, now presented three…
  - Not: `trigger-removal` (removal cancels the cue by declaration, and one firing afterwards to show nothing happens is still removal; label…); `counterconditioning` (that pairs the cue with a new incompatible response; extinction pairs it with nothing); `semantic-satiation` (satiation repeats any word in a rapid run until its meaning blurs; extinction presents an installed cue, spaced, and…)

### Operant conditioning (`operant-conditioning`)

Devices that change how often the listener does something by controlling what follows it: reward, relief, cost, or nothing.

- `positive-reinforcement` (unit) **Positive reinforcement** - Right after the listener does what you want, give them something they want, so they do it again.
  - Cues: instruction followed at once by a pleasant consequence; 'because you ...', 'for doing that', 'as your reward'; 'every time you ..., you get/feel ...'; 'you've earned', 'that's what you get for'; reward wording that names the response it follows
  - Not: `stimulus-pairing` (pairing presents the good feeling alongside a cue whatever the listener does; reinforcement gives it only after a…); `contingent-suggestion` (a contingent suggestion hangs the wanted response on something that is happening anyway ('as you breathe out, you…); `ratification` (ratification confirms that a response is happening and real; reinforcement adds a wanted consequence for it)
- `praise` (variant, variant of `positive-reinforcement`) **Praise** - Say a short word of approval the moment the listener does the wanted thing.
  - Cues: evaluation of the listener: 'well done', 'good boy/girl', 'you did that perfectly',…; the speaker's approval stated: 'I'm proud of you', 'I'm pleased', 'that's exactly what I…; 'good' or 'very good' extended with what was done well: 'good, just like that breath'; placed in the beat right after an instruction or a pause; bare 'good' or 'that's right' alone is ratification, not praise
  - Not: `ratification` (ratification confirms the response is happening; praise judges the listener as having done well); `labeling` (labeling assigns a standing trait to get matching behavior later; praise rewards one response just made); `love-bombing` (affection flooding is heavy approval given up front, not contingent on any response)
- `pleasure-reward` (variant, variant of `positive-reinforcement`) **Pleasure as reward** - Suggest a burst of pleasant feeling that arrives the moment the listener complies.
  - Cues: 'wave of pleasure', 'pulse', 'rush', 'glow' placed directly after a response; 'because you ..., feel ...'; 'every time you obey/follow/let go, it feels ...'; 'your reward is', 'here it comes'; sensation wording that is conditional on an act, not free-standing description
  - Not: `pleasure-pairing` (pairing presents pleasure together with a word or idea, no response required; here pleasure is given for a response); `pleasure-deepener` (the deepener ties pleasure to depth of trance, not to a specific act of compliance); `suggested-sensation` (a suggested sensation on its own is a phenomenon or proof; it becomes a reward only when made contingent on a response)
- `clicker-training` (variant, variant of `positive-reinforcement`) [text-detectable: partial] **Marker signal** - Use one brief, distinct sound or word to mark the exact moment the listener gets it right, with the reward to follow.
  - Cues: stage directions: [click], [chime], [snap] placed right after a response; explanation of the signal: 'that sound means you did it right'; one fixed short word repeated at response moments, where the script has earlier said…; an early passage pairing the signal with reward before it is used; signal followed, not preceded, by the response it marks
  - Not: `sound-trigger` (a sound trigger comes before the response and evokes it; a marker comes after the response and rewards it); `praise` (praise carries approval in its meaning and varies in wording; a marker is one fixed signal whose value comes from…); `ratification` (ratification confirms a response in ordinary words; a marker is a trained signal backed by reward)
- `negative-reinforcement` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Negative reinforcement** - Let the wanted response end something unpleasant, so the relief strengthens that response.
  - Cues: 'the moment you ..., it stops/eases/drains away'; 'as soon as you give in, the tension is gone'; 'relief', 'release', 'let it stop', 'no more effort'; an unpleasant state described first, then ended by compliance; 'it only gets easier when you ...'
  - Not: `punishment` (punishment adds something unpleasant after an unwanted response to reduce it; negative reinforcement removes something…); `fear-then-relief` (fear-then-relief uses the relieved moment to slip in a request; here relief is the consequence of the response itself); `tension-release` (tense-and-release is a relaxation method using muscle contrast, with no contingency on compliance)
- `punishment` (unit) **Punishment** - Follow the unwanted response with something unpleasant, so the listener does it less.
  - Cues: 'if you ..., then' followed by an unpleasant consequence; 'you will be corrected', 'there are consequences', 'you'll regret'; extra work added after a named lapse: 'do it again', assigned lines or chores (nothing…; suggested unpleasant sensation tied to a named lapse; scolding after a lapse: 'no', 'bad', 'wrong', 'I'm disappointed'
  - Not: `negative-reinforcement` (negative reinforcement increases a response by ending something unpleasant; punishment decreases a response by adding it); `differential-reinforcement` (there the unwanted response only goes unrewarded; punishment adds something unpleasant for it); `humiliation` (humiliation uses shame as emotional charge and need not be contingent on any lapse)
- `response-cost` (variant, variant of `punishment`) **Response cost** - After the unwanted response, take away something the listener values.
  - Cues: 'you lose', 'I take away', 'is withdrawn', 'forfeit'; an accumulated total wiped: 'the streak goes back to zero', 'your points reset'; 'minus', 'deduct', 'costs you'; privilege or permission revoked after a named lapse; the thing removed was given or earned earlier in the script or series
  - Not: `punishment` (plain punishment adds something unpleasant or extra work; response cost removes something already held); `time-out` (time-out suspends the chance to earn or receive rewards for a stated period; response cost takes back a specific thing…); `deprivation` (deprivation withholds in advance to raise a reward's value; response cost removes as a consequence of a lapse)
- `time-out` (variant, variant of `punishment`) **Time-out** - After the unwanted response, shut off the listener's access to rewards for a short, stated period.
  - Cues: 'for the next ten breaths / one minute / until I count to', attached to a named lapse; 'no praise', 'no reward', 'nothing from me', 'you get nothing until'; 'sit out', 'wait out', 'you wait', 'time-out'; 'switches off', 'is paused', 'on hold' said of a pleasant state, with a stated return; 'then it comes back', 'then we go on' closing the interval
  - Not: `response-cost` (response cost takes back something already held (points, a streak); time-out removes the chance to receive anything…); `extinction` (extinction stops rewarding one response with no end date and no announcement; time-out is a brief, bounded suspension…); `deprivation` (deprivation withholds in advance to raise a reward's value; time-out withholds afterwards as a consequence)
- `shaping` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Shaping** - Reward a small, easy version of the response first, then require a little more each time before rewarding.
  - Cues: 'a little more each time', 'this time, go further before ...'; 'that was enough before; now ...'; the same response requested in increasing size or duration, with reward after each; 'first just ..., then ..., now ...' with praise between steps; explicit reference to an earlier, easier standard
  - Not: `compliance-ladder` (the ladder relies on consistency with earlier agreements; shaping relies on a reward criterion that moves); `escalation` (graded escalation asks for more each time; shaping is the reinforcement of each approximation along the way); `chaining` (chaining links several different responses into a sequence; shaping refines one response)
- `intermittent-reinforcement` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Intermittent reinforcement** - Reward the response only some of the time, unpredictably, so it keeps going when rewards stop.
  - Cues: 'sometimes', 'now and then', 'not every time', 'you never know when'; 'maybe this time', 'you'll have to keep going to find out'; repeated identical instruction where only some repetitions are followed by reward; praise or reward density that drops across a passage while the instruction continues; 'unpredictable', 'at random', 'when you least expect'
  - Not: `trauma-bonding` (hot-and-cold alternates warmth with coldness or hostility in a relationship; an intermittent schedule only omits some…); `extinction` (extinction stops the reward entirely so the response fades; an intermittent schedule keeps paying occasionally so it…); `anticipation-building` (anticipation announces something coming and delays it once; a schedule governs reward across many responses)
- `differential-reinforcement` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Differential reinforcement** - Reward one response and give nothing for the other, so the listener shifts toward the rewarded one.
  - Cues: paired contrast: 'when you A, nothing; when you B, you get ...'; 'I only reward', 'only when you', 'nothing for ..., everything for ...'; 'instead of X, do Y, and ...'; 'every minute/moment without ... earns'; explicit ignoring of one response beside reward of another
  - Not: `punishment` (punishment adds an unpleasant consequence for the unwanted response; here the unwanted response just goes unrewarded); `extinction` (extinction only withholds what used to follow; differential reinforcement withholds from one response and rewards…); `stimulus-discrimination` (discrimination restricts a response to one cue or setting; differential reinforcement chooses between two responses)
- `chaining` (unit) **Behavior chaining** - Link several responses into a fixed sequence, so finishing each one is the signal to start the next.
  - Cues: 'and that is your signal to', 'which leads into', 'tells you to'; three or more distinct acts given in a fixed order; 'always in that order', 'each step leads to the next'; 'then ... then ... then ...' ending in a single reward; a completed act named as the start condition for the next act
  - Not: `trigger-chaining` (chaining anchors links installed cues and states; behavior chaining links the listener's own acts); `habit-cue` (habit stacking attaches one new behavior to one existing daily cue; a chain has three or more acts, each cueing the next); `contingent-suggestion` (a contingent suggestion ties one response to one ongoing event in a sentence; a chain is a trained sequence of several…)
- `premack-principle` (unit) **Earn-it contingency** - Make something the listener wants to do available only after they do something they want less.
  - Cues: 'first ..., then you may ...'; 'once you have ..., you can ...'; 'you're allowed to ... only after'; 'earn', 'earned the right to', 'not until you'; permission to do something wanted, conditional on a completed act
  - Not: `reward-contingent-on-depth` (that variant belongs to tease and denial and gates built-up release; the earn-it rule is the general first-then form…); `implied-directive` (an implied directive makes an inner response signal itself by a movement; no wanted activity is being earned); `contingent-suggestion` (a contingent suggestion links a response to something already happening; here a wanted activity is withheld until a…)
- `token-economy` (unit) **Points and tokens** - Give symbolic rewards (points, stars, a streak count) that add up toward something the listener wants.
  - Cues: 'point', 'points', 'score', 'star', 'token', 'credit'; 'streak', 'days in a row', 'level up', 'unlock'; 'that's one', 'add one to your count'; an exchange rule: 'N points earns ...'; instruction to keep or report a running total
  - Not: `clicker-training` (a marker signal marks the moment and is not saved up; tokens accumulate and are exchanged); `depth-scale` (a depth scale puts a number on the state, not on rewards earned); `sunk-cost` (the sunk-cost appeal cites past investment as a reason to continue; a token system is the counting itself)
- `deprivation` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Deprivation** - Hold back something the listener wants for a while, so it is worth more when you offer it as a reward.
  - Cues: 'I'm not going to give you ... for now', 'none of that for a while'; 'save it', 'go without', 'until I say', 'until next time'; 'the longer you wait, the better/stronger'; a named reward the listener has already had, explicitly withheld before a task or…; no rising description of the wanted thing coming closer (that is tease and denial) and…
  - Not: `tease-and-denial` (tease and denial actively raises desire and then withholds satisfaction; deprivation is the withholding alone, with…); `response-cost` (response cost takes something away because of a lapse; deprivation withholds in advance to raise value); `anticipation-building` (anticipation announces and delays an event; deprivation restricts access to a reward the listener already knows)
- `satiation` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Satiation** - Give so much of a reward, or so much of a behavior, that the listener stops wanting it.
  - Cues: 'as much as you want', 'all of it', 'more than you need'; 'until you've had enough', 'until it's boring', 'until you're tired of it'; instruction to repeat an urge or behavior on purpose, many times, with a stated end…; 'you don't need it any more', 'it's lost its pull'
  - Not: `reverse-psychology` (reverse psychology instructs the opposite and expects the listener not to do it; satiation expects them to do it, in…); `semantic-satiation` (semantic satiation repeats one word until it loses meaning; this repeats a reward or behavior until it loses value); `extinction` (extinction withholds the outcome until the response fades; satiation supplies the outcome until it loses value)
- `self-reinforcement` (unit) **Self-administered reinforcement** - Teach the listener to reward themselves each time they do the wanted behavior, so it continues without the speaker.
  - Cues: 'tell yourself', 'say to yourself', 'give yourself'; 'reward yourself', 'let yourself feel proud'; 'each time you notice you have ..., you ...'; 'you don't need me to say it'; reflexive pronoun plus reward wording, conditional on a behavior
  - Not: `affirmation` (affirmations are repeated on a schedule whatever the listener did; self-reward is given only after the behavior); `self-surveillance` (self-policing has the listener watch for and report lapses; self-reinforcement has them reward successes); `internal-trigger` (an internal-event trigger makes a thought or feeling the cue for a response; here the listener's response is followed…)
- `group-contingency` (unit) **Group contingency** - Make a reward or penalty depend on how other members of a group behave, so members watch and press one another.
  - Cues: 'when everyone has', 'once all of you', 'if the whole group', 'only when every one of you'; a reward or unlock stated for 'all of you', 'the group', 'the team', 'you both'; one person's outcome tied to another's act: 'if your partner ..., you ...'; a group total or shared goal: 'together you need', 'the group's count'; instructions to check on, remind or encourage other members
  - Not: `social-proof` (social proof reports what others do as a guide; a group contingency makes the listener's outcome depend on what others…); `token-economy` (a token economy counts one listener's own responses toward that listener's own reward; here the count is across people); `unity` (unity invokes shared identity with no consequence attached; a group contingency attaches a stated reward or cost)

### Triggers and anchors (`triggers-anchors`)

Devices that set up a cue so that, later, the cue alone brings back a state or sets off an action.

- `anchoring` (unit) **Anchoring** - Build or recall a state, and at its strongest moment attach a distinct cue so the cue can bring the state back.
  - Cues: 'as that feeling peaks', 'at its strongest', 'when it is at its fullest' followed by a…; 'press your thumb and finger together', 'touch your wrist', 'say the word ... to…; the words 'anchor', 'anchored', 'anchor this feeling'; state elicitation ('remember a time when...') immediately before a gesture or word…; 'hold... and release' timing around the cue
  - Not: `trigger-installation` (installation declares a cue-response rule in words ('whenever X, you Y'); anchoring has the cue applied while the…); `physical-trigger` (that is a stated when-then rule whose cue is a touch or gesture; a touch applied at the peak of a live state with no…); `stimulus-pairing` (pairing is the general repeated co-presentation of cue and state; anchoring is the specific peak-timed, usually…)
- `trigger-installation` (unit) **Trigger installation** - Tell the listener that a named cue will produce a named response from now on.
  - Cues: 'whenever I say...', 'each time you hear...', 'every time you see...' followed by a…; 'from now on', 'from this moment', 'in the future' before a cue-response rule; a quoted or capitalized word or phrase named as a cue; 'automatically', 'instantly', 'without thinking', 'all on its own' attached to the…; the words 'trigger', 'cue', 'signal', 'keyword'
  - Not: `anchoring` (anchoring attaches the cue during a live peak state; installation states the rule in words); `posthypnotic-suggestion` (the broader form; label it alone when the later response has no discrete, reusable cue (a time, a general change). A…); `contingent-suggestion` (ties a response to something already going on in the session (the breath, the voice, a count) and names no cue for…)
- `trigger-word` (variant, variant of `trigger-installation`) **Trigger word / phrase** - Install a spoken or written word or short phrase as the cue.
  - Cues: a word or phrase set off in quotes, capitals or italics and then reused verbatim later; 'the word...', 'the phrase...', 'when I say...', 'when you hear the words...'; 'keyword', 'trigger word', 'command word', 'special word'; the same marked word appearing alone as a one-word line later in the script
  - Not: `mantra` (a mantra is repeated for its effect while repeating; a trigger word is said once to set off a response); `reinduction-trigger` (that is a trigger of any cue type whose response is specifically return to trance); `sound-trigger` (non-verbal sound, not language)
- `sound-trigger` (variant, variant of `trigger-installation`) [text-detectable: partial] **Sound trigger** - Install a non-verbal sound, such as a snap, bell or tone, as the cue.
  - Cues: 'snap', 'click', 'bell', 'chime', 'tone', 'gong', 'clap' inside a when-then rule; bracketed or parenthesized sound directions: [snap], (bell), *click*; 'when you hear the sound of...', 'that sound means...', 'each time it rings'; 'on the snap', 'at the sound'
  - Not: `sound-effects` (that is the production choice of using sounds; this is a sound given a stated response); `clicker-training` (a marker signal says 'that was right' after a response; a sound trigger comes before and causes the response); `metronome` (a continuous beat for pacing, not a discrete cue with a response)
- `physical-trigger` (variant, variant of `trigger-installation`) **Touch / gesture trigger** - Install a touch, gesture, posture or worn object as the cue.
  - Cues: 'press', 'squeeze', 'touch', 'tap', 'place your hand' inside a when-then rule; a named body location with a gesture: 'thumb and finger', 'your wrist', 'your shoulder'; 'when you wear', 'when you put on', 'while it is on' with an object (ring, bracelet,…; 'when you kneel', 'when you sit in this position', 'each time you take this posture'; 'when I touch your...', 'a tap on the...'
  - Not: `anchoring` (anchoring applies a touch at the peak of a live state without stating a rule; label this only when a when-then rule…); `physical-deepener` (a limb drop used once to deepen, with no standing cue for later); `symbolic-ritual` (an imagined or one-time act that stands for the change, not a reusable cue)
- `visual-trigger` (variant, variant of `trigger-installation`) **Visual trigger** - Install a seen image, color, symbol or shown object as the cue.
  - Cues: 'whenever you see', 'when you look at', 'each time I show you' followed by a specific…; named colors, shapes, symbols or pictures inside a when-then rule; 'on your screen', 'this image', 'this symbol', 'the picture of'; 'when I show you', 'when I hold up', 'when it appears': the image is presented by…
  - Not: `trigger-word` (a word is a trigger word in any medium, including text on a screen; label this only when the cue is recognized by its…); `environmental-trigger` (that uses sights the day supplies without anyone deciding ('every red light'); this is one chosen image that the…); `spiral-fixation` (looking at a visual to induce trance, with no installed response for later)
- `environmental-trigger` (variant, variant of `trigger-installation`) **Everyday-cue trigger** - Use an ordinary event that recurs in the listener's day as the cue, so daily life fires the trigger without anyone present.
  - Cues: 'every time you', 'whenever you', 'each time you' followed by a routine event (open a…; 'throughout your day', 'in your daily life', 'wherever you are'; 'even when I'm not there', 'without anyone saying anything'; common objects or events named as cues: doorways, mirrors, traffic lights,…
  - Not: `habit-cue` (habit stacking attaches a deliberate behavior to a routine; this attaches an automatic state or response); `visual-trigger` (a specific chosen image shown on purpose, not whatever the day supplies); `stimulus-generalization` (spreading an existing response to more cues, not installing a rule on a named everyday event)
- `internal-trigger` (variant, variant of `trigger-installation`) **Internal-event trigger** - Use one of the listener's own thoughts, feelings or urges as the cue.
  - Cues: 'whenever you feel', 'whenever you think', 'each time you notice', 'the moment you catch…; an emotion, urge, sensation or quoted thought in the when-clause; 'the first sign of', 'as soon as that feeling starts'; 'the urge to', 'the thought of' followed by a response rule
  - Not: `thought-replacement` (the narrower case where a thought is stopped or swapped for another thought; here any response can follow); `contingent-suggestion` (ties a response to a feeling present now in session, not a standing rule for later); `environmental-trigger` (the cue is an outside event, not a thought or feeling)
- `thought-replacement` (variant, variant of `trigger-installation`) **Thought replacement** - Suggest that a named unwanted thought stops, or is automatically swapped for a chosen one, whenever it appears.
  - Cues: 'whenever you think ...' + replacement; 'that thought turns into ...' / 'in its place'; 'the thought stops'; quoted thought paired with a quoted replacement
  - Not: `internal-trigger` (an internal-event trigger can fire any response; this targets thought content and replaces it); `cognitive-quieting` (mind-blanking quiets thought in general; this swaps a specific one); `inner-voice` (inner-voice suggestion changes whose voice thinks, not which thought)
- `trigger-test` (unit) **Trigger test** - Fire the trigger during the session so the listener feels it work.
  - Cues: 'let's test that', 'let's try it', 'feel it for yourself', 'see how it works'; an installed cue fired within a few lines of its installation; 'in a moment I'll say...' followed by the cue alone on its own line; ratification right after the cue: 'there', 'that's right', 'feel how fast that was'; a partial wake or reset between install and cue
  - Not: `overlearning` (a drill fires and resets many times for strength; a test fires once or twice to prove); `convincer` (any staged proof of trance; a trigger test proves one installed cue); `fractionation` (wake-and-drop for depth, which may use a cue but aims at depth, not at showing the cue works)
- `trigger-stacking` (unit) **Stacking anchors** - Attach several states or responses to the same cue, so one firing brings all of them.
  - Cues: 'as well as', 'on top of that', 'now add', 'and also' applied to an already named cue; the same cue instruction repeated after different state elicitations; a cue installed earlier named again with a new response attached: 'from today it also…; 'the same touch', 'the same word', 'that same signal'; 'stack', 'layer', 'add to your anchor'
  - Not: `trigger-installation` (one rule whose response has several parts is still one install; stacking needs a response added to an existing cue, or…); `trigger-chaining` (chaining is a sequence across several cues; stacking is several responses on one cue); `trigger-reinforcement` (strengthening makes the same response stronger; stacking adds different ones)
- `trigger-chaining` (unit) **Chaining anchors** - Set triggers up in sequence so that each one's response is the cue for the next.
  - Cues: 'which in turn', 'and that brings', 'leads to', 'sets off', 'and as soon as you feel that'; a response from one rule reappearing as the when-clause of the next; ordered sequence of states: 'first... then... then'; 'chain', 'cascade', 'one after another', 'like dominoes'
  - Not: `contingent-suggestion` (a run of 'as you X, you Y' lines about what is happening now is contingent suggestion, however many links; label…); `chaining` (behavior chaining links trained actions through reinforcement; this links installed cues and states by suggestion); `trigger-stacking` (stacking puts several responses on one cue at once)
- `collapsing-anchors` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Collapsing anchors** - Fire the anchor for an unwanted state and the anchor for a stronger wanted state together, so the unwanted one loses its hold.
  - Cues: two cues set on two different places or signals for opposed states; 'both at once', 'at the same time', 'together now', 'fire both'; 'let them mix', 'blend', 'merge', 'cancel out', 'settle'; 'release the first... then the second'; a re-test of the old situation right after: 'think of that now... what is different'
  - Not: `counterconditioning` (the general pairing of a cue with an incompatible response; collapsing is the specific two-anchor, fire-together…); `trigger-removal` (removal cancels a trigger by stating it is gone; collapsing overrides a state by pitting another against it); `apposition-of-opposites` (a phrasing that sets opposites side by side, with no anchors)
- `trigger-reinforcement` (unit) **Trigger strengthening** - Tell the listener the trigger gets stronger, faster or deeper every time it is used.
  - Cues: 'every time', 'each time' with 'stronger', 'faster', 'more automatic', 'easier' said of…; 'twice as fast', 'twice as strong' said of how the trigger will work on later uses, not…; 'the more you use it, the more...'; 'it only grows', 'it never weakens', 'stronger with every use'; 'than the time before', 'than last time'
  - Not: `stepwise-doubling` (there each firing adds a stated amount to the state ('each snap, twice as deep'); here the claim is that the trigger…); `positive-reinforcement` (that follows a response with a reward; this only asserts that strength increases); `reinduction-seed` (the same claim applied to future sessions and inductions, not to one trigger)
- `trigger-scope` (unit) **Trigger conditions** - State who can fire the trigger, where and when it works, and when it does nothing.
  - Cues: 'only when', 'only if', 'only while', 'only in my voice', 'only from me'; 'never while driving', 'never when it would be unsafe', 'not at work'; 'if anyone else says it', 'from anyone else it is just a word'; 'when it is safe and appropriate', 'when you choose', 'if you want it to'; 'nothing happens', 'has no effect' in a stated exception
  - Not: `stimulus-discrimination` (both are passages that narrow a response; label this when the clause sets conditions on a named installed trigger, and…); `scope-limits` (bounds the session's suggestions in general; this bounds one named trigger); `trigger-expiry` (expiry sets when the trigger ends in time; conditions set who and where while it lasts)
- `shared-trigger` (variant, variant of `trigger-scope`) **Shareable trigger** - Write the trigger so that people other than the author can fire it.
  - Cues: 'anyone you choose', 'anyone you share it with', 'someone you trust'; 'your partner', 'your hypnotist', a role named as allowed to use the cue; 'works for', 'will work when ... says it', 'in anyone's voice'; 'give', 'hand over', 'share', 'pass on' applied to the trigger; 'and you can take it back'
  - Not: `stimulus-generalization` (widens a response across similar cues, settings or media; this widens only who may fire one named trigger. 'It works…); `trigger-scope` (the parent covers any condition; use this label when the clause adds people who may fire the trigger); `recruitment-ask` (asks the listener to bring others in to the content, not to let others fire a cue)
- `trigger-removal` (unit) **Trigger removal** - Tell the listener, in plain words, that a named trigger or suggestion no longer has any effect.
  - Cues: 'no longer', 'from now on has no effect', 'is just a word', 'ordinary again'; 'cancel', 'remove', 'clear', 'erase', 'lift', 'undo', 'release' applied to a trigger or…; 'all suggestions from this session are...' followed by a cancellation; an earlier-installed cue named again together with a negation of its response; a cue fired followed by 'nothing happens'
  - Not: `trigger-expiry` (expiry is a time limit set at install; removal is an act of cancelling done later); `extinction` (fades the response by presenting the cue with no outcome, without saying it is cancelled); `amnesia-suggestion` (forgetting the trigger hides it and leaves it active; removal ends it)
- `trigger-expiry` (unit) **Expiry clause** - State, when installing, the time or event after which the trigger or suggestion stops working.
  - Cues: 'until', 'for the next ... days / hours', 'for one week', 'by tomorrow'; 'fades', 'wears off', 'switches off', 'expires', 'lapses'; 'unless you listen again', 'unless renewed', 'each listen renews it'; a count limit: 'three times', 'once only'; 'after that it is just a word', 'and then it is gone'
  - Not: `trigger-removal` (removal cancels by a later statement; expiry is a limit written in from the start); `trigger-scope` (conditions cover who, where and when it may fire; expiry covers how long it exists); `permanence-claim` (the opposite move: asserting the effect never ends)

## Compliance and commitment (`compliance`)

Build agreement in small steps, then use the listener's own words and actions as the reason to go further.

- `compliance-ladder` (unit) **Compliance ladder** - Ask for a series of small agreements or acts, ordered easy to hard, so each one makes the next easier.
  - Cues: three or more requests in a row that increase in size or intimacy; short confirmation after each step ('good', 'that's right') before the next request; 'now' or 'and now' opening successive instructions; early requests are trivial (breathe, sit back, listen) and later ones are the session's…; 'just', 'only', 'for a moment' minimizing the early steps
  - Not: `pacing-and-leading` (pacing states verifiable facts and then one suggestion; a ladder asks for a rising series of responses); `shaping` (shaping reinforces approximations to one final behavior; a ladder orders separate requests by size); `chaining` (chaining makes each response the cue for the next, without any increase in size)
- `yes-set` (variant, variant of `compliance-ladder`) **Yes set** - Give a run of statements or questions the listener will answer yes to, then make the one that matters.
  - Cues: three or more consecutive undeniable statements or easy yes/no questions; tag questions: 'isn't it?', 'aren't you?', 'haven't you?', 'wouldn't it?'; final item introduced with 'and', 'so' or 'which means' and not itself verifiable; parallel sentence shape across the run; explicit prompts such as 'yes?' or 'right?' after each item
  - Not: `pacing-and-leading` (pace and lead is built from statements about present, observable experience (sitting, hearing, breathing); label yes…); `truism` (a truism is one undeniable statement; the yes set is the run of them plus the target); `agreement-harvest` (that asks outright for one yes at a chosen moment, with no run-up of easy items)
- `foot-in-the-door` (variant, variant of `compliance-ladder`) [text-detectable: partial] **Foot in the door** - Get a small request granted first, then make the larger related request you wanted all along.
  - Cues: a minimized first request ('just', 'only', 'for one breath', 'that's all I ask'); explicit completion check or acknowledgment before the next request; second request larger and in the same domain as the first; bridging phrases: 'since you did that', 'now that you have', 'that was easy, so'; exactly two request steps, small then large
  - Not: `low-ball` (low-ball keeps one request and raises its cost after agreement; FITD makes a second, larger request); `door-in-the-face` (opposite order: a large request refused, then a smaller one); `micro-compliance` (that is many trivial acts to build a habit of following, not one meaningful small request before a target)
- `micro-compliance` (variant, variant of `compliance-ladder`) **Small physical tasks** - Give trivial physical instructions (breathe, nod, uncross your legs) so the listener starts following before anything important is asked.
  - Cues: short imperatives for trivial body actions (breathe, nod, sit back, uncross, swallow,…; 'for me' appended to an instruction; several such imperatives in a row near the start of the script; brief acknowledgment after each ('good', 'that's it'); comment on how easy following is ('see how easy that was')

## Social influence (`social-influence`)

Appeals that get a suggestion accepted because of other people: what they do, who the speaker is, and what the listener owes or shares.

- `social-proof` (unit) **Social proof** - Tell the listener what other people do or feel in this situation, so they treat it as the right response.
  - Cues: 'most people', 'many people', 'everyone', 'thousands of listeners'; 'people like you', 'others who', 'listeners often'; a statistic or count about other people's responses; third-person plural subject followed by the response the script wants; 'you're not alone', 'just like everyone else who'
  - Not: `normalizing` (normalizing reassures that a response the listener has, or fears having, is ordinary ('that's normal', 'nothing is…); `modeling` (modeling shows one person performing the response and what it earned them; social proof reports what many do, with no…); `unspecified-referent` ('people can relax' states a possibility about a vague subject; social proof reports what others actually do)
- `testimonial` (variant, variant of `social-proof`) **Testimonial** - Report what a particular earlier listener experienced, in enough detail that the new listener expects the same.
  - Cues: 'one listener', 'someone told me', 'a client of mine', 'a woman who wrote to me'; reported speech or a quoted remark from one past listener; before-and-after story about a third person ('she was sure it wouldn't work... then'); 'they said', 'he described it as', 'she reported', 'told me afterwards'; past-tense report of a finished session, not a scene narrated as it happens
  - Not: `modeling` (a model is shown in the act, with what the response earned them; a testimonial is the person's own report of a…); `my-friend-john` (My friend John delivers live suggestions through the third person; a testimonial reports a finished result); `vicarious-deepening` (vicarious deepening narrates someone going under in the present for the listener to follow)
- `manufactured-consensus` (variant, variant of `social-proof`) [text-detectable: partial] **Manufactured consensus** - Stage the appearance of many people agreeing, using extra voices, crowd sound or characters who all respond the same way.
  - Cues: stage directions for a chorus, crowd, murmur or multiple voices saying the same line; 'we' lines spoken by a group voice that is not the main speaker; scene description in which every other character has already complied; 'all of them', 'every one of them', 'the whole room'; the same short line repeated by different named or numbered voices
  - Not: `social-proof` (plain social proof asserts what others do; this stages the others so the listener seems to witness it); `layered-voices` (layered voices is the audio technique of overlapping tracks, whatever they say); `vicarious-deepening` (vicarious deepening follows one person going under; this shows a unanimous group)
- `authority` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Authority** - Establish the speaker as expert, experienced or entitled to direct, so instructions are followed with less checking.
  - Cues: first-person statements of training, years, numbers of clients or sessions; 'trust me', 'I know', 'leave that to me', 'that's my job'; titles or roles the speaker claims (doctor, hypnotist, teacher, a dominant title); explicit division of roles: 'I lead, you follow'; unhedged declaratives about what will happen, attributed to the speaker's experience
  - Not: `borrowed-authority` (borrowed authority cites an outside source; authority is the speaker's own standing); `prestige-framing` (the potency claim is about how strong the recording or technique is, not who the speaker is); `role-assignment` (role assignment tells the listener what they are and how that role behaves; authority states the speaker's standing or…)
- `borrowed-authority` (variant, variant of `authority`) **Borrowed authority** - Cite science, tradition or a respected expert as backing for what the script says will happen.
  - Cues: 'studies show', 'research shows', 'science tells us', 'it's been proven'; 'doctors', 'psychologists', 'researchers', 'experts' as the subject of a claim; 'for centuries', 'ancient', 'traditional', 'monks have long'; a named expert, school or institution cited in support; technical vocabulary (brainwaves, neurons, the nervous system) used as backing
  - Not: `authority` (authority is the speaker's own expertise or role, not an outside source); `reason-giving` (reason-giving attaches any 'because'; borrowed authority needs a cited source); `social-proof` (social proof cites many ordinary peers, not an expert or a body of knowledge)
- `liking` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Liking** - Make the listener like the speaker, through warmth, shared ground and sincere compliments, before asking for anything.
  - Cues: 'I'm glad you're here', 'it's good to have you', greetings with warmth; 'I'm like that too', 'just like you, I' - stated similarity; compliments not tied to a response just performed; 'I like', 'I enjoy', 'I care about' with the listener as object; light humor shared with the listener
  - Not: `praise` (praise follows a specific response as its reward; liking builds affection before or apart from any response); `matching` (matching mirrors the listener's pace or words; liking is expressed warmth and similarity); `love-bombing` (affection flooding is intense, sustained and out of proportion; liking is ordinary warmth)
- `parasocial-intimacy` (variant, variant of `liking`) [text-detectable: partial] **Parasocial intimacy** - Speak as if in a private, ongoing, one-to-one relationship with the listener, though the recording goes to many.
  - Cues: 'just us', 'just you and me', 'nobody else', 'alone together'; 'I missed you', 'I was thinking about you', 'I'm glad you came back'; references to shared history: 'last time', 'like we always do'; 'I'm right here', 'I'm watching you', 'I can see you' in a recording; pet names and terms of endearment
  - Not: `liking` (liking shows warmth and similarity; parasocial intimacy claims a private ongoing relationship); `isolation` (isolation runs down or replaces the listener's other people ('they wouldn't understand'); intimacy says only that…); `love-bombing` (affection flooding is overwhelming approval used to bind; this is the simulated one-to-one frame at any intensity)
- `self-disclosure` (variant, variant of `liking`) [text-detectable: partial] **Self-disclosure** - The speaker reveals a small personal feeling, flaw or difficulty of their own, which makes them more likeable and easier to trust.
  - Cues: 'I'll admit', 'I have to confess', 'to be honest with you', 'between us' followed by a…; first-person feeling or flaw statements: 'I get nervous', 'I used to struggle with',…; 'stepping out of character', 'speaking as myself for a moment', 'out of role'; a short passage whose subject is 'I' in a script otherwise addressed to 'you'; the speaker naming their own enjoyment, doubt or mistake
  - Not: `confession` (confession has the listener disclose private material to the speaker; here the speaker discloses to the listener); `parasocial-intimacy` (that claims a private ongoing relationship with the listener; a disclosure is one revealed fact about the speaker and…); `meta-commentary` (naming the technique tells the listener what is being done to them; self-disclosure tells them something about the…)
- `reciprocity` (unit) **Reciprocity** - Give the listener something first, name it as given, then ask for something in return.
  - Cues: 'I gave you', 'my gift to you', 'I've done this for you'; 'in return', 'in exchange', 'now do something for me'; 'you owe', 'the least you can do', 'it's only fair'; a benefit described as coming from the speaker, followed within a few lines by a request; 'free', 'at no cost' attached to the session, followed by an ask
  - Not: `positive-reinforcement` (reinforcement rewards after the response; reciprocity gives before the ask); `door-in-the-face` (door in the face trades a concession on a refused large request, not a prior gift); `gratitude-induction` (thanking has the listener express gratitude; reciprocity asks for repayment)
- `scarcity` (unit) **Scarcity** - Present the experience or offer as limited in time, quantity or access, so the listener values it more and acts now.

  - Not: `deprivation` (deprivation actually withholds something; scarcity describes the offer as limited); `loss-gain-framing` (loss framing recasts an outcome as an avoided loss without claiming limited supply); `warning-as-lure` (a warning-as-lure forbids or cautions; scarcity says supply or time is short)
- `exclusivity` (variant, variant of `scarcity`) **Exclusivity** - Tell the listener they have been singled out, or that this is offered only to a few people like them.
  - Cues: 'only for you', 'just for you', 'not for everyone', 'not everyone can'; 'chosen', 'selected', 'singled out', 'special'; 'the few who', 'most people never', 'I don't tell most people'; access wording: 'reserved', 'invitation', 'members only', 'you've earned'; a qualifying condition followed by privileged content
  - Not: `scarcity` (plain scarcity limits time or supply for everyone; exclusivity limits who is allowed); `unity` (unity says speaker and listener share an identity ('we', 'one of us'); exclusivity says the listener was admitted to…); `labeling` (labeling assigns a trait to get matching behavior; exclusivity raises the value of what is offered)
- `unity` (unit) **Unity** - Tell the listener that they and the speaker belong to the same group, so the suggestion comes from one of their own.
  - Cues: 'one of us', 'you belong', 'part of', 'our kind'; a named group or collective noun the listener is placed in; 'we' or 'us' as subject of a statement about what the group is or does ('we're the kind…; family or kin terms used for a non-family group; 'people like us' followed by a norm of the group
  - Not: `liking` (liking is similarity and warmth between two individuals; unity is membership in one group); `social-proof` (social proof cites what others do; unity makes the listener one of them); `labeling` (labeling gives the listener alone a trait and then asks for behavior that fits; unity puts speaker and listener, or…)
- `in-group-language` (variant, variant of `unity`) [text-detectable: partial] **In-group language** - Use special words that members of the group know, so that understanding and using them marks the listener as one of them.
  - Cues: 'we call it', 'what we call', 'regulars call', 'those of us who'; a coined or capitalized term for a state or practice, used without explanation; scare quotes or italics around a term presented as the group's word; 'you know what that means', 'you know the one'; the same coined term recurring across files in a series
  - Not: `loaded-language` (loaded language builds a verdict into the term and narrows thought; in-group terms only mark membership); `state-labeling` (naming the state tells the listener what a state is called; in-group language presents the name as the group's own); `trigger-word` (a trigger word is installed to produce a response; an in-group term only signals belonging)
- `modeling` (unit) **Modeling** - Show or describe someone else doing the wanted response and being rewarded for it, without telling the listener to copy them.
  - Cues: a third person (he, she, a named character, 'the last person who sat here') performing…; the model's reward stated right after the act: praise received, relief, pleasure, approval; 'watch how...', 'notice what happens when she...', 'I once worked with someone who...'; no imperative addressed to the listener within the passage; the model's initial doubt or hesitation described, then overcome
  - Not: `social-proof` (social proof reports what many people do as a norm; modeling shows one person doing it and what it earned them); `my-friend-john` (there the third person receives live suggestions step by step for the listener to carry out; a model is only watched); `vicarious-deepening` (that is the narrower use where the listener follows a described person down into trance; label it when depth is the…)

## Expectation and framing (`framing`)

Devices that set, before and during the session, what the listener expects and how they interpret what happens.

- `contrast-principle` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Contrast** - Put the thing you want judged next to something more extreme, so it seems smaller, easier or better by comparison.
  - Cues: 'compared with', 'compared to', 'next to', 'after all that' linking the target to…; 'all I'm asking', 'just', 'only' directly after a larger effort or request has been…; a harder or more extreme alternative named and then set aside by the speaker ('I could…; 'so much easier than', 'nothing like as', comparatives with the standard stated; an earlier, worse state named so the present one is judged against it ('compared with…
  - Not: `door-in-the-face` (label door in the face only when the large request is actually put to the listener and declined or withdrawn as too…); `anchoring-number` (if the comparison point is a numeral or multiplier ('ten times', 'twice as'), label the reference point; contrast sets…); `antithesis` (antithesis is a balanced figure of opposed ideas; contrast is a comparison meant to shift a judgment)
- `expectation-setting` (unit) **Expectation setting** - Tell the listener in advance what they will experience, so they look for it and read what happens as that.
  - Cues: you will notice; you'll probably find; here's what will happen; in a few minutes; some people feel ... others feel
  - Not: `seeding` (seeding drops the idea casually without presenting it as the plan); `direct-suggestion` (direct suggestion asks for the response now; expectation setting predicts it for later); `normalizing` (normalizing says a response is ordinary so the listener stops worrying about it; expectation setting forecasts what…)
- `prestige-framing` (variant, variant of `expectation-setting`) **Potency claim** - Assert in advance that the recording or technique is unusually strong or effective.
  - Cues: strongest; most powerful; intense; warning: this file; takes hold within
  - Not: `warning-as-lure` (a warning-as-lure frames the strength as a reason not to listen, inviting defiance); `authority` (authority is about the speaker's standing, not the file's strength)
- `warning-as-lure` (variant, variant of `expectation-setting`) **Warning as lure** - Issue a warning about the file's effects that reads as an invitation to listen.
  - Cues: do not listen unless; fair warning; you have been warned; only if you're ready; warning followed by an effect presented as desirable
  - Not: `content-warning` (a content note informs so the listener can decline; a lure is built to make declining less likely); `listening-safety` (listening conditions name a real hazard (driving, operating machinery); a lure names the file's wanted effect as the…); `reverse-psychology` (reverse psychology instructs the opposite of a wanted in-session response; the lure is about whether to listen at all)
- `seeding` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Seeding** - Mention an idea early and in passing so it is already familiar when the real suggestion arrives later.
  - Cues: an image, word or sensation mentioned in passing in the pre-talk or induction that…; asides and anecdotes: 'funny how', 'you know how', 'I once', 'some mornings'; nothing is asked of the listener in the sentence and nothing is predicted for the session; the later suggestion repeats the seed's key word or image
  - Not: `interspersal` (interspersal scatters suggestion words through a passage; seeding plants the idea ahead of time); `expectation-setting` (expectation setting states the plan openly); `curiosity-hook` (a curiosity hook opens a question to hold attention; a seed is not meant to be noticed)
- `reframing` (unit) **Reframing** - Give an experience, behavior or sensation a different meaning so the listener responds to the new meaning.
  - Cues: that just means; what that really is; is not a problem, it's; another way to see it; is exactly what you want when
  - Not: `redefinition` (redefinition changes a word's meaning, not an experience's); `complex-equivalence` (complex equivalence is the 'X means Y' sentence form given to any event; label reframing when the listener already…); `utilization` (utilization gives what the listener brings a job in the process; reframing only changes what it means)
- `sensation-relabeling` (variant, variant of `reframing`) **Relabeling sensation** - Tell the listener that a body sensation they already feel has a specific cause or meaning other than the one they would assume.
  - Cues: that tingling is; that warmth means; if you notice ... that's; that feeling in your ... is the; a named body sensation followed by its meaning
  - Not: `suggested-sensation` (suggested sensation creates the feeling; relabeling reinterprets one already there); `ratifying-trance-signs` (if the meaning given is 'you are going into trance' or 'you are relaxing', label pacing trance signs; relabeling…); `complex-equivalence` (complex equivalence is the general 'X means Y' form; use this label when X is a felt body sensation)
- `failure-reframing` (variant, variant of `reframing`) **No-fail framing** - Declare in advance or after the fact that whatever the listener experiences, including apparent failure, counts as success.
  - Cues: there's no wrong way; whatever happens is right; you can't do this wrong; even if you ... that's fine; if you don't feel anything, that's
  - Not: `open-ended-suggestion` (open-ended suggestion phrases one suggestion to accept all responses; no-fail framing redefines outcomes across the…); `doubt-reframing` (doubt-as-a-sign reframes the listener's doubt, not their outcome); `covering-all-possibilities` (covering all possibilities lists the classes of response for one suggestion)
- `surrender-as-freedom` (variant, variant of `reframing`) **Surrender as freedom** - Present letting go of control, or following instructions, as relief and liberation rather than loss.
  - Cues: the freedom of; such a relief to let go; letting go is; nothing left to carry; free from deciding
  - Not: `comfort-safety` (comfort and safety reassure without reframing control as gain); `desire-attribution` (desire attribution says the listener wants it, not that it frees them); `not-doing-not-knowing` (not doing tells the listener effort is unneeded; it does not claim release as the meaning)
- `resistance-reframing` (variant, variant of `reframing`) **Resistance as participation** - Describe the listener's resistance as one more way of taking part in the process.
  - Cues: resisting is just; even the part of you that pushes back; holding on is another way; your resistance is part of; resistance described as attention or participation
  - Not: `resistance-deepens` ('the harder you resist' claims resistance strengthens the effect); `resistance-utilization` (using resistance gives resistance a job; this only redefines it); `doubt-reframing` (doubt-as-a-sign targets doubt, not active resistance)
- `doubt-reframing` (variant, variant of `reframing`) **Doubt as a sign** - Tell the listener their doubt is itself a sign that trance or the change is happening.
  - Cues: 'if you're wondering whether..., that's a sign'; 'doubt/skepticism means...' or 'is a sign that...'; 'people who doubt often go deepest'; doubt or questioning named, followed by a complex equivalence
  - Not: `resistance-reframing` (resistance as participation re-labels active resistance; this re-labels doubt); `failure-reframing` (no-fail framing makes every outcome success; this targets doubt only); `complex-equivalence` (the general X-means-Y form; this is the case where X is doubt)
- `fiction-frame` (unit) **Fiction frame** - Present the content as fiction, play or pretend, which lowers how strictly the listener evaluates it.
  - Cues: just a story; let's pretend; let's play a game; in this story; none of this has to be real
  - Not: `as-if-frame` (the as-if frame asks the listener to suppose the outcome true and explore it); `story-frame-structure` (the story frame structure is the scene the session is built on; this is the statement declaring it fiction); `fantasy-reality-boundary` (a fantasy boundary states that claims end with the session, a safety move)
- `meta-commentary` (unit) **Naming the technique** - State openly which technique is being used on the listener while using it.
  - Cues: I'm going to use; this is a technique called; notice how I; that's on purpose; what I'm doing now is
  - Not: `expectation-setting` (expectation setting predicts the experience, not the method); `myth-correction` (myth correction addresses beliefs about hypnosis in general); `state-labeling` (naming the state names the listener's experience, not the speaker's technique)
- `myth-correction` (unit) **Myth correction** - Correct mistaken beliefs about hypnosis that would make the listener afraid, skeptical or disappointed.
  - Cues: hypnosis isn't; you won't be unconscious; you can't get stuck; contrary to what you may have seen; a lot of people think
  - Not: `objection-preemption` (objection preemption answers this listener's likely objection, not general misconceptions); `failure-reframing` (no-fail framing redefines outcomes; myth correction fixes false beliefs about hypnosis); `protected-core` (protected core claims suggestions against one's values will be rejected, a specific assurance that may itself be…)
- `objection-preemption` (unit) **Preempting objections** - Name the listener's likely objection before they raise it, and answer it.
  - Cues: you might be thinking; maybe you're wondering; some of you will say; I know what you're thinking; you may feel that ... but
  - Not: `myth-correction` (myth correction targets general misconceptions about hypnosis); `doubt-reframing` (doubt-as-a-sign reinterprets doubt as progress instead of answering it); `mind-reading` (mind reading claims to know the listener's inner state; preemption uses that claim to raise and answer an objection)
- `state-labeling` (unit) **Naming the state** - Tell the listener what the state they are in right now is called.
  - Cues: this is what ... feels like; this is trance; that feeling has a name; this is called; right here is
  - Not: `ratifying-trance-signs` (pacing trance signs lists observable signs as evidence; naming the state gives the whole state a label); `sensation-relabeling` (relabeling sensation gives one bodily feeling a meaning); `labeling` (labeling assigns a trait to the person, not a name to the present state)
- `loss-gain-framing` (unit) **Gain / loss framing** - Describe the same outcome either as something gained or as something lost or avoided, choosing the frame that moves the listener.
  - Cues: you'll miss; you get to keep; don't lose; what you gain; what it costs you to
  - Not: `reframing` (reframing changes an experience's meaning; gain/loss framing changes only its valence description); `scarcity` (scarcity claims the opportunity is limited; a loss frame need not)
- `anchoring-number` (unit) **Reference point** - Offer a number or comparison point that the listener's later judgment of depth, time or intensity will adjust from.
  - Cues: a specific figure for a felt quantity: 'about three minutes', 'around fifty pounds'; 'most people take about', 'usually around', 'as much as' + a number; the figure is followed by a question or invitation to estimate: 'how heavy is yours',…; 'take a guess', 'see how long', 'see how far'; no count, dial or scale attached to the number
  - Not: `stepwise-doubling` ('twice as deep' or 'ten times deeper' tied to a count or breath is an instruction to increase, not a value to judge…); `depth-scale` (a depth scale is a numbered dial or scale for depth; a reference point is one value offered before an estimate); `contrast-principle` (contrast sets two experiences side by side; a reference point is a number)
- `redefinition` (unit) **Redefinition** - Give a familiar word a new definition that suits the suggestion, then keep using the word with that meaning.
  - Cues: real ... is; true ... means; what ... really means; ... is just another word for; a valued word followed by 'is' and an unusual definition
  - Not: `reframing` (reframing changes what an experience means, not what a word means); `complex-equivalence` (in complex equivalence X is an event or behavior that is said to show Y; in redefinition X is a word or value term…); `loaded-language` (loaded language is a group's whole vocabulary of judgment-laden terms)
- `inevitability-framing` (unit) **Inevitability** - Present the outcome as certain, so the only open question is when or how it happens.
  - Cues: sooner or later; it's only a matter of time; you can't help; you can't stop; inevitably
  - Not: `direct-suggestion` (a plain 'you will' states the response; label inevitability only when the wording says it will happen whatever the…); `automaticity-framing` ('it happens by itself' is about who is doing it; inevitability is about whether it can fail to happen); `presupposed-completion` (already-happened framing treats the result as done via grammar instead of asserting it will come)
- `as-if-frame` (unit) **As-if frame** - Ask the listener to suppose the outcome is already true and explore what that is like.
  - Cues: suppose; what would it be like if; imagine that you already; just for a moment, pretend that you; act as though you already
  - Not: `fiction-frame` (the fiction frame declares the whole content to be play); `rehearsal-imagery` (mental rehearsal walks through a specific future behavior); `pretend-induction` (the pretend induction asks the listener to act hypnotized as the route into trance)
- `title-and-description-priming` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Title and description priming** - Use the title, tags, cover text and description to set the listener's expectations before the audio starts.
  - Cues: Title:; Description:; Tags:; Effects:; text outside the spoken script that names effects or style
  - Not: `expectation-setting` (expectation setting is spoken inside the session); `content-warning` (a content note states what the file contains so the listener can decide; it may share the description but has a…); `prestige-framing` (a potency claim may appear in a title but asserts strength, not content)

## Convincers and tests (`convincers`)

Suggested experiences staged so the listener notices something unusual happening and takes it as proof of trance or of the speaker's influence.

- `convincer` (unit) **Convincer** - Suggest an effect, have the listener check it, and treat the result as proof of trance or of the speaker's influence.
  - Cues: 'try to ...' after a run of suggestions about the same body part; 'and you can't', 'it won't', 'the harder you try the more'; 'stop trying', 'let it go now', 'back to normal' right after the test; 'that's how you know', 'proof', 'see how deep you are'
  - Not: `challenge-suggestion` (the challenge is the single 'try and you can't' sentence; the convincer is the whole staged passage from build-up to…); `ratification` (ratification is the short comment on a result ('that's right'); the convincer is the suggestion and test that produce…); `suggestibility-test` (a suggestibility test is run awake before the induction and framed as imagination; a convincer is run in trance and…)
- `eye-catalepsy` (variant, variant of `convincer`) **Eyelid lock** - Suggest the eyelids are so relaxed or stuck that they will not open, then invite the listener to try.
  - Cues: 'eyelids', 'eyes' with 'glued', 'stuck', 'sealed', 'locked', 'won't open'; 'relax them so much they won't work'; 'test them', 'try to open them' after eye-closure suggestions; 'stop testing', 'stop trying' immediately after
  - Not: `eye-closure` (closure only asks the eyes to close; the lock adds a test of opening); `eye-roll-induction` (eye-roll uses the eyes as a fixation start; the lock tests them after closure)
- `arm-catalepsy` (variant, variant of `convincer`) **Rigid arm** - Suggest an outstretched arm becomes rigid like a bar and will not bend, then invite the listener to try bending it.
  - Cues: 'arm' with 'stiff', 'rigid', 'steel', 'bar', 'board', 'locked'; 'stretch your arm out', 'make a fist' before the lock; 'try to bend it'; 'let it go loose', 'soft again' afterward
  - Not: `stuck-convincer` (stuck fixes a part to a surface; rigid arm fixes the joint itself); `arm-levitation` (levitation produces movement; rigidity prevents it); `freeze` (freeze locks the whole body, not one limb)
- `stuck-convincer` (variant, variant of `convincer`) **Stuck** - Suggest a hand, foot or the body is stuck to a surface and cannot be lifted, then invite a try.
  - Cues: 'stuck to', 'glued to', 'stuck fast', 'magnet'; 'hands locked together', 'fingers interlocked and stuck'; 'try to lift', 'try to pull apart', 'try to stand'; 'the glue dissolves', 'unstuck', 'free again'
  - Not: `arm-catalepsy` (rigid arm locks the joint; stuck fixes the limb to a surface or to another body part); `freeze` (freeze stops all movement; stuck fixes one contact point); `suggestibility-test` (a hand clasp done awake before the induction and framed as imagination is a suggestibility test; the same lock in…)
- `name-amnesia` (variant, variant of `convincer`) **Name / number amnesia** - Suggest that one familiar item, such as a number or the listener's name, is gone, then ask them to count or say it to find it missing.
  - Cues: 'forget the number', 'the number ... is gone'; 'you can't remember your name', 'your name slips away'; 'count your fingers', 'tell me your name' right after; 'it comes back now', 'remember it again'
  - Not: `amnesia-suggestion` (amnesia suggestion targets events or content of the session; this removes one known item and checks at once that it is…); `losing-numbers` (in losing the numbers the listener counts down a long run and all the numbers fade as a route to depth; here one item…); `speech-inhibition` (speech lock blocks saying a word that is still known ('you can't say it'); here the item is suggested as unknown…)
- `speech-inhibition` (variant, variant of `convincer`) **Speech lock** - Suggest the listener cannot speak, or can say only one fixed word or phrase, then invite them to try.
  - Cues: 'can't speak', 'can't say', 'no words come out'; 'the only word you can say is'; 'voice is resting', 'throat is quiet', 'mouth won't open'; 'your voice comes back'
  - Not: `name-amnesia` (amnesia removes the item from memory; speech lock blocks saying it); `freeze` (freeze locks the whole body; speech lock targets the voice); `repeat-after-me` (repeat-after-me asks for speech; a one-word lock forbids everything else)
- `freeze` (variant, variant of `convincer`) **Freeze** - Suggest the whole body is locked in place and cannot move until released, then invite a try.
  - Cues: 'can't move', 'frozen', 'statue', 'paralysed', 'locked in place'; 'whole body', 'every muscle' with an inability; 'try to move' after a body-wide stillness suggestion; 'free to move again', 'stretch' as a release
  - Not: `stuck-convincer` (stuck fixes one contact point; freeze stops the whole body); `heaviness-lightness` (heaviness describes weight without testing movement); `physical-deepener` (a deepener uses the body to go deeper; freeze stages a test of control)
- `ideomotor-signal` (unit) **Ideomotor signal** - Ask the unconscious to answer by lifting a chosen finger or moving the head, so the movement feels like it happened on its own.
  - Cues: 'yes finger', 'no finger', 'I don't know finger'; a movement assigned a meaning: 'this finger means yes', 'a nod for yes'; 'let your unconscious answer', 'let the finger answer'; a yes/no question followed by a pause or a wait for movement; 'lift on its own', 'by itself' said of the assigned finger or the head
  - Not: `implied-directive` (the implied directive is the sentence 'when X has happened inside, your finger will lift'; the signal is a movement…); `ideomotor-induction` (ideomotor induction uses a movement as the route into trance; the signal uses it to answer); `automaticity-framing` (automaticity framing words any response as happening by itself; the signal is one movement assigned a meaning)
- `suggested-sensation` (unit) **Suggested sensation** - Suggest a felt sensation (warmth, tingling, pressure, touch) in a named place with no physical cause.
  - Cues: 'tingling', 'warmth', 'coolness', 'pressure', 'buzzing' tied to a named body part; 'notice a sensation', 'you may begin to feel'; 'as if a hand', 'like a touch', 'a breath on your skin'; a sensation stated with no outside cause and no instruction to imagine a scene
  - Not: `positive-hallucination` (positive hallucination is perception of an absent sight, sound, smell or taste; suggested sensation is a feeling in…); `sensation-relabeling` (relabeling renames a feeling already there; this creates one); `heaviness-lightness` (heaviness or floating is a whole-body weight feeling tied to depth; this is a local feeling in a named place with no…)
- `suggested-anesthesia` (variant, variant of `suggested-sensation`) **Numbness** - Suggest a body area grows numb and cannot feel, often a hand first, then test with a pinch or pressure.
  - Cues: 'numb', 'no feeling', 'can't feel', 'like the dentist'; 'glove', 'wrapped', 'wooden', 'cold' leading into numbness; 'pinch', 'press' as a test, 'compare with the other hand'; 'feeling returns', 'normal sensation' as a release
  - Not: `negative-hallucination` (negative hallucination blocks an outside percept; numbness blocks body feeling); `body-dissociation` (dissociation detaches from the body; numbness removes feeling in it); `sensation-transfer` (transfer moves an established numbness; this creates it)
- `sensation-transfer` (variant, variant of `suggested-sensation`) **Sensation transfer** - Tell the listener that a sensation already established in one place moves to another place, usually by touching it there.
  - Cues: 'let the feeling flow into', 'transfer', 'move the numbness to'; 'place your hand on' after a sensation in that hand; 'as it leaves your hand, your hand returns to normal'; earlier established sensation named again with a new location
  - Not: `travelling-sensation` (a travelling sensation sweeps through the body region by region and stays wherever it has been; a transfer moves an…); `suggested-anesthesia` (numbness creates the feeling; transfer moves one that exists); `deepening-by-linkage` (linkage ties any stimulus to going deeper; transfer moves one sensation to a new place)
- `travelling-sensation` (variant, variant of `suggested-sensation`) **Travelling sensation** - Describe one suggested sensation moving through the body region by region, stating what it brings wherever it goes.
  - Cues: one sensation noun as the subject of motion verbs: 'spreads', 'flows', 'moves',…; 'a wave of ...' followed by body regions; body regions named in order with path prepositions: 'up through', 'down into', 'across',…; a start point: 'begins in', 'starting at'; an arrival effect: 'wherever it goes', 'everything it touches', 'as it reaches', 'it…
  - Not: `progressive-relaxation` (progressive relaxation instructs each region in turn to relax; here a sensation travels and the regions change because…); `sensation-transfer` (transfer declares an established sensation moved from one area to another, usually by touch; a travelling sensation is…); `embodied-metaphor` (state-as-substance describes the state itself as a material (fog, honey, light); a travelling sensation is a literal…)
- `positive-hallucination` (unit) **Positive hallucination** - Suggest the listener sees, hears, smells or tastes something that is not present, often as a test.
  - Cues: 'you can smell', 'you can taste', 'you can hear' with an absent source; 'now you notice', 'becoming real', 'really there'; named smells and tastes (coffee, lemon, flowers) without a scene to imagine; 'and now it fades' as a removal
  - Not: `guided-imagery` (imagery asks the listener to picture; hallucination claims they perceive); `suggested-sensation` (body feeling belongs there; this covers sight, sound, smell and taste); `imagined-substance` (imagined substance is a fictional object whose effects are suggested; here the percept itself is the proof)
- `negative-hallucination` (unit) **Negative hallucination** - Suggest the listener cannot see, hear or otherwise perceive something that is present.
  - Cues: 'you can't see', 'you can't hear', 'invisible', 'not there'; 'fades until there is no', 'disappears from your awareness'; 'look at ... and notice it's gone'; 'it's back now', 'you can see it again'
  - Not: `positive-hallucination` (adds a percept; negative removes one); `narrowing-attention` (narrowing says ignore; negative hallucination says it is not there); `suggested-anesthesia` (numbness removes body feeling, not outside percepts)
- `suggestibility-test` (unit) **Suggestibility test** - Before the induction, run a short imagination exercise that produces a small automatic response the listener can notice.
  - Cues: 'imagine a lemon', 'bite into it'; 'fingers' with 'magnets', 'pulling together'; 'sway', 'falling backward', 'pendulum'; 'this is just a test of imagination', 'before we begin'
  - Not: `convincer` (a convincer is run in trance and framed as proof; a suggestibility test is run awake before the induction and framed…); `magnetic-hands` (the same magnet exercise is an induction when contact is tied to closing the eyes or going under; with no trance claim…); `heavy-light-arms` (book and balloon is one named form of this test; use its label when the two-arm weight and balloon exercise appears)
- `heavy-light-arms` (variant, variant of `suggestibility-test`) **Book and balloon** - Have both arms out, suggest one carries a heavy weight and the other is lifted by a balloon, then let the listener open their eyes to see the gap.
  - Cues: 'one arm' / 'the other arm' with 'heavy' and 'light' in alternation; 'book', 'bucket', 'dictionary', 'weight' paired with 'balloon', 'helium'; 'open your eyes and look at your hands'; 'notice the distance between them'
  - Not: `apposition-of-opposites` (apposition is the sentence form that pairs two opposite experiences; book and balloon is the whole two-arm exercise…); `heaviness-lightness` (heaviness-lightness is a whole-body deepener with no visible check; this one sets two arms against each other and ends…); `arm-levitation` (levitation lifts one arm and ties trance to its rise; here two arms diverge and the gap is shown as a test)
- `body-control` (unit) **Puppeteering** - Suggest the speaker's words move parts of the listener's body without the listener choosing to move them.
  - Cues: first-person verb with the listener's body part as object: 'I lift your', 'I'm moving…; 'puppet', 'pulling the strings', 'my strings', 'remote control'; 'my words move you', 'your body follows my voice, not you'; 'just watch it move' paired with the speaker as the cause; 'control returns to you', 'I give your body back'
  - Not: `automaticity-framing` (automaticity framing says the movement happens by itself and names no mover; puppeteering names the speaker as the one…); `ideomotor-signal` (the signal is a movement given a meaning and used as an answer; puppeteering is a movement the speaker claims to drive); `obedience-task` (a task asks the listener to choose to act on an instruction; here the movement is framed as not chosen)
- `hands-free-response` (unit) **Suggested arousal / orgasm** - Suggest a build of arousal and a peak without touch, usually released on a word or count.
  - Cues: 'on my count', 'when I say the word', 'not until I say'; rising count paired with 'building', 'higher', 'closer', 'edge'; 'hands-free', 'without touching'; 'let go', 'release', 'now' as a single-word cue after a hold
  - Not: `countdown-finale` (the finale is the session-level shape of ending on a counted release of any kind; this is the suggested build and…); `tease-and-denial` (tease-and-denial withholds the peak; this delivers it on a cue); `suggested-sensation` (a plain sensation has no build, hold or release cue)
- `post-session-proof` (unit) **After-the-fact proof** - After waking, point the listener to evidence that something happened: more time gone than it felt like, a lingering heaviness, parts they cannot recall.
  - Cues: 'check the time', 'look at the clock'; 'longer than it felt', 'where did the time go'; 'still a little heavy', 'lingering' after the count-up; 'try to remember' after waking
  - Not: `time-distortion` (time distortion is the suggestion that time feels different, given during trance; this is the check of the clock after…); `amnesia-on-waking` (amnesia on waking suggests the forgetting; this invites the listener, once awake, to look for the gap); `ratification` (ratification confirms a response at the moment it happens in the session; this comes after the wake and points at…)

## Memory and time (`memory-time`)

Suggestions that change what the listener remembers or forgets, or where in time they place themselves and their traits.

- `amnesia-suggestion` (unit) **Amnesia suggestion** - Tell the listener that some part of the session, or a specific detail, will be forgotten or hard to recall.
  - Cues: forget / forgetting / forgotten; you don't need to remember; drift away / fade like a dream; remember to forget; whether you remember or forget
  - Not: `name-amnesia` (a brief in-trance convincer: one familiar item is suggested away and tested at once so the listener notices the gap); `losing-numbers` (the listener's own count is relaxed away as a deepener; the fading is the route to depth, not a suggestion about…); `negative-hallucination` (suppresses perception of something present, not memory of something past)
- `amnesia-on-waking` (variant, variant of `amnesia-suggestion`) **Amnesia on waking** - As part of the wake-up, suggest that some or all of the session will not be remembered once the listener is awake.
  - Cues: 'forget', 'won't remember', 'slips away', 'fades', 'out of reach' inside or just before…; 'when you wake', 'as you wake', 'once your eyes open' governing a forgetting clause; 'like a dream', 'the harder you try to remember'; contrast of memory and effect ('the words fade, the feeling stays'); a release clause ('until I say', 'you will remember when')
  - Not: `amnesia-suggestion` (the general device, usable anywhere; this row is its use tied to the wake); `source-amnesia` (that keeps the idea and removes only where it came from); `amnesia-by-distraction` (that gets forgetting from an abrupt topic change, with no forgetting suggestion in the text)
- `structured-amnesia` (variant, variant of `amnesia-suggestion`) [text-detectable: partial] **Structured amnesia** - After trance, pick up exactly where the conversation stopped before trance, so the middle drops out of memory.
  - Cues: a sentence broken off just before the induction and resumed word for word after emergence; as I was saying (directly after emergence); same topic in the last pretalk line and the first post-emergence line; no mention of the trance between the resumed lines
  - Not: `amnesia-by-distraction` (changes to a new subject after the material; structured amnesia returns to the old one); `amnesia-on-waking` (states the forgetting aloud; structured amnesia never mentions it); `nested-loops` (opens and closes stories inside the session around the suggestions; structured amnesia brackets the whole trance with…)
- `source-amnesia` (variant, variant of `amnesia-suggestion`) **Forgetting where it came from** - Suggest the listener keeps the idea or change but forgets that it was suggested, so it feels self-generated.
  - Cues: 'feel like your own idea', 'your own choice', 'your own thought' said of a suggestion…; 'you won't remember where it came from', 'you won't know why'; 'as if you decided it yourself', 'something you just decided'; 'it will simply be yours'; future tense about how a suggested thought or act will seem when it occurs ('it will…
  - Not: `retroactive-framing` (says the trait was always there; source amnesia says a newly suggested idea will lose its origin or seem self-made); `amnesia-suggestion` (removes the content itself, not only its source); `desire-attribution` (claims the listener wants the response now or already; source amnesia predicts how a suggested idea will be…)

- `revivification` (unit) **Revivification** - Have the listener recall one past experience in enough sensory detail that its feeling or state returns now.
  - Cues: remember a time when; go back to that moment / be there now; what do you see / hear / feel there; past memory narrated in present tense; that feeling coming back now

## Identity and belief (`identity-belief`)

Devices that change how the listener names, describes and understands themselves, or what they hold to be true.

- `identity-statement` (unit) **Identity statement** - State plainly what the listener is, as a fact about them rather than a feeling or action.
  - Cues: 'you are' + kind-of-person noun phrase ('a calm person', 'a listener', 'a natural…; 'you're someone who ...' / 'you're the one who ...' / 'you're the kind of person who…; 'I am' + trait or kind-of-person noun in a line for the listener to hear or repeat; 'that is who you are' / 'this is what you are'; series of short 'you are a/an X' sentences
  - Not: `direct-suggestion` ('you are relaxed' or 'you are ready' states a passing condition and is a plain direct suggestion; an identity…); `labeling` (labeling attaches a trait to justify a requested behavior; an identity statement stops at the trait); `retroactive-framing` (it-was-always-so adds the claim that the trait was always true)
- `renaming` (unit) **Renaming** - Give the listener a new name, number or title that stands for the identity the script installs.
  - Cues: 'your name is ...' / 'you will be called ...'; 'from now on you are ...' + proper noun or number; 'your designation/title is ...'; a capitalized new term then used as a form of address
  - Not: `name-use` (name-use is addressing by a name; renaming is the act of assigning it); `role-assignment` (a role carries expected behavior; a name may carry none); `alter-persona` (a persona is a separate self switched in by cue, not a new name for the listener)
- `role-assignment` (unit) **Role assignment** - Assign the listener a role (student, pet, assistant, drone) and spell out the behavior that role entails.
  - Cues: 'you're my ...' + role noun; 'your role is ...' / 'your job here is ...'; role noun followed by 'and a [role] ...' describing behavior; 'as my [role], you ...'
  - Not: `identity-statement` (an identity statement names a trait; a role names a position with duties); `character-identification` (character identification casts the listener as a story character, not in a role toward the speaker); `alter-persona` (a persona is a separate named self switched in by cue)
- `old-self-new-self` (unit) **Old self / new self** - Contrast who the listener was with who they are becoming, placing the old traits in the past.
  - Cues: 'the old you' / 'the new you'; 'the person who used to ...'; 'leave behind' / 'step away from' + former self; past-tense description of the listener followed by present-tense contrast; 'that was then ... this is now'
  - Not: `retroactive-framing` (it-was-always-so denies any earlier self; this device needs one); `time-progression` (future progression relocates the listener in time; this contrasts two selves); `antithesis` (antithesis is the balanced phrasing; this is the old/new identity content it may carry)
- `pronoun-shift` (unit) **Pronoun shift** - Change the pronoun used for the listener partway through, so the grammar itself carries the shift in self.
  - Cues: second-person passage followed by the same content in first person; 'you' replaced by 'we' mid-passage; listener referred to as 'it' after earlier 'you'; announced switch: 'now say it as I'
  - Not: `first-person-scripting` (first-person suggestion uses 'I' throughout; a pronoun shift is the change from one to another); `depersonalization-language` (object language is the content; a shift to 'it' is one way of delivering it); `second-person-narrative` (second-person narration keeps 'you' as protagonist without a switch)
- `third-person-self-reference` (variant, variant of `pronoun-shift`) **Third-person self-reference** - Have the listener refer to themselves by an assigned term instead of 'I' and 'me'.
  - Cues: 'no more I' / 'don't say I'; 'say it as ...' + third-person sentence; listener speech lines using a name or 'this one' instead of 'I'; correction of 'I' to an assigned term
  - Not: `renaming` (renaming assigns the term; this requires the listener to use it for themselves); `first-person-scripting` (first-person suggestion has the listener say 'I'; this removes 'I')
- `inner-voice` (unit) **Inner-voice suggestion** - Suggest that the speaker's words are, or are becoming, the listener's own thoughts and inner voice.
  - Cues: 'my voice becomes your ...' (thoughts, inner voice, own voice); 'your thoughts' + 'my words' in the same sentence; 'inside your head' / 'the voice you think with'; 'you won't hear me as me' / 'as if you had thought it yourself'; 'my words sound like your own thinking'
  - Not: `first-person-scripting` (first-person suggestion writes the thoughts directly as 'I' lines; this entry is the claim, made in the second person,…); `source-amnesia` (source amnesia removes the memory of the source; this suggests the speaker becomes the inner voice); `voice-focus` (voice focus narrows attention onto the voice without placing it inside)
- `values-linking` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Linking to values** - Connect the suggestion to something the listener already values, so accepting it expresses who they are.
  - Cues: 'because you care about ...' + suggestion; 'you value ...' / 'what matters to you' / '... matters to you'; 'as someone who values/believes in ...' + suggestion; value word (health, family, freedom, honesty) + 'and that means ...' / 'which is why…
  - Not: `reason-giving` (giving a reason uses any 'because', including 'for your own good' and 'you deserve it'; this names a value the…); `reframing` (reframing changes the meaning of an experience; this ties a request to an existing value); `desire-attribution` (it's-what-you-want attributes a wish for the response; this appeals to a value the response would serve)
- `desire-attribution` (unit) **It's what you want** - Attribute the suggested response to the listener's own wish, so compliance is framed as getting what they want.
  - Cues: 'you want ...' / 'this is what you want', stated by the speaker as fact; 'deep down you ...' / 'secretly you ...' + want or need; 'part of you wants ...'; 'you've wanted this' / 'you've been wanting this'
  - Not: `choice-as-ownership` (you-chose-this cites the act of choosing; this asserts the wish directly); `mind-reading` (mind reading claims any inner experience; this claims a wish for the suggestion); `self-perception-proof` (your-actions-prove-it points at behavior; this asserts desire without evidence)
- `ownership-language` (unit) **Ownership language** - Refer to the listener, their mind or their body with possessive words that place them as belonging to the speaker.
  - Cues: 'mine' / 'my' + listener or their body or mind; 'you belong to ...'; 'my property' / 'owned'; 'I own your ...'
  - Not: `role-assignment` (a role gives a position with duties; ownership language gives a possessor); `depersonalization-language` (object language says what the listener is; ownership says whose); `name-use` (a possessive pet name may count as both, but name-use is about addressing)
- `depersonalization-language` (unit) **Object / it language** - Describe the listener as an object, animal or machine instead of a person.
  - Cues: listener referred to as 'it'; nouns: 'doll', 'drone', 'machine', 'toy', 'pet', 'thing' applied to the listener; machine verbs: 'processing', 'program', 'powering down', 'malfunction'; 'no longer a person'
  - Not: `role-assignment` (role assignment casts the listener in a position and states its duties ('you are X, and X does ...'); object language…); `pronoun-shift` (pronoun shift is the switch point from 'you' to 'it'; sustained 'it' and object nouns after the switch are this entry); `embodied-metaphor` (state-as-substance gives the state a form, not the listener)
- `belief-installation` (unit) **Belief statement** - State a belief or rule as plain truth, for the listener to hold after the session.

- `numbered-rules` (variant, variant of `belief-installation`) **Numbered rules** - Present beliefs or rules as a numbered list, often to be recited back.
  - Cues: 'rule one' / 'the first rule' / 'law number'; ordinal or cardinal numbers before short imperative or declarative lines; 'repeat the rules'; 'there are N rules'
  - Not: `recap-structure` (a recap restates suggestions already given; numbered rules are presented as a code); `countdown-structure` (a counted passage uses numbers to pace, not to index rules); `tricolon` (a tricolon is three parallel items, not a numbered code)
- `permanence-claim` (unit) **Permanence claim** - Assert that the change is permanent or cannot be reversed.
  - Cues: 'forever' / 'for the rest of your life' / 'from now on, always' + the effect (pointing…; 'permanent' / 'permanently'; 'can never be undone' / 'can't be removed' / 'no way back'; 'for good' / 'never fades' / 'never wears off'
  - Not: `inevitability-framing` (inevitability says it will happen; permanence says it will not stop); `trigger-expiry` (an expiry clause sets an end; a permanence claim denies one); `suggestion-sealing` (sealing states what stays after waking; a permanence claim adds that it never fades)
- `progressive-transformation` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Piece-by-piece transformation** - Describe the change taking over one body part, trait or habit at a time until it is complete.

- `skill-capacity-suggestion` (unit) **Capacity suggestion** - Suggest that a mental or physical ability is stronger, weaker or gone.
  - Cues: ability noun or verb + 'sharpens' / 'improves' / 'comes easily' / 'fades' / 'gets harder'; 'can't think' / 'harder to think' together with a duration marker ('from now on', 'each…; 'smarter' / 'dumber' / 'IQ'; 'you'll find it easy/hard to ...' + mental or physical skill
  - Not: `cognitive-quieting` (mind-blanking quiets thought as a passing state inside the session; this changes an ability for a stated period or for…); `hypermnesia` (suggested better recall is memory enhancement; this entry covers other abilities); `speech-inhibition` (speech lock is a staged convincer with an invitation to test; this is a standing change in capacity)

## Resistance handling (`resistance`)

Ways a script deals with the listener's doubt, objection and holding back, by using, deferring, inviting or disarming it.

- `reverse-psychology` (unit) **Reverse psychology** - Flatly forbid the wanted response, or order its opposite, so the listener's opposition produces it.
  - Cues: 'don't ... yet', 'not yet'; 'whatever you do, don't...'; 'try not to (relax/drift/go under)'; 'stay awake/alert as long as you can' in an induction context
  - Not: `negative-suggestion` (negative suggestion relies on representing the negated response; reverse psychology relies on the listener opposing…); `challenge-suggestion` (a challenge invites a test of something suggested impossible; reverse psychology asks for the opposite of the goal); `reverse-set-bind` (a reverse-set bind wraps the reversal in a choice)
- `permission-to-resist` (unit) **Permission to resist** - Explicitly invite the listener to resist, so resisting becomes something the script asked for.
  - Cues: 'go ahead and resist'; 'you can resist if you want'; 'feel free to fight it / hold on'; 'let's see how long that lasts'
  - Not: `but-you-are-free` (but-you-are-free grants freedom to refuse the request; permission to resist invites resistance inside the session); `permission-giving` (permission giving licenses a wanted response; this licenses the opposing one); `reverse-psychology` (reverse psychology instructs the opposite of the goal)
- `resistance-container` (unit) **Setting doubt aside** - Give the listener's doubts or concerns a place to wait during the session, with a promise they can be picked up afterward.
  - Cues: 'set aside', 'park', 'put to one side', 'leave at the door'; 'for now' or 'for the next while' attached to doubts or worries; 'they'll be there when you return/when we're done'; a container or place offered specifically for doubts
  - Not: `container-imagery` (container imagery is the box image for any content; this is the move of postponing doubt, with or without a box); `doubt-reframing` (doubt as a sign keeps the doubt present and re-labels it); `cognitive-quieting` (mind-blanking fades all thought, not doubt in particular, and promises no return)
- `critical-faculty-bypass` (unit) **Bypassing the critic** - Suggest that the listener's analytical, judging mind relaxes, steps aside or stops checking, so suggestions go straight in.
  - Cues: 'conscious mind' or 'critical/analytical/thinking mind' plus 'rest', 'step aside', 'step…; 'straight to your unconscious/subconscious'; 'nothing you need to think about/check'; 'no need to analyse'
  - Not: `distraction-of-conscious-mind` (occupying the conscious mind keeps the critic busy with a task; this tells it to stand down); `conscious-unconscious-split` (the split addresses the unconscious as a listener; this specifically retires the critic); `cognitive-quieting` (mind-blanking fades all thought; this targets evaluation only)
- `exhausting-resistance` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Wearing down** - Keep the process going, often with repeated effortful tasks, until resisting costs more effort than giving in.
  - Cues: 'keep trying', 'again' repeated across a passage; 'I can wait', 'I have all night', 'I'm not going anywhere'; 'getting tired of fighting/holding on'; 'wouldn't it be easier to just...'; unusually long induction or repeated effortful task
  - Not: `resistance-deepens` (the-harder-you-resist claims effort backfires immediately; wearing down relies on cumulative cost); `challenge-suggestion` (a challenge sets one test whose failure is the proof; wearing down repeats or prolongs the effort and points at what…); `negative-reinforcement` (negative reinforcement is the stated contingency that giving in ends the strain; wearing down is the prolonging and…)
- `pre-agreement` (unit) **Advance agreement** - Get the listener to agree, before hearing it, to accept whatever the script says next.
  - Cues: 'agree to (everything/whatever) I say/suggest'; 'say yes now to whatever comes next'; 'you already agreed', 'you said yes when...'; acceptance asked for before content is given
  - Not: `public-commitment` (stated commitment pledges a known behavior; advance agreement accepts unseen content); `yes-set` (the yes set earns agreement with undeniable statements; advance agreement asks for blanket acceptance outright); `choice-as-ownership` (you-chose-this cites a past choice to be here; advance agreement secures a forward promise)
- `protected-core` (unit) **Protected core** - State that any suggestion against the listener's values or interests will simply be rejected, which lets them relax their guard.
  - Cues: 'never do anything against your values/morals/will'; 'only accept what is right/good for you'; 'a part of you is always looking after you'; 'anything else simply slides away / is rejected'; a safety assurance followed by 'so you can relax/let go'
  - Not: `myth-correction` (myth correction addresses beliefs about hypnosis in general; this promises a filter on the suggestions); `scope-limits` (scope limits bound where suggestions apply; this promises the listener's mind will reject harmful ones); `comfort-safety` (general safety and ease, without the claim that unwanted suggestions are filtered)

## Motivation and emotion (`motivation-emotion`)

Raise, hold back or steer what the listener wants and feels, so that wanting or feeling carries the suggestion.

- `tease-and-denial` (unit) **Tease and denial** - Build desire for something, bring it close, then withhold it, so the wanting grows and stays.
  - Cues: 'not yet', 'stop', 'almost', 'so close' at the end of a build-up toward a named release; rising description that halts, fades or backs off just before the release arrives; 'edge', 'on the edge', 'right up to the edge', 'hold it there'; 'again', 'once more', 'a little higher this time' restarting the build after a stop; the wanting described as stronger after each stop ('still wanting', 'wanting it more')
  - Not: `deprivation` (withholds a known reward to raise its value, with no build toward it; label tease and denial only when the text first…); `anticipation-building` (announces a coming event, delays it and then delivers it as promised; tease and denial brings a desire to the brink…); `reward-contingent-on-depth` (the variant: use it when the text states what response or permission opens the release; plain tease and denial states…)
- `reward-contingent-on-depth` (variant, variant of `tease-and-denial`) **Pleasure gated on compliance** - Make release or pleasure available only when the listener gives the wanted response or receives permission.
  - Cues: 'only when', 'not until', 'when you ... then you can' tied to release; 'permission', 'when I say', 'I'll tell you when'; release explicitly conditioned on depth, obedience or a spoken phrase; a stated gate followed later by its opening
  - Not: `negative-reinforcement` (compliance ends an unpleasant state such as tension, effort or mental noise; when what compliance opens is a release…); `premack-principle` (the general first-then rule with any liked activity as the reward; this variant applies only when the reward is a…); `positive-reinforcement` (delivers a reward after the response with no prior build-up or withholding)
- `fear-then-relief` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Fear then relief** - Raise a brief alarm, take it away, and make the request in the moment of relief that follows.
  - Cues: a sentence of worry or alarm about something going wrong, followed at once by…; a request or command in the sentence directly after the reassurance; 'for a moment you might wonder/worry ... you haven't' or 'what if ... no' turns; alarm wording ('wait', 'something's wrong', 'did you hear that') cancelled within two or…
  - Not: `negative-reinforcement` (relief is the stated result of complying; here the alarm is removed first, unconditionally, and the request follows); `comfort-safety` (reassurance on its own; label fear then relief only when the text itself raised the worry just before and a request…); `tension-release` (muscles tightened and let go for relaxation; fear then relief needs a worry or alarm about something, not a clenched…)
- `craving-framing` (unit) **Craving frame** - Describe the listener's relation to the voice, file or state as need, craving or addiction rather than choice.
  - Cues: 'need', 'crave', 'craving', 'hooked', 'addicted', 'ache' applied to the session, file,…; 'come back', 'press play again', 'listen again' described as an urge or pull the…; the urge described as growing between listens ('a little more each day'); 'can't resist coming back', 'can't get enough', with no statement of what stopping would…
  - Not: `dependency-building` (claims the speaker is the only source ('only I can', 'not on your own'); the craving frame names a need without…); `phobia-of-leaving` (attaches dread or loss to stopping; the craving frame describes the pull to return and says nothing about what…); `desire-attribution` (says the listener wants the response; no need, craving or addiction vocabulary)
- `comfort-safety` (unit) **Safety and comfort** - Tell the listener they are safe, comfortable and not being judged, so vigilance can drop.
  - Cues: 'you're safe', 'safe here', 'safe and comfortable', 'nothing can harm you'; 'comfortable', 'supported', 'at ease' stated as fact about the listener now; 'no one is judging you', 'nothing to worry about', 'nothing can disturb you'; the same safety statement repeated at transitions
  - Not: `not-doing-not-knowing` (says no effort, action or understanding is required ('nothing to do', 'no need to try'); safety and comfort says there…); `failure-reframing` (says no outcome is wrong ('no right or wrong way'); safety and comfort makes no claim about outcomes); `protected-core` (promises that a part of the listener will reject unwanted suggestions; safety and comfort is general reassurance with…)
- `emotional-peak-timing` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Timing to the peak** - Hold the most important suggestion back and deliver it at the moment the feeling or sensation is described as strongest.
  - Cues: 'right now', 'in this moment', 'at the very top', 'at the peak', 'at its strongest'…; a build-up of a named feeling or sensation ending in a single statement; the last number of a count that has been raising a feeling, used to deliver one key line; 'as the wave breaks', 'as it peaks', 'as it crests' followed by a suggestion
  - Not: `anchoring` (applies a cue to be reused later (a word, touch or gesture) at the peak; timing to the peak places a suggestion's…); `pleasure-pairing` (pairs a target with pleasure repeatedly rather than placing one suggestion at a single peak); `countdown-finale` (the session shape that ends on a counted release; timing to the peak is the placing of a suggestion at that or any…)
- `humiliation` (unit) **Humiliation** - Evoke embarrassment or shame and use that charged feeling to intensify the experience or the suggestion.
  - Cues: 'embarrassing', 'embarrassed', 'blush', 'flush', 'cheeks warm', 'ashamed', 'shame'; the listener's ease or eagerness ('how easily', 'how fast') named as something to be…; an imagined audience or exposure ('if anyone could see you now'); shame named and followed directly by deepening or a suggestion
  - Not: `self-perception-proof` (cites the listener's act as proof of what they want; humiliation cites it as something to be embarrassed by, and a…); `depersonalization-language` (recasts the listener as an object; humiliation evokes a feeling without changing what they are called); `confession` (has the listener disclose; humiliation is done to them by the speaker's words)
- `gratitude-induction` (unit) **Thanking** - Have the listener say or feel thanks for what is being done to them.
  - Cues: 'say thank you', 'thank me', 'say thanks'; 'feel grateful', 'gratitude' as an instruction; thanks requested after a sensation, a change or the session; praise or ratification following the listener's thanks
  - Not: `repeat-after-me` (has the listener voice any suggestion; thanking voices gratitude specifically); `reciprocity` (the speaker gives in order to ask; thanking has the listener acknowledge a gift); `self-perception-proof` (points at an act as evidence; thanking produces the act)
- `anticipation-building` (unit) **Anticipation** - Announce that something good is coming and delay it, so the listener leans toward it.
  - Cues: 'soon', 'in a moment', 'in just a few seconds', 'wait for it', 'almost time'; a named experience or event announced before it happens ('in a moment you will feel...'); a count announced toward that named event; references back to the promised experience before it arrives ('it's getting nearer'); the promised event then delivered once, as announced
  - Not: `tease-and-denial` (builds a desire to the brink and pulls it back, usually more than once; anticipation announces, delays once and delivers); `curiosity-hook` (withholds what is coming or what the answer is; anticipation says what is coming and delays only its arrival); `expectation-setting` (states what will be experienced as a plain forecast; anticipation marks and stretches the wait for it)

## Rhetoric, rhythm and sound (`rhetoric-sound`)

Classical figures of repetition, structure, sound and pacing that shape how hypnotic language sounds and flows, whatever it suggests.

- `anaphora` (unit) **Anaphora** - Start several successive clauses or sentences with the same word or phrase.
  - Cues: two or more consecutive sentences or clauses beginning with the same two-plus words; repeated 'And you can...' / 'With every...' / 'Now...' line openings; line-initial repetition in a stanza-like block
  - Not: `epistrophe` (epistrophe repeats the ending, not the opening); `refrain` (a refrain is a whole line returning at intervals across the script, not a consecutive run); `parallelism` (parallelism repeats grammatical shape; anaphora repeats the actual words)
- `epistrophe` (unit) **Epistrophe** - End several successive clauses or sentences with the same word or phrase.
  - Cues: two or more consecutive clauses or sentences ending with the same word or phrase; a repeated depth or state word in sentence-final position; line-final repetition in a block of short lines
  - Not: `anaphora` (anaphora repeats the opening words); `refrain` (a refrain returns as a full line at intervals, not as the tail of consecutive clauses)
- `epizeuxis` (unit) **Immediate repetition** - Say the same word two or more times in a row with nothing between.
  - Cues: the same word two to four times consecutively, separated only by commas or ellipses; 'deeper, deeper' / 'down, down' / 'sleep, sleep'; a line made only of one repeated word
  - Not: `deepening-by-repetition` (that is repetition of depth words spread across a passage, not back-to-back doubling); `mantra` (a mantra is a fixed phrase repeated at high count as the activity itself; epizeuxis is one word or short phrase said…); `semantic-satiation` (satiation repeats one word so many times that it loses meaning; epizeuxis stops at a handful and the word keeps its…)
- `parallelism` (unit) **Parallelism** - Write successive clauses or sentences in the same grammatical shape so they read as a set.
  - Cues: consecutive clauses with matching word order and part-of-speech sequence; repeated 'As X, Y' or 'The X verbs your Y' templates; balanced clauses of similar length joined by semicolons
  - Not: `anaphora` (anaphora repeats the same opening words, not only the shape); `tricolon` (a tricolon is parallelism fixed at three members); `antithesis` (antithesis is parallel form used to set opposed ideas against each other)
- `tricolon` (variant, variant of `parallelism`) **Rule of three** - Group items, clauses or phrases in threes so the set feels complete.
  - Cues: three coordinated items joined by commas and a final 'and'; three parallel clauses in one sentence; three short sentences of the same shape in a row
  - Not: `parallelism` (parallelism is any run of matching shapes; label tricolon when there are exactly three members and the third closes…); `pacing-and-leading` (pacing and leading is defined by verifiable statements followed by a suggested one, whatever the count; a…); `polysyndeton` (polysyndeton is defined by a conjunction before every member, whatever the count; three members each joined by 'and'…)
- `polysyndeton` (unit) **And... and... and** - Link many clauses with repeated 'and', so the passage flows on without a stop.
  - Cues: three or more clauses or items in one sentence each joined by 'and' (or 'or', 'nor')…; a conjunction before every member of a list, including the last two and often the first; a run of consecutive short sentences that each begin with 'And', with nothing else…; a long sentence with no full stop whose only joints are 'and'
  - Not: `compound-suggestion` (compound suggestion uses one 'and' to transfer acceptance from a true statement to a suggestion); `sentence-lengthening` (lengthening is a trend across the script; polysyndeton is the linking device inside one run)
- `gradatio` (unit) **Gradatio** - Begin each clause with the end of the one before and go one step further each time.
  - Cues: the last word of one clause repeated as the first word of the next; chains of 'X brings Y, Y brings Z'; a progression of three or more linked terms rising in intensity
  - Not: `the-more-the-more` (that ties two increases together in one comparison, without the linked chain); `contingent-suggestion` (contingent suggestion attaches a response to an ongoing event; gradatio is the repeated-word chain); `cause-effect-claim` (cause-effect is the assertion that X makes or brings Y; gradatio is the carrying of the last term of one clause into…)
- `antithesis` (unit) **Antithesis** - Set two opposed ideas side by side in balanced, matching phrasing.
  - Cues: two adjacent clauses of the same shape whose key words are opposites (tense / loose, out…; 'not X but Y' / 'you don't have to X; you only have to Y'; 'out there... in here' / 'before... now' set in matching clauses; a semicolon or comma at the midpoint of a balanced contrast
  - Not: `apposition-of-opposites` (apposition of opposites suggests two opposed experiences at once; antithesis is the balanced wording); `the-more-the-more` (that yokes two changing amounts with a correlative comparative, even when one rises and one falls; antithesis opposes…); `old-self-new-self` (old self / new self contrasts identities; antithesis is any balanced contrast)
- `chiasmus` (unit) **Chiasmus** - Repeat two terms in reverse order across a pair of clauses (A-B, then B-A).
  - Cues: two terms appearing A...B in one clause and B...A in the next; 'not X into Y, but Y into X'; mirrored phrase pairs separated by a semicolon or 'and'
  - Not: `antithesis` (antithesis opposes ideas in parallel order; chiasmus mirrors the order); `compounding-loop` (the loop also runs A-B then B-A, but it states that each term increases the other; label the loop when the clauses are…); `wordplay-confusion` (circular wordplay loops sound-alikes to confuse; chiasmus is a single clean reversal)
- `rhyme` (unit) **Rhyme** - Repeat end-sounds across lines or phrases so the suggestion sounds like a chant or saying.
  - Cues: line-final or phrase-final words sharing end sounds; short couplets; rhyming pairs inside one sentence ('slow... go', 'deep... sleep')
  - Not: `alliteration` (alliteration repeats initial consonants, not end sounds); `meter` (meter is a regular beat, which can exist without rhyme)
- `meter` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Rhythm and meter** - Write lines with a regular, repeating pattern of stressed and unstressed syllables.
  - Cues: consecutive lines or phrases with the same number of stressed syllables; a run of same-shaped feet, such as stressed-unstressed pairs ('slowly drifting, softly…; verse-like lineation with lines of near-equal syllable count; word order bent or filler words added ('and down and down') to keep the beat
  - Not: `metronome` (a metronome is an audio beat under the voice, not a beat in the words); `breath-synced-delivery` (breath-synced pacing is how the lines are timed in performance); `parallelism` (parallelism matches grammar; meter matches stress pattern)
- `alliteration` (unit) **Alliteration and sibilance** - Repeat the same initial consonant, or soft hissing and humming sounds, across nearby words.
  - Cues: three or more nearby words starting with the same consonant; dense s / sh / z sounds in a short span; repeated m or l sounds in a soothing phrase
  - Not: `rhyme` (rhyme repeats end sounds); `whisper` (whisper is a voice choice; sibilance is written into the words)
- `rhetorical-question` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Rhetorical question** - Ask a question whose answer is obvious or not expected, to make a point.
  - Cues: a question whose only sensible answer is built in ('Who doesn't...?', 'Why would…; a sentence-opening negative question that asserts its content ('Isn't it nice to...?',…; the question makes a general claim or evaluation, not a request to check a present…; the speaker moves on without answering it and without leaving time for an answer
  - Not: `suggestion-by-question` (there considering the question produces the response; a rhetorical question asserts a point); `hypophora` (hypophora answers its own question aloud); `tag-question` (a tag question is a short add-on to a statement)
- `hypophora` (unit) **Ask and answer** - Pose a question the listener might have, then answer it yourself at once.
  - Cues: a question immediately followed by a declarative answer from the speaker; 'What happens if...? ...' / 'And where does it go? ...'; question-answer pairs in the pretalk
  - Not: `rhetorical-question` (a rhetorical question leaves its answer implied); `objection-preemption` (preemption names and answers an objection; hypophora is the question-answer form, used for any content)
- `refrain` (unit) **Refrain** - Bring back the same line, word for word, at intervals throughout the script.
  - Cues: the same full sentence appearing three or more times, separated by other text; a line repeated at section boundaries; verbatim return of an earlier line after a gap
  - Not: `mantra` (a mantra is repeated many times in a row; a refrain returns at intervals); `anaphora` (anaphora repeats an opening across consecutive clauses); `repetition-of-suggestion` (that repeats the suggestion, possibly varied; a refrain is one fixed line)
- `ellipsis-pause` (unit) **Written pause** - Mark pauses in the text with ellipses, dashes, line breaks or explicit beat notes.
  - Cues: ellipses ('...') mid-sentence; dashes isolating a word; blank lines between short lines; bracketed stage notes like [pause], [beat], (wait)
  - Not: `silence-deepener` (a silence deepener gives the silence a job (going deeper); a written pause only marks timing); `silence` (silence is the heard gap in the audio); `analog-marking` (marking sets selected words apart (by pause, capitals or italics) so that together they carry a separate message; a…)
- `sentence-lengthening` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Lengthening sentences** - Let sentences grow longer and looser as the script goes on, so the listener stops tracking where they end.
  - Cues: average sentence length rising across the passage; late-script sentences of 40+ words with few full stops; clauses joined by 'and', 'as', 'while' in long chains
  - Not: `polysyndeton` (polysyndeton is the 'and' linking inside a run; lengthening is the trend across the script); `cognitive-overload` (overload piles up simultaneous demands; lengthening only loosens syntax); `sentence-shortening` (the opposite trend)
- `sentence-shortening` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Shortening sentences** - Shrink sentences to fragments and single words as the state deepens.
  - Cues: average sentence length falling across a passage; a run of one- to three-word lines or fragments; verbless lines near the end of a deepener
  - Not: `sentence-lengthening` (the opposite trend); `epizeuxis` (epizeuxis repeats one word back to back; shortening reduces length over a run); `cognitive-quieting` (cognitive quieting suggests thoughts stopping; shortening is the stylistic reduction that can accompany it)
- `euphemism` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Euphemism** - Use a mild or pleasant word in place of a blunt one for the same thing.
  - Cues: a mild or pleasant term where the script's own context shows a blunter one applies ('a…; the same effect named plainly in one place (title, content note, another section) and…; stock softeners for bodily, sexual or control content ('down there', 'take care of you',…; minimizers attached to a large effect ('just a little', 'only for a while')
  - Not: `unspecified-verb` (a vague process verb ('let go', 'allow', 'drift') leaves out how; it is a euphemism only when the script shows a…); `surrender-as-freedom` (that reframe claims that giving up control is a gain; euphemism makes no claim and only substitutes a gentler word); `redefinition` (redefinition openly assigns a new meaning to a word)
- `hyperbole` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Hyperbole** - Overstate a sensation, depth or effect beyond literal truth for emphasis.
  - Cues: large round multipliers ('a thousand times', 'a million miles'); superlatives about felt states ('the deepest you've ever been'); impossible comparisons ('heavier than stone')
  - Not: `stepwise-doubling` (a multiplier or increment attached to each count, breath or repetition ('twice as deep with every number') is stepwise…); `anchoring-number` (a reference point is a figure on a usable scale that later judgments are measured against ('on a scale where ten is…); `comparative-deletion` (a bare comparative with no standard ('deeper', 'more relaxed') is not overstated; hyperbole names an impossible or…)
- `synesthesia` (unit) **Sense crossing** - Describe something from one sense in the terms of another, such as a voice that is felt.
  - Cues: a sound, voice or silence described with touch, temperature or weight words ('warm voice…; 'feel my words' / 'feel my voice' / 'taste the sound'; a sound or touch given a colour or brightness ('a soft blue hush', 'a bright tingle'); a perception verb paired with the wrong sense's object ('hear the warmth', 'see how…
  - Not: `sensory-overlap` (sensory overlap leads the listener in steps from a sense already active into another; sense crossing is a single…); `embodied-metaphor` (state-as-substance turns a state (calm, blankness) into a material that moves through the body; sense crossing starts…); `sensory-specific-language` (sensory language describes each sense in its own terms)
- `present-tense` (unit) **Present tense and progressive** - Write suggestions in the present tense, often with -ing forms, so they describe what is happening now.
  - Cues: a run of two or more progressive verbs describing a response that is not yet verifiable…; bare -ing participles stacked without a subject ('drifting... floating... sinking'); 'now' attached to a present-tense description of the suggested response ('lifting now'); a visible switch from future or modal phrasing ('you will', 'you can') to the present…; label once per passage, not per sentence
  - Not: `pacing` (pacing describes in the present what the listener can verify now; this entry is the present tense used for a response…); `direct-suggestion` (direct suggestion is the open statement or command of the response in any tense; this label marks the tense choice and…); `presupposed-completion` (already-happened framing speaks of the effect as done; present tense has it under way)
- `countdown-structure` (unit) **Counted passage** - Use a sequence of numbers to pace a passage, placing suggestions or description between the counts.
  - Cues: numbers in sequence as their own sentences or line openings; sentences of content between consecutive numbers; 'ten... nine... eight' or 'one... two... three' spread over a passage
  - Not: `countdown-deepener` (label the deepener when a rule is stated or plain that each number means deeper ('with each number, deeper'); label…); `counting-induction` (counting induction ties each number to going into trance); `count-up-emergence` (a count upward in which each number restores alertness is the wake-up)

## Coercive-control patterns (`coercive`)

Moves described in thought-reform, cult and abusive-relationship research that narrow the listener's options, inputs and self-trust in the speaker's favor.

- `love-bombing` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Affection flooding** - Pour intense affection, praise and approval on the listener early, before anything has been earned.
  - Cues: several affection or praise statements in close succession with no linked response; superlatives about the listener: 'perfect', 'special', 'no one else', 'everything'; declared closeness early in the script, before rapport is built; 'I adore', 'I love how you', 'so precious'
  - Not: `praise` (rewards a specific response right after it; flooding is unearned and continuous); `exclusivity` (exclusivity is one claim that the listener was selected or is one of few; label flooding only for a run of several…); `liking` (builds affection gradually; flooding overwhelms at once)
- `gaslighting` (unit) **Gaslighting** - Tell the listener that what they perceived, felt or remember did not happen, or happened differently, and that the speaker's version is the true one.
  - Cues: 'you think ... but', 'you only think', 'you didn't really'; 'you misremember', 'that's not what happened', 'that never happened'; denial of a feeling the listener was just told they had ('that wasn't doubt'); the speaker's account presented as more reliable than the listener's ('trust my voice,…; 'you can't trust your own ...'
  - Not: `suggested-memory` (suggested memory adds something; gaslighting disputes what the listener already perceived); `retroactive-framing` (retroactive framing reinterprets the past without telling the listener their own account is wrong); `doubt-reframing` (doubt reframing keeps the doubt and calls it a good sign; gaslighting says the doubt was not real)
- `isolation` (unit) **Isolation** - Tell the listener that other people do not understand, matter or help, so that the speaker becomes their only real connection.
  - Cues: 'no one else', 'only me', 'only I', 'just between you and me'; 'they wouldn't understand', 'they don't see you', 'out there'; unfavorable contrast between the speaker and other people; instructions to keep the experience secret from others; 'you don't need anyone else'
  - Not: `milieu-control` (milieu control limits information sources; isolation limits relationships); `exclusivity` (exclusivity flatters membership in a select group; isolation devalues everyone outside the speaker); `parasocial-intimacy` (parasocial intimacy builds closeness to the speaker without running down anyone else)
- `loaded-language` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Loaded language** - Use special terms whose meaning carries the speaker's judgments, so that adopting the word means adopting the judgment.
  - Cues: recurring coined terms with built-in value ('clean', 'noise', 'static', 'pure', 'good…; correction of the listener's word choice ('say it the right way'); a negative coined term for doubt or questioning; terms used without definition, as if already shared; the same evaluative label applied to many different states
  - Not: `in-group-language` (in-group language marks belonging; loaded language carries a fixed judgment in each term); `redefinition` (redefinition changes one word's meaning; loaded language is a standing vocabulary); `euphemism` (euphemism softens one expression; loaded language evaluates the listener's whole experience)
- `thought-terminating-cliche` (unit) **Thought-terminating cliche** - Answer a doubt or question with a short stock phrase that sounds final and closes the topic instead of addressing it.
  - Cues: a short fixed phrase placed right after a mentioned doubt or question; 'there is no why', 'don't think', 'thinking is not your job', 'it is what it is'; the same stopper phrase recurring at each point of doubt; dismissal of the listener's question without an answer
  - Not: `doubt-reframing` (doubt reframing answers the doubt with a new meaning; the cliche ends it with no answer); `mantra` (a mantra is repeated for absorption; a stopper is placed at the point of doubt); `resistance-container` (setting doubt aside asks the listener to park it for later; a stopper dismisses it)
- `confession` (unit) **Confession** - Instruct the listener to disclose private, shameful or secret material to the speaker.
  - Cues: 'tell me', 'admit', 'confess', 'say it out loud'; 'what you've never told anyone', 'your secret', 'what you're ashamed of'; disclosure framed as relief or cleansing ('no secrets from me'); an instruction to write down or speak private material
  - Not: `self-persuasion` (self-persuasion asks for the listener's reasons for wanting the change; confession asks for a secret, a failing or…); `self-surveillance` (self-policing reports lapses against a rule; confession reveals private material); `repeat-after-me` (repeat after me supplies the words; confession asks for the listener's own)
- `dependency-building` (unit) **Sole-source claim** - Tell the listener that a need, relief or feeling can only come from the speaker, so they must keep coming back.
  - Cues: 'only I', 'only my voice', 'no one else can'; 'you'll need me', 'you'll need to come back'; claims that the listener cannot reach a state on their own; a feeling described as owned by the speaker or the file
  - Not: `manufactured-problem` (create the need sell the cure manufactures the lack first; dependency claims sole access to a need); `isolation` (isolation devalues other people; dependency devalues other sources of relief); `reinduction-seed` (next-time-deeper invites return without claiming the listener cannot do without it)
- `manufactured-problem` (unit) **Create the need, sell the cure** - Describe a lack, flaw or discomfort in the listener, then present the speaker or the next session as the remedy.
  - Cues: a vague standing deficit attributed to the listener ('something missing', 'that empty…; the deficit said to predate the speaker ('you've felt it for a while', 'long before you…; the deficit followed closely by the speaker, file or session named as what fills or…; 'that's what was missing', 'that's why you're here', 'this is what you were looking for'
  - Not: `dependency-building` (a sole-source claim asserts a monopoly on a need; this move first tells the listener they have the need); `phobia-of-leaving` (fear of leaving predicts harm if the listener stops; this asserts a lack the listener has now, whatever they do, and…); `craving-framing` (the craving frame describes wanting the content itself; this describes a lack in the listener's life and offers the…)
- `phobia-of-leaving` (unit) **Fear of leaving** - Attach dread, loss or punishment to the idea of stopping, leaving or ignoring the speaker.
  - Cues: 'if you ever stop', 'if you tried to leave', 'imagine deleting'; a bad feeling or loss attached to quitting ('empty', 'cold', 'undo everything'); contrast between the discomfort of leaving and the comfort of staying; 'you'll always come back', 'you can't stop'
  - Not: `punishment` (punishment follows a specific act; fear of leaving targets the exit itself); `permanence-claim` (a permanence claim says a change stays; fear of leaving makes stopping feel dangerous); `sunk-cost` (a sunk-cost appeal cites what has been invested; fear of leaving attaches dread to quitting)
- `blame-reversal` (unit) **Blame reversal** - When the listener complains, doubts or fails, place the fault on the listener and cast the speaker as the one wronged or blameless.
  - Cues: 'that's because you', 'it's your fault', 'you're holding back'; the speaker's role denied while failure is attributed to the listener; the speaker presented as hurt or wronged ('after everything I've given you'); 'try harder' following a reported failure or complaint
  - Not: `failure-reframing` (no-fail framing calls every outcome success; blame reversal calls failure the listener's fault); `resistance-reframing` (resistance reframing makes resistance part of trance; blame reversal makes it a failing); `gaslighting` (gaslighting denies what the listener perceived; blame reversal accepts it and assigns the fault)
- `trauma-bonding` (unit) [text-detectable: partial] **Hot and cold** - Alternate warmth and coldness from the speaker, unpredictably, so the listener works harder for the warmth and attaches more tightly.
  - Cues: abrupt shift from praise or affection to distance without a stated cause; 'maybe I will, maybe I won't', 'you'll have to wait and see'; warmth returned on the speaker's whim, not for anything the listener did ('because I…; speaker approval described as unpredictable or revocable
  - Not: `intermittent-reinforcement` (intermittent reinforcement schedules rewards for a behavior; hot and cold switches the speaker's warmth regardless of…); `deprivation` (deprivation announces that a known reward is held back so it is worth more later; hot and cold turns the speaker's…); `love-bombing` (love bombing floods warmth; hot and cold alternates it with withdrawal)
- `milieu-control` (unit) **Controlling the inputs** - Tell the listener to limit, avoid or distrust other information, voices or sources, so the speaker's input dominates.
  - Cues: 'only my voice', 'the only one that gets in', 'other voices'; other sources described as noise, confusing or harmful; 'don't read', 'don't listen to', 'you don't need to look it up'; claims that other creators' content will not work
  - Not: `isolation` (isolation targets relationships; controlling the inputs targets information sources); `narrowing-attention` (narrowing attention focuses within the session; this restricts sources outside it); `exclusivity` (exclusivity flatters the listener's access; this forbids alternatives)
- `purity-demand` (unit) **Impossible standard** - Set a standard the listener can keep approaching but never fully meet, so they always owe more effort and feel some guilt.
  - Cues: absolute ideals ('perfect', 'completely', 'no thoughts at all', 'never'); 'not yet', 'not quite', 'never enough', 'always deeper'; praise immediately followed by a remaining shortfall; the bar raised as soon as it is reached
  - Not: `shaping` (shaping rewards reachable approximations; an impossible standard keeps the target out of reach); `escalation` (graded escalation raises requests; an impossible standard keeps the same request unmeetable); `deepening-by-repetition` (deeper and deeper invites more depth without telling the listener they fall short)
- `self-surveillance` (unit) **Self-policing** - Have the listener watch their own thoughts and behavior for lapses against the speaker's rules and report or correct them.
  - Cues: 'a part of you is always watching', 'you'll notice every time'; 'you'll tell me', 'report', 'keep a count', 'confess it next time'; 'correct yourself', 'before I have to'; monitoring framed as continuous or extending past the session
  - Not: `confession` (confession discloses private material; self-policing reports lapses against a rule); `self-reinforcement` (self-administered reinforcement rewards good behavior; self-policing watches for bad); `parts-work` (parts work addresses a part for therapy or role; self-policing installs a part as monitor)

## Safety and consent (`safety-consent`)

Lines a writer adds so the listener stays safe, stays able to stop, and the effects stay bounded in time, place and person.

- `scope-limits` (unit) **Scope limits** - State plainly where, when and with whom the session's suggestions apply, and where they do not.
  - Cues: 'only while you are listening'; 'only when ... and never when'; 'outside this session ... no effect'; naming excluded settings (work, driving, in public); 'these suggestions apply only'
  - Not: `trigger-scope` (bounds one named trigger (who may fire it, where); scope limits bound the session's suggestions as a whole); `stimulus-discrimination` (narrows one conditioned response to one cue against look-alikes ('this word from this voice, not when you hear it…); `trigger-expiry` (sets how long a suggestion lasts (a date, a duration, a number of uses); scope limits set the contexts in which it…)
- `safety-override` (unit) **Emergency wake** - Tell the listener they will wake fully and at once if anything needs their attention or anything feels wrong.
  - Cues: 'if anything needs your attention'; 'you will wake immediately / at once'; 'fully alert and clear-headed'; 'nothing in this session can stop you'; 'in an emergency'
  - Not: `safeword` (the listener speaks or thinks a word to stop; the emergency wake fires on outside need without any act); `count-up-emergence` (the planned ending of the session, not an override); `conditional-wake` (its opposite: restricts waking, which the emergency wake must override)
- `consent-check` (unit) **Consent check** - Stop at a stated point, describe what comes next, and tell the listener to continue only if they agree, with a real way out.
  - Cues: 'only continue if'; 'if you don't want ..., stop the recording now'; 'that's completely fine' attached to stopping; description of the next section followed by an exit; 'check in with yourself'
  - Not: `agreement-harvest` (prompts the listener to say or nod yes and names no way to stop; a consent check names the exit and treats taking it…); `but-you-are-free` (a clause tacked onto a request ('but you are free to say no') with no stopping point and no exit action; a consent…); `choice-as-ownership` (cites the decision to continue as proof of desire; a consent check leaves the decision neutral)
- `content-warning` (unit) **Content note** - Before the session starts, state plainly what the file contains, what it suggests, and what, if anything, lasts afterward.
  - Cues: 'this recording contains'; 'this file includes'; list of themes or triggers before the induction; 'nothing in it is permanent' / 'lasts until'; 'so you can decide'
  - Not: `expectation-setting` (predicts what the listener will feel so they look for it; a content note lists what the file contains, installs and…); `warning-as-lure` (a warning with a dare or prohibition in it and no specifics; a content note is a flat, specific list); `prestige-framing` (asserts that the file is strong without saying what is in it)
- `aftercare` (unit) **Aftercare** - After intense content, slow down, reassure, step out of role, and tell the listener how to look after themselves next.
  - Cues: 'stepping out of the role'; 'whatever you're feeling is okay'; 'have some water' / 'take a minute before you stand'; reassurance after intense content; 'you did well' followed by settling
  - Not: `gentle-reorientation` (restores body feeling, orientation and alertness; aftercare settles feelings and reassures); `positive-closing-suggestions` (general suggestions for the hours and days ahead (sleep, calm, confidence); aftercare addresses how the listener feels…); `comfort-safety` (reassurance of safety before or during the work, so vigilance drops; aftercare comes after intense content and refers…)
- `safeword` (unit) **Safeword** - Give the listener a word that, said or thought, ends the session or cancels an effect at once.
  - Cues: 'your safeword is'; 'say it or think it'; 'nothing can override'; a named word paired with waking or cancelling; 'stop word'
  - Not: `safety-override` (fires on outside need without any act by the listener); `trigger-removal` (the writer cancels a trigger; a safeword is the listener's tool); `trigger-word` (a cue that produces a response the writer wants, not an exit)
- `fantasy-reality-boundary` (unit) **Fantasy boundary** - State that the roleplay's claims about the listener are fiction and end when the session ends.
  - Cues: 'part of the fantasy'; 'when the session ends, it ends'; 'outside of this recording you are'; 'leave it here'; out-of-role statement about scene claims
  - Not: `fiction-frame` (says the content is a story or game and stops there; the boundary says the scene's claims end, naming the session's…); `scope-limits` (bounds where suggestions apply rather than declaring a scene's claims fictional); `gentle-reorientation` (cancels session-only sensations and states (heaviness, numbness, lost time); the boundary declares the scene's claims…)
- `listening-safety` (unit) **Listening conditions** - Tell the listener when and where not to listen, and how to set up a safe place to listen.
  - Cues: 'never listen while driving'; 'operating machinery'; 'somewhere comfortable where you won't be disturbed'; 'leave yourself a few minutes'; medical caution before the induction
  - Not: `content-warning` (describes what the file contains rather than the setting to listen in); `scope-limits` (bounds where suggestions apply after the session, not where to listen)

## Session structure (`structure`)

The large parts and overall shapes of a script: the sections it is built from and the patterns those sections are arranged in.

- `pretalk` (structure) **Pre-talk** - The opening stretch before the induction, where the speaker sets expectations, corrects misconceptions, states safety terms and asks the listener to get ready.
  - Cues: opening span before the first eye-closure, fixation or breathing instruction; 'before we begin', 'in this session', 'what you'll experience'; statements about what hypnosis is or is not ('it isn't sleep', 'you stay in control'); listening-condition and stopping instructions ('don't listen while driving', 'you can…; set-up instructions ('get comfortable', 'headphones on', 'find a quiet place')
  - Not: `expectation-setting` (a device that can appear anywhere; the pre-talk is the opening span where it usually sits); `induction-section` (starts at the first instruction that aims to produce trance); `content-warning` (one statement about contents, often placed in the pre-talk or the description)
- `induction-section` (structure) **Induction section** - The span of the script whose job is to start trance, from the first trance-directed instruction to the point where deepening begins.
  - Cues: first sustained instruction to fix the gaze, close the eyes, breathe in a set rhythm or…; shift into slow, repetitive, present-tense language after the pre-talk; an entry marker: 'eyes closed', 'that's right', 'now you're beginning to go into trance'; the span ends where depth words ('deeper', 'further down', countdowns to depth) take over
  - Not: `induction` (the family of techniques; this is the span of the script where they are used); `deepener-section` (intensifies a trance already begun); `pretalk` (talks about trance without yet trying to produce it)
- `deepener-section` (structure) **Deepener section** - The span after the induction whose job is to intensify trance before the main suggestions are given.
  - Cues: dense depth vocabulary: 'deeper', 'further down', 'sinking', 'twice as deep'; countdown or descent imagery (stairs, elevator) immediately after the induction; an arrival line: 'all the way down', 'at the bottom', 'deeper than before'; absence of change-work content (no new habits, traits or triggers yet)
  - Not: `deepening` (the family of techniques; this is the span where they are used); `induction-section` (starts trance rather than intensifying it); `fractionated-structure` (repeated wake-and-drop cycles as the skeleton of the whole session, not one span)
- `suggestion-section` (structure) **Suggestion section** - The middle span where the script delivers the suggestions it exists to deliver: the change, skill, trigger or experience the file is about.
  - Cues: transition line after the deepener: 'and now', 'while you rest here', 'your mind is open'; future-tense or posthypnotic language: 'from now on', 'each time you', 'whenever'; dense identity, trigger or belief statements on the file's topic; span ends at a recap, sealing passage or count-up
  - Not: `deepener-section` (builds depth without delivering the file's content); `recap-structure` (restates suggestions already given rather than giving them); `convincer-section` (stages proof of trance or control, not the change itself)
- `convincer-section` (structure) **Convincer section** - A span set aside to stage one or more convincers, so the listener experiences proof that trance or control is real before or after the main work.
  - Cues: 'go ahead and try', 'try to', 'you'll find you can't'; a lock, stuck, rigid or forgotten phenomenon followed by a test; explicit cancellation: 'stop trying', 'let it go', 'back to normal'; a bridge line using the success as evidence for later suggestions
  - Not: `convincer` (the single device; this is the span that stages one or more of them); `suggestibility-test` (done before induction, in waking state, to show responsiveness); `suggestion-section` (delivers the file's content rather than proof of trance)
- `emergence-section` (structure) **Emergence section** - The closing span that ends trance or hands it off: sealing, cancelling what should not persist, waking or sending to sleep, and reorienting.
  - Cues: 'in a moment I'll bring you back', 'it's time to return', 'coming back up'; cancellation of temporary effects: 'back to normal', 'all sensation returns'; ascending count or 'wide awake', 'eyes open' near the end of the file; alternatively, 'drift into sleep' or a return to the opening lines
  - Not: `emergence` (the family of exit devices; this is the span where they sit); `fractionated-structure` (a mid-session wake followed by another drop is not the exit); `recap-structure` (restates suggestions before the exit begins)
- `loop-structure` (structure) [text-detectable: partial] **Loop** - Build the whole file so its end flows straight into its beginning, letting it play on repeat with no wake and no seam.
  - Cues: no count-up, wake instruction or 'eyes open' anywhere in the file; final line ends on a connective ('and', 'again', 'back to') that leads into the opening…; short repeated core suggestions or mantra lines; 'loop', 'on repeat', 'as many times as you like' in the title or description
  - Not: `loop-ending` (the single closing device; this is the whole-file design); `repetition-induction` (an induction using a repeated phrase, inside a normal session); `series-structure` (several distinct files heard in order, not one file repeated)
- `series-structure` (structure) [text-detectable: partial] **Series / curriculum** - Spread the work over several files meant to be heard in order, each assuming the earlier ones and asking a little more.
  - Cues: 'part two', 'level three', 'session 4 of 6' in title or opening; references to earlier sessions: 'last time', 'as you learned before', 'the word I gave…; forward references: 'next session', 'in the next part'; triggers fired without being installed in this file
  - Not: `escalation` (the device of asking a bit more each time; the series is the multi-file layout that carries it); `practice-schedule` (repeat listening of the same file, not a sequence of different ones); `instructional-structure` (a single file framed as a lesson; a series may or may not be lessons)
- `story-frame-structure` (structure) **Story frame** - Set the whole session inside one fictional scene, so induction, suggestions and wake all happen as events in the story.
  - Cues: scene-setting opening: place, time, who 'you' are; induction and wake described as story actions (sitting, entering, leaving, a door); a consistent fictional setting or character across the whole file; 'roleplay', 'scenario', 'story' in title or description
  - Not: `guided-imagery` (an imagined scene used inside one section; here the scene contains the whole session); `fiction-frame` (a framing statement that the content is play; the story frame is the layout of the file); `narrative-transportation` (the absorption device; a story frame relies on it but is a structural choice)
- `call-and-response-structure` (structure) **Call and response** - Build the session so the listener answers at regular points throughout, aloud or inwardly, repeating lines or confirming responses.
  - Cues: regular reply prompts: 'repeat after me', 'say it', 'answer me', 'say yes'; short quoted first-person lines followed by marked pauses; stage directions such as [pause], [listener repeats] at regular intervals; an opening instruction explaining how the listener should respond
  - Not: `repeat-after-me` (label the unit on each prompt to say a line; add this label to the file only when reply prompts recur on a regular…); `agreement-harvest` (one requested yes at a chosen moment); `instructional-structure` (practice rounds of a skill, not continuous verbal exchange)
- `countdown-finale` (structure) **Countdown finale** - End the session's build-up with a counted run to a single peak moment, so tension accumulates over the numbers and releases on the last one.
  - Cues: a descending or ascending count in the last part of the file, after the main build; 'when I reach zero', 'on one', 'at the end of the count' tied to a release; lengthening gaps, holds or restarts between numbers; 'now' or 'let go' on the final number, followed by settling language
  - Not: `countdown-deepener` (counts toward depth, and suggestions that use that depth follow; after a finale's last number only settling or the…); `count-up-emergence` (the last number brings alertness; a finale's last number brings a release inside trance, and any wake comes separately…); `countdown-structure` (numbers used to pace any passage; label both when the paced passage is the end-of-session climax)
- `fractionated-structure` (structure) **Multi-drop session** - Build the session as several cycles of partial waking and dropping back into trance, with each drop deeper, rather than one induction and one wake.
  - Cues: repeated partial wakes ('eyes opening', 'coming up a little') followed by 'drop',…; 'deeper than last time', 'each time deeper' across several cycles; a re-induction trigger fired several times in one file; three or more induction-like passages in one session
  - Not: `fractionation` (label the unit on each passage of up-down cycles; add this label to the file only when the cycles recur across the…); `partial-emergence` (one wake that holds the listener up while still in trance, with no drop back and no repeated cycle); `overlearning` (repeated firing of one trigger as drill for speed, with no claim that each cycle goes deeper)
- `instructional-structure` (structure) **Instructional / practice session** - Frame the session as a lesson that teaches one response, then has the listener practice it in repeated rounds with feedback.
  - Cues: an opening statement of the skill to be learned: 'today you'll learn', 'this lesson',…; an explanation or demonstration of the step before the listener is asked to do it: 'here…; practice rounds separated by feedback on progress: 'good, that was quicker', 'better…; a closing summary of the skill and how to practice it alone: 'now you know how to',…; teacher and student wording throughout: 'lesson', 'exercise', 'you're learning', 'well…
  - Not: `overlearning` (label drill on the block of fire-and-reset rounds; add this label to the file only when the rounds sit inside a stated…); `series-structure` (several files in order; a lesson is one file); `call-and-response-structure` (continuous verbal exchange, not practice rounds of a skill)
- `recap-structure` (structure) **Recap** - Near the end, before waking, restate briefly the main suggestions the session has given, in the order given.
  - Cues: 'before you come back', 'so remember', 'to summarize', 'everything you've learned'; a block of short lines repeating key words from earlier suggestions; position directly before the count-up or sealing passage; no new suggestions introduced
  - Not: `suggestion-sealing` (states that suggestions stay, without listing them); `refrain` (one line repeated at intervals through the file); `repetition-of-suggestion` (repeating a suggestion where it is given, not summarizing them all at the end)
- `choose-your-own-structure` (structure) [text-detectable: partial] **Branching** - Build the session so the listener's choice at certain points decides which section comes next, by picking a track or following an instruction.
  - Cues: 'if you'd like... go to', 'skip to', 'choose now', 'track two'; named options with different continuations; file or track references in the script or description; a rejoin point where branches meet
  - Not: `illusion-of-choice` (options differ only in detail and lead to the same response); `forced-choice` (a choice inside the script's content, not one that selects the next section); `series-structure` (files in a fixed order, not chosen)

## Delivery and production (`delivery`)

Voice and audio choices a script calls for: how lines are spoken and what else is heard around them.

- `analog-marking` (delivery) [text-detectable: partial] **Analog marking** - Set chosen words apart by a change of tone, pace, volume or a pause, so they stand out as a second message.
  - Cues: italics, bold or capitals on a short phrase inside a sentence; stage directions: '[lower tone]', '[pause]', '[slower]' around specific words; ellipses or dashes fencing off a phrase mid-sentence; the marked phrases, read alone in order, form instructions; production notes telling the reader to emphasize listed words
  - Not: `embedded-command` (the embedded command is the wording; marking is how it is voiced); `tone-shift` (tone shift is a marked change of voice for a whole passage, not a signal on selected words); `interspersal` (interspersal is the scattering of suggestion words in a narrative; marking is one way of making them stand out)
- `matching` (delivery) [text-detectable: partial] **Matching** - Match the listener's tempo, breathing or way of speaking, so the script moves with them before it moves them.
  - Cues: stage directions that refer to the listener's observed behavior: '[match their…; tempo directions that start at conversational speed and then slow: '[normal pace, then…; opening lines in a brisk everyday register that loosens over the first section; a direction to copy the listener's register or sentence length, without reusing their…; live-session notes about mirroring posture or speech rate
  - Not: `pacing` (pacing states what is true of the listener; matching copies how the listener is behaving without stating it); `breath-synced-delivery` (phrases timed to a fixed or typical breathing rhythm, as in any recording, are breath-synced delivery; label matching…); `symptom-utilization` (repeating the listener's own words, interests or complaint is that variant; matching copies only how they speak and…)
- `fast-speech` (delivery) [text-detectable: partial] **Rapid delivery** - Deliver instructions or statements faster than the listener can evaluate each one.
  - Cues: stage directions: [fast], [rapid], [no pauses], [quickly]; run of five or more clauses of one to three words; commas or nothing where full stops would normally go; 'don't think, just', 'quickly now', 'keep up'; marked slowdown or pause directly after the run
  - Not: `micro-compliance` (a run of trivial bodily instructions is micro-compliance whatever its speed; add this label only when a stage…); `sentence-shortening` (short fragments set apart by full stops, ellipses or line breaks for slow, sparse delivery; label rapid delivery when…); `polysyndeton` (a figure of repeated conjunctions; it can be delivered at any speed)
- `slow-pacing` (delivery) [text-detectable: partial] **Slowing pace** - Speak more slowly, with longer pauses, as the session moves from induction into depth.
  - Cues: stage directions: '[slower]', '(slowing)', '(pause longer)'; ellipses or line breaks between words growing across a passage; notes like 'read at half speed from here'
  - Not: `silence` (silence is a deliberate gap; slowing pace is a falling tempo across many lines); `fast-speech` (the opposite tempo choice, used to overload); `sentence-lengthening` (a written rhythm choice in the text, not the spoken rate)
- `tone-shift` (delivery) [text-detectable: partial] **Tone shift** - Change the voice's register for a passage: lower, firmer, softer or flatter than the lines before it.
  - Cues: a voice direction at the start of a section or paragraph: '[lower voice]', '[firm]',…; directions that give a duration: 'from here on', 'for this section', 'until the count'; a direction to return: '[back to normal voice]', '[brighter]' at the wake; production notes assigning a named voice to each section ('trance voice', 'command voice')
  - Not: `analog-marking` (label by scope: a voice direction that covers a few words inside a sentence is analog marking; one that covers a whole…); `whisper` (when the direction names a whisper or close-mic voice, label whisper; tone shift covers every other change of register); `novelty-orienting` (the orienting cue is the scripted job of pulling attention back just before a key line; tone shift is the voice…)
- `whisper` (delivery) [text-detectable: partial] **Whisper** - Deliver lines in a whisper or very close to the microphone so the voice feels near and private.
  - Cues: stage directions: '[whisper]', '[close-mic]', '(softly, right in the ear)'; ASMR in the title or tags; phrases like 'right next to your ear', 'so close' with production notes
  - Not: `tone-shift` (tone shift is any other change of register (lower, firmer, flatter); label whisper when the direction names a whisper…); `layered-voices` (layering stacks several tracks; a whisper alone is one track); `parasocial-intimacy` (parasocial intimacy is a relational framing in the words; whisper is the vocal choice that often carries it)
- `layered-voices` (delivery) [text-detectable: partial] **Layered voices** - Play two or more vocal tracks at once so the listener cannot follow every word.
  - Cues: track labels: '[track A]', '[layer]', left/right channel columns; notes: 'overlapping', 'under the main voice', 'background whispers'; two scripts set side by side for simultaneous reading
  - Not: `double-induction` (double induction is a named recipe of two full inductions; layering is any multitrack voice); `subliminal-track` (a subliminal track is mixed to be barely audible; layered voices are audible); `dual-task` (dual task gives the listener a task; layering only presents competing input)
- `subliminal-track` (delivery) [text-detectable: partial] **Subliminal track** - Mix spoken suggestions below easy audibility, under music or the main voice, so they are not consciously heard.
  - Cues: stage directions: '[subliminal]', '[masked]', '-NN dB under'; title or description words: 'subliminal', 'subs', 'hidden affirmations'; a separate list of affirmations marked as the hidden track
  - Not: `layered-voices` (layered voices are audible; a subliminal track is meant not to be); `background-music` (a music bed has no hidden speech); `analog-marking` (marking sets words apart audibly within the main voice)
- `binaural-beats` (delivery) [text-detectable: partial] **Binaural / isochronic tones** - Play tones whose beat frequency is meant to pull brain rhythm toward a target state, usually under the voice.
  - Cues: stage directions: '[binaural]', '[isochronic]', 'Hz', 'theta', 'delta'; listening notes: 'headphones required'; titles or tags: 'brainwave', 'entrainment'
  - Not: `metronome` (a metronome is an audible beat the listener can count; tones are a carrier aimed at entrainment); `background-music` (a music bed is ordinary music or drone, not tuned beat frequencies)
- `stereo-panning` (delivery) [text-detectable: partial] **Panning** - Move the voice, or different voices, between the left and right ears during the recording.
  - Cues: stage directions: '[pan left]', '[L]', '[R]', 'ear to ear', 'circling'; scripts in two channels labeled left and right; words about where the voice is: 'in this ear', 'all around you'
  - Not: `layered-voices` (layering stacks simultaneous voices; panning moves placement); `spatial-confusion` (left-right confusion is a verbal confusion device; panning is audio placement); `conscious-unconscious-split` (the split is a verbal frame; one voice per ear is one way to voice it)
- `metronome` (delivery) [text-detectable: partial] **Metronome / steady beat** - Run a steady tick, pulse or beat under the voice, often slowing as the session deepens.
  - Cues: stage directions: '[metronome]', '[tick]', 'bpm'; phrases like 'with each tick', 'every beat', 'the clock'; notes on beat tempo changes
  - Not: `binaural-beats` (tones are a carrier for entrainment, pulsing too fast to count; a metronome is a slow countable beat); `sound-effects` (an effect is a single sound placed at one moment; a tick or pulse that runs on at a regular rate under the voice is a…); `meter` (meter is rhythm in the wording; a metronome is a separate sound)
- `background-music` (delivery) [text-detectable: partial] **Music bed** - Lay music, a drone or ambient sound under the voice for the whole session or a section.
  - Cues: stage directions: '[music]', '[bed fades in/out]', '[drone]', '[ambient]'; credits for music or soundscapes; words about the music: 'let the music carry you'
  - Not: `binaural-beats` (tones are tuned to a beat frequency; a music bed is ordinary music or drone); `sound-effects` (effects are discrete cue sounds; a bed is continuous); `subliminal-track` (a subliminal track hides speech; a bed carries none)
- `sound-effects` (delivery) [text-detectable: partial] **Sound effects** - Insert short non-speech sounds, such as a snap, bell or chime, as cues at chosen moments.
  - Cues: stage directions: '[snap]', '[bell]', '[chime]', '[click]', '(SFX)'; references in the words: 'when you hear the bell'; production notes listing sound cues and timings
  - Not: `sound-trigger` (a sound trigger is a trained association; a sound effect is the sound itself, trained or not); `clicker-training` (a marker signal is a specific conditioning use of one sound); `background-music` (music is continuous; effects are discrete)
- `echo-reverb` (delivery) [text-detectable: partial] **Echo** - Add echo, delay or reverb to chosen words so they repeat or linger after they are spoken.
  - Cues: stage directions: '[echo]', '[reverb]', '[delay]'; a word written repeatedly with a fading or echo mark; notes on effect settings
  - Not: `epizeuxis` (immediate repetition is spoken by the voice and written as repeated words; echo is a production effect named in a…); `analog-marking` (analog marking sets words apart with the voice itself (pitch, pause, volume); echo does it with an added effect, so…); `tone-shift` (tone shift changes the speaker's register for a passage; echo and reverb are processing added to the recording)
- `breath-synced-delivery` (delivery) [text-detectable: partial] **Breath-synced pacing** - Time phrases to a breathing rhythm, usually placing suggestions on the out-breath.
  - Cues: timing directions tied to breath: '(on the exhale)', '(on the out-breath)', '(wait for…; '(inhale)' / '(exhale)' marks placed before lines whose words are not about breathing; notes on breath timing: 'one phrase per breath', 'match their breathing', 'leave a…; evenly spaced gap marks between short phrases with a stated interval of a few seconds
  - Not: `breathe-in-breathe-out` (that entry is in the words: the inhale and exhale are each given content; breath-sync is a timing direction and the…); `breath-induction` (the induction tells the listener to attend to or change their breathing; this entry times the voice to breath without…); `matching` (matching is the rapport move of copying any feature of the listener; use this label when the direction is specifically…)
- `visual-accompaniment` (delivery) [text-detectable: partial] **Visuals** - Pair the audio with on-screen visuals: a spiral, flashing words, captions or imagery.
  - Cues: stage directions: '[on screen]', '[spiral]', '[caption]', '[flash]'; video production notes with frame or duration markings; listening notes mentioning photosensitivity
  - Not: `spiral-fixation` (spiral fixation is the spoken instruction to watch; visuals are the production track, labeled from the direction that…); `visual-trigger` (a visual trigger is an image given a stated response; visuals may or may not train one); `subliminal-track` (a subliminal track is hidden speech in the audio; words flashed on screen too fast to read are labeled visuals and…)
- `silence` (delivery) [text-detectable: partial] **Silence** - Leave deliberate stretches of silence in the recording, from a few seconds to minutes.
  - Cues: a gap direction with a duration: '[silence, 20 seconds]', '(pause, 5 seconds)', '[30s]'; directions for an extended gap: '[long pause]', '[long silence]', '[no voice, music only]'; a task handed over just before the gap: 'take a moment to...', 'in your own time'; timed gaps listed in production notes
  - Not: `silence-deepener` (if the words before the gap say that depth grows during the quiet, label the silence deepener; a gap with any other…); `ellipsis-pause` (an ellipsis, dash, line break or untimed '[pause]' or '[beat]' inside running speech is a written pause; label silence…); `slow-pacing` (slowing pace is a tempo held across many lines; silence is one discrete gap)
